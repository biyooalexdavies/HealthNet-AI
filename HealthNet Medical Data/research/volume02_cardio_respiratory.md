## 1. HYPERTENSION

### 1.1 Clinical Overview

Hypertension (HTN) is defined as sustained elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure. It is the most common preventable risk factor for cardiovascular disease, stroke, and chronic kidney disease. The 2017 ACC/AHA Guideline changed the classification system, lowering the threshold for hypertension diagnosis and treatment initiation compared to the earlier JNC-7 framework [^1^].

### 1.2 Blood Pressure Classification (2017 ACC/AHA)

| Category | Systolic BP (mmHg) | | Diastolic BP (mmHg) |
|----------|-------------------|---|---------------------|
| Normal | <120 | and | <80 |
| Elevated | 120-129 | and | <80 |
| Stage 1 Hypertension | 130-139 | or | 80-89 |
| Stage 2 Hypertension | >=140 | or | >=90 |
| Hypertensive Crisis | >=180 | and/or | >=120 |

- Adults with SBP or DBP in two categories should be designated to the higher BP category
- BP should be based on an average of >=2 careful readings on >=2 occasions [^23^]

**Comparison with JNC-7:**
| SBP | DBP | JNC-7 Classification | 2017 ACC/AHA |
|-----|-----|---------------------|--------------|
| <120 | <80 | Normal | Normal |
| 120-129 | <80 | Prehypertension | Elevated |
| 130-139 | 80-89 | Prehypertension | Stage 1 HTN |
| 140-159 | 90-99 | Stage 1 HTN | Stage 2 HTN |
| >=160 | >=100 | Stage 2 HTN | Stage 2 HTN |

### 1.3 Diagnostic Workup

**Initial Evaluation:**
- Confirm diagnosis with proper technique (seated, rested 5 min, appropriate cuff size)
- Out-of-office BP monitoring (ABPM or HBPM) recommended for white coat/masked HTN
- Physical exam: fundoscopic exam, thyroid, cardiac auscultation, abdominal bruits, peripheral pulses, edema
- Laboratory: BMP (creatinine, electrolytes, eGFR), fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipid panel, TSH, UA
- ECG to assess LVH, prior MI
- Consider echocardiogram if concern for LV dysfunction or LVH
- Consider sleep study if OSA suspected

**Secondary Hypertension Workup (indicated for):**
- Onset <30 years without risk factors or obesity
- Abrupt onset or worsening of previously controlled HTN
- Resistant hypertension (>=3 drugs including diuretic)
- Target organ damage disproportionate to HTN duration
- Hypokalemia with/without metabolic alkalosis (primary aldosteronism)
- Abdominal bruit (renal artery stenosis)
- Elevated creatinine after ACE-I/ARB initiation
- Family history of kidney disease or early CVA
- Symptoms suggestive of pheochromocytoma (episodic headache, sweating, palpitations)

**Common Secondary Causes:**
- Primary aldosteronism (most common, 5-10% of resistant HTN)
- Renal artery stenosis (atherosclerotic or fibromuscular dysplasia)
- Chronic kidney disease
- Obstructive sleep apnea
- Pheochromocytoma
- Cushing syndrome
- Coarctation of aorta
- Hyper/hypothyroidism
- Drug-induced (NSAIDs, OCPs, steroids, decongestants, stimulants, EPO)

### 1.4 Management

#### 1.4.1 Lifestyle Modifications (All Patients)

| Modification | Recommendation | Expected BP Reduction |
|-------------|----------------|---------------------|
| Weight loss | Achieve/maintain ideal body weight (BMI 18.5-24.9) | 5-20 mmHg/10 kg |
| DASH diet | Fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy; reduce saturated fat | 8-14 mmHg |
| Sodium restriction | <2,300 mg/day (ideally <1,500 mg/day) | 2-8 mmHg |
| Physical activity | >=150 min/week moderate aerobic activity | 4-9 mmHg |
| Alcohol moderation | Men: <=2 drinks/day; Women: <=1 drink/day | 2-4 mmHg |
| Smoking cessation | Absolute requirement | Immediate CVD benefit |
| Potassium intake | 3,500-5,000 mg/day (if no CKD/hyperkalemia) | 4-5 mmHg |

#### 1.4.2 Pharmacologic Treatment

**ACC/AHA Treatment Thresholds:**
- Stage 1 HTN (130-139/80-89): Pharmacologic therapy if clinical CVD or 10-year ASCVD risk >=10%
- Stage 1 HTN + ASCVD risk <10%: Lifestyle modification first for 3-6 months
- Stage 2 HTN (>=140/90): Pharmacologic therapy + lifestyle modification
- Target: <130/80 mmHg for most adults

**First-Line Agents:**

| Class | Examples | Key Considerations |
|-------|----------|--------------------|
| Thiazide diuretics | Chlorthalidone, HCTZ, indapamide | Preferred diuretic; monitor electrolytes, glucose, uric acid |
| ACE inhibitors | Lisinopril, enalapril | First-line with CKD, diabetes; check creatinine and K+ |
| ARBs | Losartan, valsartan, olmesartan | Alternative to ACE-I if cough; pregnancy contraindicated |
| Dihydropyridine CCBs | Amlodipine, nifedipine ER | Effective in Black patients; avoid in HFrEF |
| Non-dihydropyridine CCBs | Diltiazem, verapamil | Contraindicated in HFrEF; useful with atrial arrhythmias |

**Compelling Indications (Specific Agents):**
- HFrEF: ACE-I/ARB/ARNI + beta-blocker + MRA (GDMT)
- Post-MI: ACE-I + beta-blocker + MRA
- CKD with albuminuria: ACE-I or ARB
- Diabetes: ACE-I or ARB preferred
- Coronary artery disease: Beta-blocker + ACE-I
- Recurrent stroke prevention: Thiazide + ACE-I

**Combination Therapy:**
- Most patients will need >=2 agents to reach target
- Preferred combinations: ACE-I/ARB + CCB or ACE-I/ARB + thiazide
- Avoid ACE-I + ARB combination (increased adverse events, no outcome benefit)
- Single-pill combinations improve adherence

#### 1.4.3 Resistant Hypertension

**Definition:** BP remains >=130/80 mmHg despite use of 3 antihypertensive agents of different classes at maximally tolerated doses, including a diuretic, OR controlled BP requiring >=4 agents.

**Approach:**
1. Confirm adherence to medication regimen
2. Confirm proper BP measurement technique
3. Screen for secondary causes (especially primary aldosteronism)
4. Optimize current regimen (ensure diuretic at adequate dose)
5. Consider adding spironolactone (PATHWAY-2 trial: most effective 4th-line agent)
6. Other 4th-line options: beta-blockers, alpha-blockers, central agents, direct vasodilators
7. Referral to hypertension specialist
8. Consider renal denervation for select patients

### 1.5 Special Populations

| Population | Considerations |
|------------|---------------|
| Pregnancy | Methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine preferred; ACE-I/ARB/DRI contraindicated; target <140/90 |
| Children/Adolescents | BP >=95th percentile for age/sex/height; evaluate for secondary causes |
| Elderly (>65) | Target <130 mmHg SBP if tolerated; orthostatic BP monitoring; start low, go slow |
| Black patients | Thiazide or CCB preferred initial agents; ACE-I/ARB less effective as monotherapy |
| CKD | ACE-I or ARB if albuminuria; loop diuretics preferred if eGFR <30 |
| Diabetes | ACE-I or ARB preferred; target <130/80 |

### 1.6 Red Flags / Emergency Indicators

- Hypertensive emergency: BP >=180/120 WITH acute target organ damage (encephalopathy, papilledema, acute stroke, aortic dissection, acute coronary syndrome, acute pulmonary edema, acute kidney injury)
- Hypertensive urgency: BP >=180/120 WITHOUT target organ damage
- Preeclampsia/eclampsia
- Pheochromocytoma crisis

**Hypertensive Emergency Treatment:**
- Reduce MAP by no more than 10-20% in first hour
- Reduce to 160/100-110 over next 2-6 hours
- Avoid precipitous drops (risk of organ hypoperfusion)
- IV agents: nicardipine, clevidipine, nitroprusside, labetalol, hydralazine

## 2. HEART FAILURE

### 2.1 Clinical Overview

Heart failure is a clinical syndrome characterized by impaired ventricular filling or ejection of blood, leading to symptoms of dyspnea, fatigue, and fluid retention. Classification is based on left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF):

| Classification | LVEF | Description |
|---------------|------|-------------|
| HFrEF | <=40% | Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction |
| HFmrEF | 41-49% | Heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction |
| HFpEF | >=50% | Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction |
| HFimpEF | >40% (improved from <=40%) | Improved ejection fraction |

### 2.2 NYHA Functional Classification

| Class | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| I | No limitation of physical activity; ordinary activity does not cause symptoms |
| II | Slight limitation; comfortable at rest; ordinary physical activity causes symptoms |
| III | Marked limitation; comfortable at rest; less than ordinary activity causes symptoms |
| IV | Unable to carry out physical activity without symptoms; symptoms at rest |

### 2.3 Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT) for HFrEF

The cornerstone of HFrEF management is **quadruple therapy** consisting of four foundational drug classes [^28^] [^19^].

#### 2.3.1 The Four Pillars of HFrEF Therapy

**1. ARNI (Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitor)**
- **Sacubitril/Valsartan** - preferred over ACE-I/ARB
- Dosing: Start 24/26 mg or 49/51 mg BID; target 97/103 mg BID
- Reduces mortality and HF hospitalizations vs. enalapril (PARADIGM-HF trial)
- Contraindicated with ACE-I (48-hour washout required) or aliskiren
- Side effects: hypotension, hyperkalemia, angioedema (lower risk than ACE-I)
- **Class I recommendation (Level A evidence)**

**2. Beta-Blocker**
- **Evidence-based agents:** Carvedilol, metoprolol succinate (XL), bisoprolol
- Dosing (target doses):
  - Carvedilol: Start 3.125 mg BID; target 25 mg BID (50 mg BID if >85 kg)
  - Metoprolol succinate: Start 25 mg daily; target 200 mg daily
  - Bisoprolol: Start 1.25 mg daily; target 10 mg daily
- Start low, titrate every 2 weeks to target
- Contraindications: severe bradycardia, decompensated HF, severe asthma
- **Class I recommendation (Level A evidence)**

**3. MRA (Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist)**
- **Spironolactone** or **Eplerenone**
- Dosing:
  - Spironolactone: Start 12.5-25 mg daily; target 25-50 mg daily
  - Eplerenone: Start 25 mg daily; target 50 mg daily
- Monitor potassium and creatinine at 1 week, 1 month, then every 3 months
- Contraindications: K+ >5.0, eGFR <30, severe hyperkalemia risk
- **Class I recommendation (Level A evidence)**

**4. SGLT2 Inhibitor**
- **Dapagliflozin** 10 mg daily or **Empagliflozin** 10 mg daily
- Reduces CV death and HF hospitalization (DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced trials)
- Benefits independent of diabetes status
- Added as Class I recommendation in 2022 ACC/AHA/HFSA guidelines [^19^]
- Class I for HFrEF; Class 2a for HFpEF and HFmrEF
- Also provides renal protection benefits
- Side effects: genital mycotic infections, volume depletion, rare euglycemic DKA
- **Class I recommendation (Level A evidence)**

#### 2.3.2 Additional GDMT Agents

| Agent | Indication | Evidence |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| Hydralazine/Isosorbide dinitrate | HFrEF in Black patients (self-identified) | A-HeFT trial; Class I |
| Ivabradine | HFrEF, sinus rhythm, HR >=70 on max beta-blocker | SHIFT trial; Class IIa |
| Diuretics | Volume overload (loop diuretics) | Symptom relief; Class I |
| Digoxin | Symptomatic HFrEF despite GDMT (add-on) | DIG trial; Class IIb |
| Vericiguat | Worsening HFrEF on GDMT | VICTORIA trial; Class IIb |
| Finerenone | HFrEF with T2DM | FINEARTS-HF; emerging |

#### 2.3.3 Quadruple Therapy Initiation Strategy

Current guidelines recommend **rapid initiation** of all four foundational therapies [^28^]:
- Initiate low doses of all 4 classes within 1 week (Greene et al. approach)
- Titrate doses over 1 month based on tolerance
- For hospitalized patients: start GDMT before discharge if hemodynamically stable
- No need to wait 6 months for sequential titration (historical paradigm is outdated)
- Estimated RR reduction in all-cause mortality with quadruple therapy: 73%

### 2.4 HFpEF Management

**Diagnostic Criteria (H2FPEF Score):**
- Heavy (BMI >30): 2 points
- Hypertensive (>=2 antihypertensives): 1 point
- Atrial fibrillation (paroxysmal or persistent): 3 points
- Pulmonary hypertension (PASP >35 mmHg): 1 point
- Elderly (>60 years): 1 point
- Filling pressure (E/e' >=9): 1 point
- Score 0-1: Low probability; 2-5: Intermediate; 6-9: High probability

**Management:**
- SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin or empagliflozin) - **Class IIa, Level B** [^19^]
- Diuretics for volume management
- Blood pressure control (target <130/80)
- Management of comorbidities (AF, diabetes, obesity, sleep apnea, CKD)
- ARNI may be considered (PARAGON-HF subgroup analysis)
- Avoid routine use of nitrates and phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors
- No mortality benefit demonstrated with ACE-I, ARB, or beta-blockers in HFpEF

### 2.5 Acute Decompensated Heart Failure (ADHF)

**Presentation:**
- Warm vs. Cold perfusion (assess extremities, mentation, urine output, BP)
- Wet vs. Dry volume status (assess JVP, pulmonary congestion, edema)
- Four hemodynamic profiles:
  - Warm and Dry: Compensated (continue outpatient GDMT)
  - Warm and Wet: Volume overload (diurese)
  - Cold and Dry: Hypoperfusion, euvolemic (inotropes)
  - Cold and Wet: Cardiogenic shock (inotropes + diuresis)

**Management:**
- IV loop diuretics for all patients with fluid overload (furosemide 20-160 mg IV) [^85^]
- Vasodilators (nitroglycerin, nitroprusside) for afterload reduction if SBP >110
- NPPV (BiPAP/CPAP) for acute pulmonary edema
- Inotropes (dobutamine, milrinone) for cold cardiogenic shock
- Vasopressors (norepinephrine) if hypotensive
- Urgent echocardiography for hemodynamically unstable patients
- Early SGLT2i initiation may facilitate decongestion (DICTATE-AHF trial) [^86^]
- Acetazolamide + loop diuretics may improve decongestion (ADVOR trial) [^86^]

**Discharge Planning:**
- Continue/optimize all 4 GDMT pillars before discharge
- Early follow-up within 1 week (cardiology within 2 weeks)
- Daily weight monitoring, sodium restriction
- Cardiac rehabilitation referral

### 2.6 Red Flags

- Acute pulmonary edema (frothy pink sputum, severe dyspnea, hypoxemia)
- Cardiogenic shock (cold extremities, altered mental status, oliguria, hypotension)
- Syncope in HFrEF (suspect arrhythmia)
- ICD shocks (call electrophysiology)
- Refractory volume overload despite IV diuretics

## 3. CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE

### 3.1 Clinical Overview

Coronary artery disease (CAD) encompasses a spectrum from chronic stable angina to acute coronary syndromes (ACS). The 2024 ESC Guidelines use the term "Chronic Coronary Syndrome" (CCS), while the 2023 ACC/AHA Guidelines use "Chronic Coronary Disease" (CCD) [^78^].

### 3.2 Clinical Presentations

**Chronic Coronary Syndrome/CCD Populations:**
1. Post-ACS or post-revascularization (non-acute)
2. HF of ischemic/cardiometabolic origin
3. Symptomatic with reproducible stress-induced angina (obstructive CAD)
4. Coronary vasomotor abnormalities/microvascular angina (ANOCA/INOCA)
5. Asymptomatic patients with screening test abnormalities

### 3.3 Stable Angina vs. Acute Coronary Syndrome

| Feature | Stable Angina | ACS (NSTEMI/UA) | STEMI |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| Pain pattern | Predictable, exertional | Prolonged (>20 min), rest or minimal exertion | Severe, crushing, >30 min |
| Response to NTG | Reliable relief | Variable/no relief | Minimal/no relief |
| ECG | Normal or stable ST changes | ST depression, T-wave inversion, dynamic changes | ST elevation >=1 mm (2 contiguous leads) |
| Troponin | Normal | Elevated (NSTEMI) or normal (UA) | Markedly elevated |
| Urgency | Outpatient workup | Urgent (within hours) | Emergency (within minutes) |

### 3.4 ACS Management

#### 3.4.1 STEMI Protocol

**Goal: Door-to-balloon <90 minutes, door-to-needle <30 minutes**

1. **Immediate:**
   - Aspirin 325 mg (chewed) + P2Y12 inhibitor (ticagrelor, prasugrel, or clopidogrel)
   - Anticoagulation (heparin or bivalirudin)
   - Primary PCI (preferred) vs. thrombolysis if PCI not available within 120 minutes
   - High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 40-80 mg or rosuvastatin 20-40 mg)
   - Beta-blocker (unless contraindicated)
   - Nitroglycerin for pain relief

2. **Reperfusion:**
   - Primary PCI is gold standard
   - Fibrinolysis if FMC-to-device >120 minutes
   - Rescue PCI if failed fibrinolysis
   - Routine staging of non-culprit lesions (COMPLETE trial)

3. **Post-PCI:**
   - DAPT duration: 12 months (aspirin + P2Y12)
   - Consider shortening to 3-6 months in high bleeding risk
   - High-intensity statin lifelong
   - ACE-I/ARB, beta-blocker
   - Cardiac rehabilitation

#### 3.4.2 NSTEMI Protocol

1. **Risk stratification:** Use GRACE score
2. **Immediate:**
   - Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor
   - Anticoagulation (enoxaparin, fondaparinux, or UFH)
   - High-intensity statin
   - Beta-blocker
   - Anti-ischemic therapy
3. **Timing of angiography:**
   - Immediate (<2 hours): Refractory angina, hemodynamic instability, VT/VF
   - Early (<24 hours): GRACE score >140, dynamic ECG changes, elevated troponin
   - Delayed (24-72 hours): Intermediate-risk patients
4. **Conservative strategy:** Consider in low-risk patients with no recurrent symptoms

### 3.5 Risk Stratification Scores

**GRACE Score (for ACS):**
- Predicts in-hospital and 6-month mortality
- Variables: Age, heart rate, systolic BP, creatinine, Killip class, ST deviation, cardiac arrest, elevated troponin
- Score <=108: Low risk; 109-140: Intermediate; >140: High risk

**TIMI Score (for NSTEMI/UA):**
- Predicts 14-day all-cause mortality, new/recurrent MI, or severe recurrent ischemia
- 7 variables: Age >=65, >=3 CAD risk factors, known CAD, ASA use in past 7 days, severe angina (>=2 episodes in 24h), ST deviation, elevated cardiac markers
- Score 0-2: Low risk; 3-4: Intermediate; 5-7: High risk

## 4. ARRHYTHMIAS

### 4.1 ATRIAL FIBRILLATION (AF)

#### 4.1.1 Clinical Overview

AF is the most common sustained arrhythmia, affecting approximately 2-4% of adults. Risk increases with age. It is associated with a 5-fold increased risk of stroke and 2-fold increased mortality.

#### 4.1.2 Classification

| Type | Duration |
|------|----------|
| Paroxysmal | Terminates spontaneously or with intervention within 7 days |
| Persistent | Continuous >7 days |
| Long-standing persistent | Continuous >12 months |
| Permanent | Accepted as ongoing (joint decision) |

#### 4.1.3 CHA2DS2-VASc Score (Stroke Risk)

| Risk Factor | Points |
|-------------|--------|
| Congestive heart failure | 1 |
| Hypertension | 1 |
| Age >=75 years | 2 |
| Diabetes mellitus | 1 |
| Prior Stroke/TIA/thromboembolism | 2 |
| Vascular disease (CAD, PAD, aortic plaque) | 1 |
| Age 65-74 years | 1 |
| Sex category (female) | 1 |

**Anticoagulation Recommendations:**
- Score 0 (male) or 1 (female): No anticoagulation (consider if additional risk factors)
- Score 1 (male) or 2 (female): Consider anticoagulation
- Score >=2 (male) or >=3 (female): Recommend anticoagulation
- **DOACs preferred over warfarin** for non-valvular AF (Class I) [^16^]

#### 4.1.4 HAS-BLED Score (Bleeding Risk)

| Risk Factor | Points |
|-------------|--------|
| Hypertension (uncontrolled, SBP >160) | 1 |
| Abnormal renal/liver function (1 point each) | 1-2 |
| Stroke history | 1 |
| Bleeding history or predisposition | 1 |
| Labile INR (if on warfarin) | 1 |
| Elderly (age >65) | 1 |
| Drugs/alcohol (1 point each) | 1-2 |

- Score >=3: High bleeding risk (does NOT contraindicate anticoagulation)
- Use to identify modifiable bleeding risk factors
- Do NOT stop anticoagulation based on bleeding risk alone [^16^]

#### 4.1.5 Rate vs. Rhythm Control

**Rate Control:**
- First-line for most patients
- Target resting HR <110 bpm (lenient) or <80 bpm (strict)
- Agents: Beta-blockers (metoprolol, carvedilol), non-DHP CCBs (diltiazem, verapamil), digoxin
- Avoid AV nodal blockers in WPW/pre-excited AF
- Digoxin reinstated as first-line option in 2024 ESC guidelines [^16^]

**Rhythm Control:**
- Consider for: symptomatic patients despite rate control, young patients, difficulty achieving rate control, tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy
- Antiarrhythmic drugs: Flecainide, propafenone (structurally normal hearts), amiodarone (structural heart disease), dofetilide, sotalol
- Catheter ablation: Class I for paroxysmal AF (first-line in 2024 ESC), Class I for AF with HF/tachycardiomyopathy [^16^]
- AF ablation improves outcomes in HF with reduced or preserved EF (CASTLE-AF, CABANA trials)

#### 4.1.6 Post-Cardioversion Anticoagulation

- Anticoagulate for at least 3 weeks before and 4 weeks after cardioversion
- If TEE excludes thrombus: may cardiovert immediately with heparin
- Minimum 4 weeks anticoagulation after cardioversion even if CHA2DS2-VA = 0 (2024 ESC)

### 4.2 SUPRAVENTRICULAR TACHYCARDIA (SVT)

#### 4.2.1 Types and Features

| Type | Mechanism | ECG Features |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| AVNRT | Re-entry in AV node | Regular narrow-complex, P wave buried in QRS or retrograde |
| AVRT (orthodromic) | Accessory pathway-mediated | Regular narrow-complex, retrograde P waves |
| Atrial tachycardia | Ectopic atrial focus | Regular narrow-complex, abnormal P wave morphology |
| Atrial flutter | Re-entry in right atrium | Sawtooth flutter waves at ~300 bpm, 2:1 or variable block |

#### 4.2.2 Acute Management [^29^] [^32^]

**Step 1: Assess hemodynamic stability**
- Unstable (hypotension, altered mental status, chest pain, acute HF): Synchronized cardioversion

**Step 2: Vagal maneuvers (if stable)**
- Modified Valsalva: Semi-recumbent -> strain 15 sec -> supine with legs elevated 45 degrees for 15 sec
- Carotid sinus massage (auscultate for bruits first; avoid if bruit, recent CVA/MI)
- Success rate: 19-43% (modified Valsalva superior to standard) [^29^]

**Step 3: Adenosine (if vagal fails)**
- 6 mg rapid IV push followed by saline flush
- If no conversion: 12 mg IV push
- May repeat 12 mg once
- Almost all AVNRT/AVRT will terminate [^29^]
- Brief asystole expected; warn patient
- Contraindicated in heart transplant recipients (use 1/4 dose)

**Step 4: Alternative agents (if adenosine fails)**
- Diltiazem or verapamil IV (avoid in WPW with AF)
- Beta-blockers IV (metoprolol, esmolol)
- Synchronized cardioversion if remains unstable

#### 4.2.3 Ongoing Management [^29^]

- Oral beta-blocker, diltiazem, or verapamil for recurrent SVT (without pre-excitation)
- Catheter ablation: Curative (>95% success for AVNRT), Class I recommendation
- Patients should learn vagal maneuvers for self-management
- Flecainide or propafenone (pill-in-the-pocket) for infrequent episodes

### 4.3 VENTRICULAR TACHYCARDIA (VT)

#### 4.3.1 Classification

- **Monomorphic VT:** Single QRS morphology; typically scar-related
- **Polymorphic VT:** Changing QRS morphology; consider ischemia, electrolytes, prolonged QT
- **Torsades de Pointes:** Polymorphic VT with twisting QRS axis; associated with prolonged QT

#### 4.3.2 Acute Management (ACLS Algorithm) [^54^] [^59^]

**Pulseless VT:** Treat as VF - Immediate unsynchronized defibrillation (200 J biphasic), CPR, epinephrine, amiodarone

**Stable VT with Pulse:**
1. Wide-complex regular: Consider adenosine if regular and monomorphic
2. IV antiarrhythmics:
   - **Amiodarone:** 150 mg IV over 10 minutes; repeat as needed; maintenance 1 mg/min x 6 hours
   - **Procainamide:** 20-50 mg/min until arrhythmia suppressed, hypotension, QRS widens >50%, or max 17 mg/kg
   - **Sotalol:** 100 mg (1.5 mg/kg) IV over 5 minutes (avoid if prolonged QT)
3. Synchronized cardioversion if becomes unstable (100 J for regular wide-complex, 200 J for irregular)

**Polymorphic VT:**
- Check and correct: K+, Mg2+, ischemia
- IV magnesium sulfate for Torsades de Pointes (2 g IV over 10-15 min)
- Defibrillation if pulseless or unstable
- Consider overdrive pacing or isoproterenol for Torsades

### 4.4 BRADYARRHYTHMIAS

#### 4.4.1 Types

| Condition | Features | Management |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| Sinus bradycardia | Rate <60 bpm, normal P waves before each QRS | Treat underlying cause; atropine if symptomatic |
| Sinus node dysfunction (sick sinus) | Sinus pauses, bradycardia-tachycardia syndrome | Pacemaker if symptomatic |
| First-degree AV block | PR >200 ms | Observation; no specific treatment |
| Second-degree AV block (Mobitz I/Wenckebach) | Progressive PR prolongation until dropped beat | Observation if asymptomatic; pacemaker if symptomatic |
| Second-degree AV block (Mobitz II) | Fixed PR with intermittent non-conducted P waves | **Permanent pacemaker** (risk of progression to complete heart block) |
| Third-degree AV block | Complete AV dissociation | **Permanent pacemaker** (unless reversible cause) |

#### 4.4.2 ACLS for Symptomatic Bradycardia
1. Atropine 0.5 mg IV q3-5 min (max 3 mg)
2. Transcutaneous pacing if atropine ineffective
3. Dopamine infusion 2-20 mcg/kg/min or epinephrine 2-10 mcg/min
4. Definitive pacing (transvenous or permanent)

## 5. VALVULAR HEART DISEASE

### 5.1 Aortic Stenosis (AS)

#### 5.1.1 Clinical Features
- Classic triad: Angina, syncope, dyspnea/heart failure (indicates severe disease)
- Harsh crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur at right upper sternal border, radiating to carotids
- Delayed and diminished carotid upstrokes (pulsus parvus et tardus)
- Ejection click may be present (bicuspid valve)

#### 5.1.2 Severity Assessment

| Parameter | Mild | Moderate | Severe |
|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| Aortic valve area (AVA) | >1.5 cm2 | 1.0-1.5 cm2 | <1.0 cm2 |
| Mean gradient | <20 mmHg | 20-40 mmHg | >=40 mmHg |
| Jet velocity | <3.0 m/s | 3.0-4.0 m/s | >4.0 m/s |

#### 5.1.3 Management [^17^]

**Severe AS Treatment:**
- Symptomatic severe AS: **Intervention is indicated regardless of LVEF** (Class I)
- Asymptomatic severe AS with LVEF <50%: Intervention indicated (Class I)
- Asymptomatic severe AS with high gradient, normal LVEF: Intervention should be considered as alternative to surveillance if low procedural risk (Class IIa, Level A) [^17^]
- Non-cardiac surgery: Safe if asymptomatic with MVA >1.5 cm2; otherwise valve intervention first

**Intervention Options:**
- **SAVR (Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement):** Gold standard for low-risk patients, younger patients
- **TAVI/TAVR (Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation):** Preferred for high/intermediate risk, elderly; expanding to low-risk patients [^17^]
- Choice based on: Age, life expectancy, procedural risk, anatomical suitability (Heart Team decision)
- Bioprosthetic vs. mechanical valve based on age and patient preference

**Follow-up:**
- Asymptomatic severe AS: Every 6 months
- Moderate AS: Yearly
- Exercise testing if symptoms uncertain

### 5.2 Aortic Regurgitation (AR)

#### 5.2.1 Clinical Features
- High-pitched decrescendo diastolic murmur at left sternal border
- Wide pulse pressure, water-hammer pulse, Quincke pulses, head bobbing
- Austin Flint murmur (functional MS from AR jet)

#### 5.2.2 Management [^17^]
**Surgical Indications:**
- Symptomatic severe AR (Class I)
- Asymptomatic severe AR with LV dysfunction (LVESD >50 mm or LVESDi >25 mm/m2, or LVEF <50%) (Class I)
- Asymptomatic severe AR with LV dilation approaching surgical thresholds (Class IIa)
- SAVR is preferred; TAVI may be considered for inoperable patients with suitable anatomy (Class IIb)

### 5.3 Mitral Regurgitation (MR)

#### 5.3.1 Classification
- **Primary MR:** Structural abnormality of mitral apparatus (prolapse, rheumatic, endocarditis, chordal rupture)
- **Secondary MR:** Normal valve leaflets; MR due to LV/LA dilation (ventricular SMR) or atrial dilation (atrial SMR)

#### 5.3.2 Management of Primary MR [^17^]
- Surgical mitral valve **repair** is preferred over replacement (Class I)
- Indicated in: symptomatic patients; asymptomatic with LV dysfunction (LVESD >=40 mm, LVESDi >=20 mm/m2, or LVEF <=60%)
- TEER (transcatheter edge-to-edge repair): For high-surgical-risk patients with suitable anatomy (Class IIa)
- Minimally invasive MV repair at experienced centers reduces LOS (Class IIb)

#### 5.3.3 Management of Secondary MR [^17^]
1. **Optimize GDMT** first (ACE-I/ARB/ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) - about 40% improve after 1-3 months
2. CRT if indicated (LVEF <=35%, wide QRS)
3. **M-TEER (MitraClip):** Recommended in symptomatic patients on optimal GDMT without CAD needing revascularization (Class I for COAPT-like patients)
4. MV surgery may be considered with concomitant CAD or unsuitable for TEER

### 5.4 Prosthetic Valves

#### 5.4.1 Antithrombotic Therapy [^17^]
**Mechanical Valves:**
- Lifelong VKA (warfarin) required
- Target INR: 2.5 (aortic) or 3.0 (mitral); higher (3.0-4.0) for older generation valves or additional risk factors
- Add low-dose aspirin (75-100 mg)
- DOACs contraindicated in mechanical valves

**Bioprosthetic Valves:**
- VKA for 3 months post-surgery (target INR 2.5) or
- Aspirin lifelong
- DOACs may be used (evidence growing)

#### 5.4.2 Surveillance
- Baseline echocardiogram post-implantation
- Annual echocardiography for bioprosthetic valves
- Clinical evaluation if new symptoms or murmur
- INR monitoring for mechanical valves

### 5.5 Sources

- [^17^] 2025 ESC/EACTS Guidelines for Valvular Heart Disease: https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf
- 2020 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Management of Patients With Valvular Heart Disease
- Otto CM, Nishimura RA, et al. 2020 ACC/AHA VHD Guideline. Circulation. 2021;143:e72-e227.

## 6. CHEST PAIN EVALUATION

### 6.1 Clinical Overview

Chest pain accounts for approximately 6-8 million emergency department visits annually in the United States. The primary goal is to identify life-threatening causes (ACS, aortic dissection, PE, tension pneumothorax) while avoiding unnecessary admissions for low-risk patients.

### 6.2 HEART Score [^20^] [^31^]

The HEART score is the most widely validated risk stratification tool for chest pain patients in the ED.

| Component | 0 Points | 1 Point | 2 Points |
|-----------|----------|---------|----------|
| **H**istory | Slightly/non-suspicious | Moderately suspicious | Highly suspicious |
| **E**CG | Normal | Nonspecific repolarization disturbance | Significant ST depression |
| **A**ge | <=45 years | >45 to <65 years | >=65 years |
| **R**isk factors | No known risk factors | 1-2 risk factors | >=3 risk factors or history of atherosclerotic disease |
| **T**roponin | <=Normal limit | >1 to <3x normal limit | >=3x normal limit |

**Risk Categories:**
- **0-3 (Low risk):** Safe for early discharge or outpatient management; 1.7% MACE at 6 weeks
- **4-6 (Moderate risk):** Observation unit admission; serial troponins; functional testing; 16.6% MACE
- **7-10 (High risk):** Immediate cardiology consultation; admission for invasive management; 50.1% MACE

**Key Points:**
- Low-risk patients (0-3) with 0 initial troponin may be safely discharged
- The HEART pathway (HEART score + 0- and 3-hour troponins) has been shown to reduce admissions by 20-40%
- Pooled sensitivity: 96%; specificity: 50% [^20^]
- Superior to GRACE score for predicting MACE in undifferentiated chest pain [^20^]

### 6.3 TIMI Score [^20^]

| Risk Factor | Points |
|-------------|--------|
| Age >=65 years | 1 |
| >=3 CAD risk factors | 1 |
| Known CAD (stenosis >=50%) | 1 |
| ASA use in past 7 days | 1 |
| Severe angina (>=2 episodes in 24 hours) | 1 |
| ST deviation >=0.5 mm | 1 |
| Elevated cardiac markers | 1 |

- **0-2: Low risk (4.7% event rate)**
- **3-4: Intermediate risk (19.2% event rate)**
- **5-7: High risk (41.1% event rate)**
- Designed for NSTEMI/UA patients; less sensitive for all-comer chest pain [^20^]

### 6.4 GRACE Score
- More comprehensive; includes vital signs and creatinine
- Originally designed for ACS patients (not undifferentiated chest pain)
- Predicts in-hospital and 6-month mortality
- Inferior to HEART and TIMI for MACE prediction in undifferentiated chest pain [^20^]

### 6.5 Differential Diagnosis

#### 6.5.1 Life-Threatening Causes (Must Not Miss)

| Condition | Key Features | Diagnostic Approach |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Acute Coronary Syndrome** | Pressure/crushing pain, radiation to arm/jaw, diaphoresis, nausea, troponin elevation | ECG, serial troponins (0, 3, 6h), coronary angiography |
| **Aortic Dissection** | Tearing pain radiating to back, BP differential, pulse deficits, new neurologic deficits | CTA chest/abdomen/pelvis, TEE |
| **Pulmonary Embolism** | Pleuritic pain, dyspnea, tachypnea, tachycardia, risk factors for DVT | Wells score, D-dimer, CTPA |
| **Tension Pneumothorax** | Sudden unilateral pain, hypotension, tracheal deviation, absent breath sounds | Clinical diagnosis; CXR if stable; immediate needle decompression |
| **Cardiac Tamponade** | Hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds (Beck's triad), pulsus paradoxus | Bedside echocardiography |
| **Esophageal Rupture** | Severe pain after vomiting (Boerhaave), subcutaneous emphysema, sepsis | Contrast esophagram, CT chest |

#### 6.5.2 Non-Life-Threatening Causes

| Category | Conditions | Distinguishing Features |
|----------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Cardiac** | Stable angina, myocarditis, pericarditis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy | Pericarditis: friction rub, diffuse ST elevation, PR depression |
| **Pulmonary** | Pneumonia, pneumothorax (simple), pleurisy, COPD exacerbation | Fever, cough, sputum, focal findings on exam |
| **GI** | GERD, esophageal spasm, peptic ulcer disease, biliary colic, pancreatitis | Relation to meals, relief with antacids, abdominal tenderness |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Costochondritis, rib fracture, muscle strain | Reproducible chest wall tenderness |
| **Psychogenic** | Panic attack, anxiety | Associated paresthesias, hyperventilation, normal vitals |
| **Other** | Herpes zoster, breast pathology | Vesicular rash, breast mass/tenderness |

- [^31^] HEART Score for Assessment (PMC): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12247455/

## 7. ASTHMA

### 7.1 Clinical Overview

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the airways characterized by variable symptoms of wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough, with variable expiratory airflow limitation. The GINA (Global Initiative for Asthma) 2024 guidelines represent the current standard of care [^21^] [^65^].

### 7.2 Diagnosis
- Compatible symptoms (wheeze, SOB, chest tightness, cough - especially nocturnal/early morning)
- Documented variable expiratory airflow limitation:
  - FEV1/FVC < lower limit of normal
  - Bronchodilator reversibility: FEV1 increase >12% and >200 mL from baseline
  - Excessive variability in twice-daily PEF over 2 weeks
  - Positive bronchoprovocation test (methacholine, exercise, mannitol)
- Consider FeNO (fractional exhaled nitric oxide) as adjunct; >=20 ppb supports T2 inflammation

### 7.3 GINA 2024 Stepwise Therapy [^21^] [^65^]

**Key Change:** SABA-only reliever therapy is NO LONGER recommended as Step 1. All adults and adolescents should use ICS-containing inhalers.

| Step | Controller | Reliever | Population |
|------|-----------|----------|------------|
| **1** | Low-dose ICS-formoterol as-needed | Same as controller | Mild/occasional symptoms |
| **2** | Low-dose ICS-formoterol maintenance + reliever | ICS-formoterol as-needed | Persistent symptoms |
| **3** | Low-dose ICS-formoterol maintenance and reliever (SMART) | ICS-formoterol | Maintenance needed |
| **4** | Medium-dose ICS-formoterol maintenance and reliever (SMART) | ICS-formoterol | Poor control on Step 3 |
| **5** | High-dose ICS-formoterol + LAMA + biologic | ICS-formoterol | Severe asthma |

**Anti-Inflammatory Reliever (AIR) Concept:**
- As-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol reduces severe exacerbations by 60-64% vs. SABA-only [^21^]
- Preferred reliever over SABA in Steps 1-5
- SMART (Single Maintenance and Reliever Therapy) with ICS-formoterol reduces exacerbations vs. ICS/LABA + SABA

### 7.4 Pharmacologic Agents

**Controller Medications:**

| Class | Examples | Notes |
|-------|----------|-------|
| Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) | Budesonide, fluticasone, mometasone, beclomethasone | Cornerstone of therapy; reduce airway inflammation |
| ICS/LABA combinations | Budesonide-formoterol, fluticasone-salmeterol, mometasone-indacaterol | Formoterol preferred for SMART approach |
| LAMA | Tiotropium, glycopyrrolate | Add-on for Steps 4-5 |
| LTRA | Montelukast | Alternative to ICS (less preferred); watch for neuropsychiatric effects |
| Biologics | See below | Step 5 severe asthma |

### 7.5 Biologics for Severe Asthma [^57^] [^60^] [^64^]

**Seven FDA-Approved Biologics:**

| Biologic | Target | Indication | Key Features |
|----------|--------|-----------|--------------|
| **Omalizumab** | Anti-IgE | Allergic asthma (IgE-mediated), age >=6 | Positive skin test/RAST; IgE 30-1500 IU/mL; SC q2-4 weeks |
| **Mepolizumab** | Anti-IL-5 | Eosinophilic asthma, age >=6 | Eos >=150/μL; SC monthly; also EGPA |
| **Reslizumab** | Anti-IL-5 | Eosinophilic asthma, age >=18 | Eos >=400/μL; IV infusion monthly; weight-based dosing |
| **Benralizumab** | Anti-IL-5R | Eosinophilic asthma, age >=12 | Eos >=150/μL; SC q4 weeks (then q8); depletes eosinophils |
| **Dupilumab** | Anti-IL-4Rα | T2-high asthma, age >=6 | Eos >=150/μL or FeNO >=25 ppb; blocks IL-4 and IL-13; also atopic dermatitis, CRSwNP |
| **Tezepelumab** | Anti-TSLP | Severe asthma (broad), age >=12 | Any phenotype; upstream target; broadest applicability |
| **Depemokimab** | Anti-IL-5 | Eosinophilic asthma (2025 approval) | Ultra-long-acting; q6 months |

**Biologic Selection Algorithm:**
- **T2-high allergic asthma** (early onset, elevated IgE, positive skin test): Omalizumab first-line; dupilumab or tezepelumab as alternatives [^53^]
- **T2-high eosinophilic asthma** (elevated eosinophils): Mepolizumab or benralizumab first-line; dupilumab or tezepelumab as alternatives [^53^]
- **T2-high with moderate eosinophilia or raised FeNO**: Dupilumab preferred (dual IL-4/IL-13 blockade) [^53^]
- **T2-low asthma**: Tezepelumab (TSLP blockade works across phenotypes)

**Response Assessment:**
- Assess at 16-24 weeks
- Targets: >=50% reduction in severe exacerbations, >=10 ppb FeNO drop, or >=0.1L FEV1 gain
- Consider switching if inadequate response

### 7.6 Asthma Exacerbation Management

**Assessment:**
- PEF or FEV1; oxygen saturation; ability to speak; accessory muscle use; mental status
- Blood gas if severe (respiratory acidosis indicates impending respiratory failure)

**Treatment:**
1. **Oxygen:** Maintain SpO2 93-95% (88-92% if at risk of hypercapnia)
2. **SABA:** MDI with spacer or nebulizer (albuterol/salbutamol) every 20 minutes x 3
3. **Systemic corticosteroids:** Prednisone/prednisolone 40-50 mg PO daily for 5-7 days; OR hydrocortisone IV
4. **Ipratropium bromide:** Add to SABA for severe exacerbations
5. **Magnesium sulfate:** 2 g IV over 20 minutes if severe (FEV1 <25-30% predicted)
6. **BiPAP/Intubation:** If progressive hypercapnia, exhaustion, altered mental status
7. **Avoid:** Sedatives, mucolytics, chest physiotherapy (not evidence-based)

**Discharge Criteria:**
- SABA can be spaced to every 4 hours
- PEF >60-70% personal best or predicted
- Patient can use inhaler correctly
- Written asthma action plan provided
- Follow-up arranged within 1 week

### 7.7 Special Populations
- **Pregnancy:** Budesonide is preferred ICS; maintain good control (risk of uncontrolled asthma > risk of medications); montelukast and biologics generally safe
- **Children:** Low-dose ICS first-line; step up cautiously; growth monitoring with ICS
- **Elderly:** Higher risk of ICS side effects (pneumonia, osteoporosis); lower doses preferred

## 8. COPD

### 8.1 Clinical Overview

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a common, preventable, and treatable disease characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation due to airway and/or alveolar abnormalities, usually caused by significant exposure to noxious particles or gases. The GOLD (Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease) 2024 and 2026 reports provide current guidance [^56^] [^61^] [^62^].

### 8.2 Diagnosis and Assessment

**Spirometric Criteria:**
- Post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC <0.70 confirms persistent airflow limitation
- Degree of severity:
  - GOLD 1 (Mild): FEV1 >=80% predicted
  - GOLD 2 (Moderate): FEV1 50-79%
  - GOLD 3 (Severe): FEV1 30-49%
  - GOLD 4 (Very severe): FEV1 <30%

**ABE Assessment (GOLD 2024):**

| Group | Exacerbation History | Symptoms (mMRC/CAT) | Initial Treatment |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| A | 0-1 moderate, no severe | mMRC 0-1 or CAT <10 | LAMA or LABA |
| B | 0-1 moderate, no severe | mMRC >=2 or CAT >=10 | LAMA + LABA |
| E | >=2 moderate or >=1 severe leading to hospitalization | Any | LAMA + LABA; consider +ICS if eos >=300/μL |

**Key Change (GOLD 2026):** Groups C and D consolidated into Group E (Exacerbation-prone). Any patient with >=1 moderate/severe exacerbation in the previous year is considered high risk and should start LABA/LAMA [^61^].

### 8.3 Pharmacologic Treatment [^56^] [^62^]

**Initial Pharmacotherapy:**

| Group | Preferred Initial Therapy |
|-------|--------------------------|
| A | Long-acting bronchodilator (LAMA or LABA) |
| B | LAMA + LABA (dual bronchodilation) |
| E | LAMA + LABA; consider triple therapy if eos >=300/μL |

**Blood Eosinophil Count (BEC) and ICS Response:**
- BEC >=300 cells/μL: Strong predictor of benefit from ICS
- BEC 100-300: Possible benefit
- BEC <100: Unlikely to benefit; pneumonia risk with ICS
- Obtain 2-3 BEC measurements before starting ICS due to variability [^61^]

**Triple Therapy (LABA + LAMA + ICS):**
- **GOLD 2024/2026 strongly prefer triple therapy over LABA/ICS** when ICS indicated [^56^]
- Evidence from IMPACT and ETHOS trials:
  - Reduced moderate/severe exacerbations by 13-15% vs. LABA/ICS
  - Reduced all-cause mortality by 42-49% vs. LABA/LAMA [^56^]
- Initiate when: Group E with BEC >=300/μL, or if LABA/LAMA inadequate control

**Specific Agents:**

| Class | Examples | Notes |
|-------|----------|-------|
| LAMA | Tiotropium, umeclidinium, glycopyrrolate, aclidinium | Once or twice daily |
| LABA | Salmeterol, formoterol, indacaterol, vilanterol, olodaterol | Combined with LAMA or ICS |
| ICS | Fluticasone, budesonide, beclomethasone | Risk of pneumonia with long-term use |
| Fixed triple | FF/UMEC/VI (Trelegy), B/G/FF | Once daily single inhaler |
| Roflumilast | PDE4 inhibitor | Add-on in severe COPD with chronic bronchitis, FEV1 <50% |
| Azithromycin | Macrolide | Prophylactic 250-500 mg 3x/week in frequent exacerbators |
| Alpha-1 antitrypsin | Augmentation | For AAT deficiency with emphysema |

### 8.4 Non-Pharmacologic Management [^62^]

1. **Smoking cessation:** Most important intervention; reduces decline in FEV1
2. **Vaccinations:** Influenza annually, pneumococcal (PCV20 or PCV15+PPSV23), COVID-19, RSV (age >=60), Tdap, shingles
3. **Pulmonary rehabilitation:** Recommended for Groups B and E; improves exercise capacity and quality of life
4. **Physical activity/exercise:** Maintain regular activity
5. **Long-term oxygen therapy:** For severe resting chronic hypoxemia (PaO2 <=55 mmHg or SpO2 <=88%); improves survival
6. **Nutritional support:** For underweight patients (BMI <21)

### 8.5 Acute Exacerbation Management [^62^]

**Definition:** Acute respiratory symptom worsening over <14 days

**Treatment:**
1. **Bronchodilators:** Short-acting beta-agonist +/- anticholinergic (albuterol + ipratropium)
2. **Systemic corticosteroids:** Prednisone 40 mg daily for 5 days (not >5 days); IV if unable to take PO
3. **Antibiotics:** If bacterial infection suspected (increased sputum purulence + increased dyspnea OR increased sputum volume)
   - Amoxicillin/clavulanate, doxycycline, azithromycin, or respiratory fluoroquinolone for 5 days
4. **Oxygen:** Target SpO2 88-92% (avoid hyperoxia in chronic CO2 retainers)
5. **Non-invasive ventilation (BiPAP):** First-line for acute hypercapnic respiratory failure; improves gas exchange, reduces intubation need, improves survival
6. **Ventilatory support:** Intubation if NIV fails or contraindicated

**Indications for Hospitalization:**
- Severe symptoms (dyspnea at rest, altered mental status)
- Failure of outpatient therapy
- Significant comorbidities
- Older age
- Unable to cope at home
- Worsening respiratory acidosis

## 9. PNEUMONIA

### 9.1 Clinical Overview

Pneumonia is an infection of the lung parenchyma, classified by setting of acquisition into community-acquired (CAP), hospital-acquired (HAP), and ventilator-associated (VAP). The IDSA/ATS 2019 guidelines provide evidence-based recommendations [^25^].

### 9.2 Classification

**Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):**
- Acquired outside hospital or within 48 hours of admission
- Common pathogens: S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. pneumoniae, C. pneumoniae, Legionella, viruses (influenza, SARS-CoV-2)

**Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia (HAP):**
- Occurs >=48 hours after hospital admission, not incubating at admission
- Common pathogens: S. aureus (including MRSA), P. aeruginosa, Enterobacteriaceae (Klebsiella, E. coli)

**Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP):**
- Pneumonia occurring >48 hours after endotracheal intubation
- Higher mortality than HAP

### 9.3 Severity Assessment [^25^]

#### 9.3.1 CURB-65 Score

| Component | Points |
|-----------|--------|
| Confusion (new) | 1 |
| Urea >7 mmol/L (BUN >19 mg/dL) | 1 |
| Respiratory rate >=30/min | 1 |
| Blood pressure (SBP <90 or DBP <=60 mmHg) | 1 |
| Age >=65 years | 1 |

- **0-1:** Low risk; outpatient treatment
- **2:** Moderate risk; short hospitalization or closely supervised outpatient
- **3-5:** Severe; hospitalization (ICU if 3-5)

#### 9.3.2 PSI (Pneumonia Severity Index) / PORT Score
- More discriminative than CURB-65; identifies larger proportion as low-risk
- PSI Class I-II: Outpatient treatment
- PSI Class III: Brief hospitalization or observation
- PSI Class IV-V: Hospital admission
- Variables: age, nursing home residence, comorbidities, vital signs, physical exam, labs

### 9.4 Treatment of CAP [^25^]

#### 9.4.1 Outpatient Treatment

| Risk Factors | Recommended Regimen |
|-------------|---------------------|
| No comorbidities, no MRSA/Pseudomonas risk | Amoxicillin 1 g TID OR Doxycycline OR Macrolide (if local pneumococcal resistance <25% and no cardiac risks) |
| With comorbidities (CKD, DM, CHF, liver disease, malignancy, alcoholism) | Amoxicillin-clavulanate OR Cephalosporin + macrolide OR Respiratory fluoroquinolone |

#### 9.4.2 Inpatient Treatment (Non-severe)

| Regimen | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Beta-lactam + macrolide | Ceftriaxone 1-2 g daily + azithromycin 500 mg daily |
| Respiratory fluoroquinolone monotherapy | Levofloxacin 750 mg daily or moxifloxacin 400 mg daily |
| Beta-lactam + doxycycline | Alternative if macrolide and fluoroquinolone contraindicated |

Beta-lactam options: Ampicillin-sulbactam, ceftriaxone, cefotaxime, ceftaroline

#### 9.4.3 Inpatient Treatment (Severe)

| Regimen | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Beta-lactam + macrolide | Ceftriaxone 1-2 g daily + azithromycin 500 mg daily |
| Beta-lactam + fluoroquinolone | Ceftriaxone + levofloxacin or moxifloxacin |

**If MRSA risk or P. aeruginosa risk:**
- Add vancomycin (target trough 15-20 mcg/mL) or linezolid for MRSA
- Add antipseudomonal agent (piperacillin-tazobactam, cefepime, meropenem) for Pseudomonas
- Obtain cultures/nasal PCR to allow de-escalation

#### 9.4.4 Duration of Therapy
- Minimum 5 days
- Afebrile for 48-72 hours AND clinically stable before discontinuation
- May extend for complicated infections (lung abscess, empyema)

### 9.5 HAP/VAP Treatment
- Empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics covering MRSA and Pseudomonas if risk factors
- De-escalate based on culture results within 48-72 hours
- 7-day course is standard (shorter courses acceptable if clinically improving)

### 9.6 Prevention
- Pneumococcal vaccination (PCV20 or PCV15 followed by PPSV23)
- Annual influenza vaccination
- Smoking cessation
- Aspiration precautions in at-risk patients

### 9.7 Red Flags (Indicating Severe Pneumonia or ICU Admission)
- Respiratory rate >=30/min
- Multilobar infiltrates
- Confusion/disorientation
- SBP <90 mmHg or requiring vasopressors
- PaO2/FiO2 <250
- Leukopenia (WBC <4,000) or thrombocytopenia
- Hypothermia (<36 C)
- Acute kidney injury

## 10. COVID-19

### 10.1 Clinical Overview

COVID-19 is caused by SARS-CoV-2. Since the initial pandemic, the disease has evolved with new variants, changing epidemiology, and updated treatment approaches. Current guidance focuses on outpatient treatment for high-risk individuals and inpatient management for severe disease [^22^] [^24^].

### 10.2 Current Variants and Epidemiology
- Multiple SARS-CoV-2 variants have emerged since the original strain
- Omicron subvariants remain dominant globally
- Vaccine effectiveness has waned against infection but remains strong against severe disease, hospitalization, and death
- Updated vaccines recommended annually

### 10.3 Diagnosis
- PCR testing (gold standard) or rapid antigen testing
- PCR preferred for high-risk patients needing treatment decisions
- Typical symptoms: Fever, cough, fatigue, myalgias, sore throat, headache, anosmia/ageusia (less common with recent variants), dyspnea
- Severe disease: Hypoxemia (SpO2 <94%), respiratory distress, infiltrates >50% on imaging

### 10.4 Outpatient Treatment [^22^]

**Who to Treat:**
- Adults and adolescents (>=12 years, >=40 kg) at high risk for progression to severe COVID-19
- High-risk factors: Age >=65, BMI >=25, cancer, CKD, diabetes, heart disease, chronic lung disease, immunocompromised, pregnancy
- Must start treatment within 5-7 days of symptom onset

| Treatment | Who | Route | Duration | Timing | Key Issues |
|-----------|-----|-------|----------|--------|------------|
| **Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid)** | Adults; children >=12 and >=40 kg | Oral | 5 days | <=5 days of symptom onset | First-line; major drug-drug interactions; eGFR dosing adjustment |
| **Remdesivir (Veklury)** | Adults; children >=28 days and >=3 kg | IV | 3 days | <=7 days of symptom onset | Second-line; requires infusion center |
| **Molnupiravir** | Adults only | Oral | 5 days | <=5 days of symptom onset | Third-line; avoid in pregnancy; lower efficacy |

#### Paxlovid (Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir) Details [^22^]
- **Dosing:** 300 mg nirmatrelvir (2 x 150 mg) + 100 mg ritonavir (1 x 100 mg) BID for 5 days
- **eGFR 30-59:** 150 mg nirmatrelvir + 100 mg ritonavir BID
- **eGFR <30:** Discuss with specialist
- **Drug interactions:** Numerous significant CYP3A interactions; check all medications using Liverpool drug interaction checker (covid19-druginteractions.org)
- **Contraindications:** Severe hepatic impairment; severe renal impairment without adjustment
- **Paxlovid rebound:** Recurrence of symptoms after completing course; generally mild; consider second course per clinician judgment

**Reduced Efficacy Context:**
- Real-world effectiveness remains significant but reduced compared to pre-Omicron era
- NICE UK has withdrawn recommendation for certain populations where cost-effectiveness is no longer favorable [^24^]
- Still recommended for high-risk immunocompromised patients

### 10.5 Inpatient Treatment

**Severe/Critical COVID-19 (requiring oxygen):**

| Therapy | Indication | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| **Dexamethasone 6 mg IV/PO daily** | All patients requiring supplemental oxygen | RECOVERY trial; reduces mortality |
| **Remdesivir 200 mg IV day 1, then 100 mg daily x 4 days** | Hospitalized patients with SpO2 <94% on room air | ACTT-1 trial; reduces time to recovery |
| **Tocilizumab 8 mg/kg IV (max 800 mg)** | Severe/critical disease with elevated inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-6) | REMAP-CAP/RECOVERY; add to dexamethasone |
| **Baricitinib 4 mg PO daily** | Alternative to tocilizumab; add to remdesivir | ACTT-2 trial |

**Organizing Principles:**
- Empiric antibiotics NOT recommended unless strong suspicion of bacterial superinfection
- NIV/High-flow nasal cannula before intubation if possible
- Prone positioning for awake hypoxemic patients and ventilated patients with ARDS
- VTE prophylaxis for all hospitalized patients

### 10.6 Long COVID / Post-Acute Sequelae of COVID-19 (PASC) [^76^] [^77^] [^79^]

**Definition:** Persistent symptoms beyond 12 weeks after acute infection that cannot be explained by alternative diagnosis

**Common Symptoms:**
- Fatigue (most common; 35-77%)
- Dyspnea (43% at 60 days)
- Cognitive impairment/brain fog (28%)
- Anosmia/ageusia (up to 46% at 1 month)
- Headache (40%)
- Chest pain
- Anxiety/depression (25-30%)
- Post-exertional symptom exacerbation (PESE)
- POTS (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome)
- Sleep disorders
- Muscle/joint pain

**Management (Symptom-Directed) [^77^] [^79^]:**

| Symptom | Management |
|---------|-----------|
| Fatigue | Energy conservation, pacing, graded exercise (if no PESE), vitamins B12/C/D, CoQ10 |
| Dyspnea | Breathing exercises, pulmonary rehabilitation, diaphragmatic breathing |
| Brain fog | Cognitive exercises, address sleep/pain/mood, luteolin (investigational) |
| Anosmia | Olfactory training (4 scents: rose, eucalyptus, clove, lemon x 20-30 sec each, twice daily for months) |
| POTS | Hydration (2-3L/day), salt intake, compression stockings, beta-blockers or ivabradine |
| Cough | Airway clearance techniques, breathing exercises, cough suppressants |
| Pain | NSAIDs, acetaminophen, physical therapy |
| Anxiety/Depression | SSRIs, psychological support, mindfulness, CBT |

**No approved disease-modifying therapy for long COVID exists.** Management is multidisciplinary and symptom-based [^77^].

### 10.7 Prevention
- Updated COVID-19 vaccines recommended annually
- High-risk patients should maintain up-to-date vaccination
- Standard infection control measures

## 11. PULMONARY EMBOLISM

### 11.1 Clinical Overview

Pulmonary embolism (PE) is a potentially life-threatening condition caused by thrombus migration to the pulmonary arteries, most commonly from deep vein thrombosis (DVT) in the lower extremities. Prompt diagnosis and risk stratification are critical.

### 11.2 Clinical Presentation
- Dyspnea (most common; 89%)
- Pleuritic chest pain (40%)
- Tachycardia
- Hypoxemia
- Syncope (indicates massive PE)
- Hemoptysis (suggests pulmonary infarction)
- Signs of DVT (11%) [^30^]

### 11.3 Diagnostic Algorithm

#### 11.3.1 Wells Score for PE

| Variable | Points |
|----------|--------|
| Clinical signs/symptoms of DVT (minimum leg swelling/pain with palpation) | 3 |
| PE is #1 diagnosis OR equally likely | 3 |
| Heart rate >100 bpm | 1.5 |
| Immobilization >=3 days OR surgery in previous 4 weeks | 1.5 |
| Previous DVT/PE | 1.5 |
| Hemoptysis | 1 |
| Malignancy (on treatment, treated within 6 months, or palliative) | 1 |

**Interpretation:**
- **<=4 (PE unlikely):** Check D-dimer
- **>4 (PE likely):** Proceed directly to CTPA

#### 11.3.2 PERC Rule (Pulmonary Embolism Rule-out Criteria)

For patients with low pre-test probability (Wells <=4), if ALL 8 criteria are met, PE can be ruled out without D-dimer:

1. Age <50 years
2. Heart rate <100 bpm
3. O2 saturation >=95%
4. No unilateral leg swelling
5. No hemoptysis
6. No recent surgery or trauma (within 4 weeks)
7. No prior DVT or PE
8. No hormone use (oral contraceptives, estrogen)

- PERC-negative patients have <2% risk of PE
- Can reduce CTPA use by 7-18% [^30^]

#### 11.3.3 D-Dimer Interpretation [^30^]

| Clinical Scenario | Recommended Threshold |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Standard | <500 ng/mL FEU |
| Age-adjusted (age >50) | Age x 10 ng/mL FEU |
| High clinical probability | D-dimer not recommended (proceed to CTPA) |

- Age-adjusted D-dimer (age x 10) can reduce CTPA use by 11-20% in patients >50 years [^30^]
- D-dimer is NOT useful in hospitalized patients, post-surgical patients, or pregnant patients (high false-positive rate)

#### 11.3.4 Imaging
- **CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA):** Gold standard for diagnosis
- **V/Q scan:** If CTPA contraindicated (pregnancy, renal failure, contrast allergy)
- **Echocardiography:** Assess RV strain (indicates severity and prognosis)
- **Lower extremity ultrasound:** Evaluate for DVT

### 11.4 Risk Stratification

| Category | Features | Management |
|----------|----------|------------|
| **Massive PE** | Sustained hypotension (SBP <90 for >15 min), pulselessness, or shock | Systemic thrombolysis or embolectomy |
| **Submassive (intermediate-high)** | RV strain on imaging OR elevated troponin/BNP, normotensive | Anticoagulation; consider thrombolysis if deterioration |
| **Submassive (intermediate-low)** | Positive RV on imaging but normal biomarkers, normotensive | Anticoagulation |
| **Low risk** | No RV strain, normal biomarkers, normotensive | Anticoagulation only; may consider outpatient |

### 11.5 Treatment

#### 11.5.1 Initial Anticoagulation (All Confirmed PE)

| Agent | Dosing | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| **Apixaban** | 10 mg BID x 7 days, then 5 mg BID | Preferred DOAC |
| **Rivaroxaban** | 15 mg BID x 21 days, then 20 mg daily | Take with food |
| **Edoxaban** | 60 mg daily (after 5-10 days of parenteral) | Reduce to 30 mg if CrCl 15-50 |
| **Dabigatran** | 150 mg BID (after 5-10 days of parenteral) | Less preferred |
| **Enoxaparin** | 1 mg/kg SC q12h or 1.5 mg/kg daily | Bridging or if DOAC contraindicated |
| **Heparin IV** | Weight-based protocol | If thrombolysis or intervention anticipated |

**Duration of Anticoagulation:**
- Provoked/transient risk factor: 3 months
- Unprovoked: Minimum 3 months; consider extended if low bleeding risk
- Recurrent VTE or active cancer: Indefinite (while risk persists)
- Thrombophilia: Indefinite in most cases

#### 11.5.2 Thrombolysis
- Indicated for massive PE (hemodynamic compromise)
- Consider for submassive PE with clinical deterioration
- Agents: Alteplase 100 mg IV over 2 hours; tenecteplase (weight-based)
- Contraindications: Active bleeding, recent surgery/stroke, intracranial tumor/hemorrhage

#### 11.5.3 Embolectomy/Intervention
- Catheter-directed thrombolysis or suction embolectomy
- Surgical embolectomy for massive PE with contraindication to thrombolysis
- Inferior vena cava filter if anticoagulation absolutely contraindicated

### 11.6 Special Populations
- **Pregnancy:** LMWH is treatment of choice; DOACs contraindicated; avoid warfarin
- **Cancer:** LMWH preferred for first 6 months; edoxaban/apixaban acceptable alternatives
- **Renal impairment:** Adjust DOAC dosing; LMWH may need anti-Xa monitoring
- **Elderly:** Increased bleeding risk with all agents; careful monitoring

### 11.7 Red Flags
- Syncope with PE (indicates hemodynamically significant clot burden)
- Hypotension (SBP <90)
- RV strain on echocardiogram or CTPA
- Elevated troponin or BNP
- Active bleeding while on anticoagulation

## 12. DYSPNEA ASSESSMENT

### 12.1 Clinical Overview

Dyspnea is a subjective sensation of uncomfortable or difficult breathing. It is one of the most common presenting complaints in emergency medicine, with diverse etiologies spanning cardiac, pulmonary, hematologic, metabolic, and psychiatric causes. Rapid differentiation between cardiac and pulmonary causes is essential for appropriate management [^80^] [^81^] [^82^].

### 12.2 Approach to Acute Dyspnea

**Initial Assessment (First 60 Seconds):**
1. Airway patency
2. Breathing pattern (rate, effort, accessory muscles)
3. Circulation (BP, HR, peripheral perfusion)
4. Oxygen saturation
5. Mental status

**Key Differentiating Features:**

| Feature | Cardiac Dyspnea | Pulmonary Dyspnea |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| Onset | Gradual (CHF) or sudden (flash pulmonary edema) | Variable |
| Orthopnea | Common | Less common (except severe COPD/asthma) |
| Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea | Highly specific for CHF | Uncommon |
| JVD | Present in right heart failure | Absent (unless cor pulmonale) |
| Edema | Bilateral, dependent | Absent |
| Wheezing | "Cardiac asthma" (pink frothy sputum) | True wheezing (asthma/COPD) |
| Crackles | Bilateral basal, fine | Focal, coarse (pneumonia) or diffuse (ARDS) |
| S3 gallop | Present in decompensated HFrEF | Absent |

### 12.3 Diagnostic Workup

**Bedside:**
- ECG: Ischemia, arrhythmia, PE (S1Q3T3), LVH
- Chest X-ray: Pulmonary edema, pneumonia, pneumothorax, pleural effusion
- Arterial blood gas (if severe)

**Biomarkers:**

| Test | Cardiac Indication | Pulmonary Indication |
|------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **BNP/NT-proBNP** | Elevated in HF; strongly supports cardiac cause [^80^] [^84^] | May be elevated with PE (RV strain), but generally lower |
| **Troponin** | ACS, demand ischemia | PE (RV strain), severe sepsis |
| **D-dimer** | May be elevated in acute HF | PE evaluation |
| **CRP/Procalcitonin** | Not specific | Bacterial pneumonia |

**BNP/NT-proBNP Interpretation for Dyspnea Differentiation [^80^] [^82^]:**
- NT-proBNP >600 ng/L: 92% sensitivity, 82% specificity for cardiac dyspnea
- NT-proBNP cutoff 800 pg/mL: 82% sensitivity, 86% specificity [^81^]
- NT-proBNP correlates with NYHA class and inversely with LVEF [^80^]
- Median NT-proBNP in acute CHF: 4,054 pg/mL vs. 131 pg/mL in non-cardiac dyspnea [^84^]
- Age-adjusted cutoffs improve diagnostic accuracy in elderly

| NYHA Class | Median NT-proBNP | Mean LVEF |
|------------|-----------------|-----------|
| II | 531 ng/L | 58.5% |
| III | 2,675 ng/L | 47.1% |
| IV | 5,724 ng/L | 40.3% |

**Additional Testing:**
- Echocardiography: LV function, valvular disease, RV strain, pericardial effusion
- CTPA: If PE suspected
- CT chest: Parenchymal lung disease, aortic pathology
- PFTs: Obstructive vs. restrictive pattern (for chronic dyspnea)
- Cardiac stress testing: Ischemia evaluation

### 12.4 Common Causes of Dyspnea

#### 12.4.1 Cardiac Causes
1. Acute decompensated heart failure (most common cardiac cause)
2. Acute coronary syndrome
3. Arrhythmias (AF with rapid ventricular response, VT, bradyarrhythmias)
4. Acute valvular emergency (papillary muscle rupture, chordal rupture)
5. Pericardial tamponade
6. Hypertensive emergency

#### 12.4.2 Pulmonary Causes
1. COPD exacerbation
2. Asthma exacerbation
3. Pneumonia
4. Pulmonary embolism
5. Pneumothorax
6. ARDS
7. Interstitial lung disease
8. Pleural effusion
9. Upper airway obstruction

#### 12.4.3 Other Causes
1. Anemia (Hb <7 g/dL often symptomatic)
2. Metabolic acidosis (Kussmaul breathing)
3. Anxiety/panic attack (diagnosis of exclusion)
4. Deconditioning
5. Obesity/hypoventilation syndrome
6. Neuromuscular disease
7. Carbon monoxide poisoning
8. Methemoglobinemia

### 12.5 Management Principles

**Cardiac Dyspnea:**
- ADHF: IV diuretics, vasodilators, oxygen, NIV if needed
- ACS: Aspirin, anticoagulation, revascularization
- Arrhythmia: Rate/rhythm control
- Tamponade: Urgent pericardiocentesis

**Pulmonary Dyspnea:**
- COPD/asthma: Bronchodilators, corticosteroids, oxygen
- Pneumonia: Appropriate antibiotics
- PE: Anticoagulation; thrombolysis if massive
- Pneumothorax: Needle decompression (tension) or chest tube

### 12.6 Red Flags
- Severe hypoxemia (SpO2 <85% on room air)
- Altered mental status
- Hemodynamic instability (SBP <90 mmHg)
- Respiratory acidosis with pH <7.25
- Severe respiratory distress (tripoding, unable to speak)
- Suspected tension pneumothorax (immediate needle decompression)
