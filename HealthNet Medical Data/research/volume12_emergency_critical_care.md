## 1. ACLS (Advanced Cardiac Life Support) Protocols {#1-acls}

### 1.1 Clinical Overview

Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS) encompasses the systematic approach to adult cardiac arrest, peri-arrest arrhythmias, and post-cardiac arrest care. The AHA 2020 and 2025 Guidelines provide the most current evidence-based recommendations [^567^][^568^][^569^].

### 1.2 Cardiac Arrest Algorithms

#### Universal Adult Cardiac Arrest Algorithm

**BLS First Approach:**
- Verify scene safety
- Check responsiveness (no breathing or only gasping, no definite pulse within 10 seconds)
- Activate emergency response and get AED
- Begin CPR: Push hard (at least 2 inches/5 cm), push fast (100-120/min), allow complete chest recoil
- Minimize interruptions (goal CCF >80%)
- Change compressor every 2 minutes
- 30:2 compression-ventilation ratio without advanced airway
- Once advanced airway placed: 1 breath every 6 seconds (10 breaths/min) with continuous compressions [^567^][^568^]

**Shockable Rhythms (VF/pVT):**
1. Start CPR, attach monitor/defibrillator
2. Shock as soon as defibrillator available (biphasic 120-200J; monophasic 360J)
3. CPR for 2 minutes immediately after shock
4. IV/IO access, epinephrine 1mg every 3-5 minutes
5. Consider advanced airway + capnography
6. After second shock: Amiodarone 300mg IV/IO bolus; may repeat 150mg once
7. Lidocaine alternative: 1-1.5 mg/kg first dose; 0.5-0.75 mg/kg second dose
8. Continue 2-minute cycles, treat reversible causes

**Non-Shockable Rhythms (Asystole/PEA):**
1. Start CPR, give oxygen, attach monitor
2. Verify in 2 leads, check for loose leads
3. CPR for 2 minutes
4. IV/IO access, **give epinephrine as soon as feasible** (1mg every 3-5 min)
5. Consider advanced airway
6. Treat reversible causes (H's and T's)
7. Continue 2-minute cycles [^568^][^571^]

**2025 AHA Key Updates:**
- IV access preferred over IO; IO reasonable if IV cannot be obtained quickly [^570^]
- Early epinephrine emphasized for non-shockable rhythms
- Routine sodium bicarbonate, calcium, magnesium NOT recommended unless specific indication (hyperkalemia, hypomagnesemia, toxicologic arrest)
- Mechanical CPR: routine use not recommended; may be considered when high-quality manual CPR not possible
- No routine steroids in cardiac arrest [^570^]

### 1.3 High-Quality CPR Standards

| Parameter | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Rate | 100-120 compressions/min |
| Depth | At least 2 inches (5 cm); avoid >2.4 inches (6 cm) |
| Recoil | Allow complete chest recoil; do not lean on chest |
| Interruptions | Minimize to <10 seconds |
| Compression fraction | Goal >80% |
| Ventilation (no advanced airway) | 30:2 ratio |
| Ventilation (advanced airway) | 1 breath every 6 seconds; do not pause compressions |
| Switch compressors | Every 2 minutes or sooner if fatigued |
| Quantitative waveform capnography | If ETCO2 <10mmHg or decreasing, reassess CPR quality |

[^567^][^568^]

### 1.4 Defibrillation

- **Biphasic:** Manufacturer recommendation (initial 120-200J); if unknown, use maximum available. Second/subsequent doses equivalent; higher doses may be considered.
- **Monophasic:** 360J for all shocks
- **Pediatric (8 years or <8):** 2-4 J/kg initial; 4 J/kg subsequent; higher doses (up to 10 J/kg) may be considered [^567^]

### 1.5 Medications in Cardiac Arrest

| Drug | Dose | Timing | Notes |
|------|------|--------|-------|
| Epinephrine | 1mg IV/IO | Every 3-5 minutes | Vasopressor; give ASAP for non-shockable rhythms |
| Amiodarone | 300mg bolus, then 150mg | After 3rd shock/2nd cycle | For refractory VF/pVT |
| Lidocaine | 1-1.5 mg/kg, then 0.5-0.75 mg/kg | After 3rd shock | Alternative to amiodarone |
| Atropine | NOT in cardiac arrest | N/A | Removed from cardiac arrest algorithm |

[^567^][^568^][^570^]

### 1.6 Post-Cardiac Arrest Care

**Immediate Priorities After ROSC:**
1. Obtain 12-lead ECG
2. Manage airway (early ETT placement + waveform capnography)
3. **Respiratory targets:** SpO2 92-98%, PaCO2 35-45 mmHg
4. **Hemodynamic targets:** SBP >90 mmHg or MAP >65 mmHg
5. Temperature control (formerly TTM) if comatose (does not follow commands) [^566^][^569^]

**Temperature Control:**
- Target: 32-37.5 degrees C for at least 36 hours
- Common methods: endovascular catheter, surface cooling devices
- Monitor core temperature (esophageal, bladder, rectal)
- TTM2 Trial showed no benefit of 33C vs normothermia at 36-37.5C with fever prevention
- Fever should be avoided; normothermia is acceptable when TTM not part of protocol
- Controlled rewarming: 0.25-0.5 degrees C/hr
- Prevent and treat shivering [^569^][^573^]

**H's and T's (Reversible Causes):**
- Hypovolemia, Hypoxia, Hydrogen ion (acidosis), Hypo-/hyperkalemia, Hypothermia
- Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade (cardiac), Toxins, Thrombosis (pulmonary/coronary) [^567^]

### 1.7 Bradycardia Algorithm

**Steps:**
1. Maintain patent airway; assist breathing if needed
2. IV access, monitor, 12-lead ECG
3. **Atropine:** 1mg IV; may repeat every 3-5 minutes (max 3mg) [^571^]
4. If atropine ineffective: Begin transcutaneous pacing
5. If pacing delayed or ineffective: Consider dopamine 5-20 mcg/kg/min infusion OR epinephrine 2-10 mcg/min infusion
6. Consider expert consultation
7. Prepare for transvenous pacing if needed

**Indications for Intervention:**
- Signs of poor perfusion: hypotension, altered mental status, signs of shock, chest pain, acute heart failure
- Heart rate typically <50/min with symptoms
- High-grade AV block (2nd degree type II, 3rd degree) [^571^][^572^]

### 1.8 Tachycardia Algorithm (With Pulse)

**Step 1: Assess Stability**
- **Unstable** (hypotension, altered mental status, signs of shock, chest pain, acute HF): Immediate synchronized cardioversion
- **Stable:** Proceed to Step 2

**Step 2: Identify Rhythm**
- **Narrow QRS (<0.12s) Regular:** Vagal maneuvers; Adenosine 6mg rapid IVP with flush; may repeat 12mg x2; rate control with diltiazem
- **Narrow QRS Irregular:** Rate control (diltiazem, beta-blockers); anticoagulation if AF
- **Wide QRS Regular:** Consider adenosine 6mg; Amiodarone 150mg/100mL over 10 min; Procainamide; expert consultation
- **Wide QRS Irregular:** Defibrillation dose (NOT synchronized) [^571^][^572^]

**Synchronized Cardioversion Energy:**
- Follow device-specific recommendations to maximize first shock success
- Typical: 75J, 120J, 150J (escalating) [^571^]

**Adenosine Administration:**
- 6mg rapid IVP followed immediately by rapid saline flush
- May repeat with 12mg; then 12mg again if needed
- Half-life <10 seconds; warn patient about transient flushing/chest discomfort

### 1.9 Special Considerations

**Cardiac Arrest in Pregnancy:**
- Perform manual left uterine displacement
- IV above diaphragm
- If receiving magnesium, stop and give calcium
- If no ROSC in 5 minutes, consider perimortem cesarean delivery [^571^]

**2025 Changes Summary:**
- 6th "Recovery" link added to Chain of Survival
- IV preferred over IO; early epinephrine for non-shockable rhythms
- Post-cardiac arrest SpO2 target changed to 92-98%
- Atropine dose changed from 0.5mg to 1mg in bradycardia
- Dopamine dosing changed to 5-20 mcg/kg/min [^571^]

### 1.10 Red Flags
- Pulseless patient = cardiac arrest
- Shockable rhythm = defibrillate immediately
- ROSC with STEMI = emergent PCI
- Cardiac arrest in pregnancy >20 weeks = manual left uterine displacement; perimortem C-section at 5 min if no ROSC

## 2. Acute Coronary Syndrome {#2-acs}

### 2.1 Clinical Overview

Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) encompasses STEMI, NSTEMI, and unstable angina. Management focuses on rapid risk stratification, antithrombotic therapy, and timely reperfusion for STEMI [^572^][^571^].

### 2.2 STEMI (ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction)

**Definition:** ST-elevation in contiguous leads or new LBBB with ischemic symptoms.

**Time Goals:**
- Door-to-balloon (PCI): <90 minutes
- Door-to-needle (fibrinolysis): <30 minutes
- FMC-to-device time: <120 minutes
- Best practice: Bypass ED straight to cath lab if team available [^571^][^572^]

**Reperfusion Strategy:**
- **Primary PCI preferred** when available within 120 minutes
- **Fibrinolysis** if PCI not available within 120 minutes
- Prehospital fibrinolysis may be considered if transport delays expected

**Fibrinolytic Agents:**
- **Tenecteplase (TNK-tPA):** Single weight-based bolus; preferred for ease of use
  - <60kg: 30mg; 60-69kg: 35mg; 70-79kg: 40mg; 80-89kg: 45mg; >=90kg: 50mg
  - IV bolus over 5 seconds
- **Contraindications:**
  - Absolute: Prior hemorrhagic stroke, ischemic stroke <3 months, known intracranial vascular lesion/malignancy, active bleeding, suspected aortic dissection, intracranial/intraspinal surgery <2 months, severe closed head/facial trauma <3 months
  - Relative: Uncontrolled BP (>180/110), current anticoagulation, pregnancy, >10 minutes of CPR, recent internal bleeding <2-4 weeks, non-compressible vascular punctures [^571^]

**Antithrombotic Therapy (STEMI):**
- Aspirin 325mg loading (if not already given), then 81mg daily
- P2Y12 inhibitor (clopidogrel 600mg, ticagrelor 180mg, or prasugrel 60mg)
- Anticoagulation: Heparin (unfractionated or enoxaparin) during PCI
- GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors (eptifibatide, tirofiban) in selected cases
- Continue dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) for at least 12 months [^572^]

### 2.3 NSTEMI / Unstable Angina

**Risk Stratification:**
- TIMI score or GRACE score for prognosis
- High-risk features: Elevated troponin, ST-changes, hemodynamic instability, recurrent ischemia

**Management:**
- Aspirin (all patients unless contraindicated)
- P2Y12 inhibitor (ticagrelor or clopidogrel)
- Anticoagulation: Enoxaparin, UFH, fondaparinux, or bivalirudin
- Early invasive strategy for high-risk patients
- Conservative strategy for low-risk patients after risk stratification
- High-intensity statin
- Beta-blockers within 24 hours if no contraindications
- ACE inhibitors/ARBs, especially with anterior MI, LVEF <40%, or diabetes

### 2.4 Dual Antiplatelet Therapy

| Agent | Loading Dose | Maintenance | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Aspirin | 325mg | 81mg daily | All ACS patients |
| Clopidogrel | 600mg | 75mg daily | If ticagrelor/prasugrel not available |
| Ticagrelor | 180mg | 90mg BID | Preferred; reversible; dyspnea side effect |
| Prasugrel | 60mg | 10mg daily | Avoid if prior stroke/TIA; age >75 or <60kg |

### 2.5 Anticoagulation

| Agent | STEMI (Fibrinolysis) | STEMI (PCI) | NSTEMI |
|-------|---------------------|-------------|--------|
| UFH | 60 U/kg bolus (max 4000U) + 12 U/kg/hr | Per ACT | Per protocol |
| Enoxaparin | 30mg IV + 1mg/kg SC q12h | 0.5-0.75mg/kg IV | 1mg/kg SC q12h |
| Bivalirudin | Alternative | During PCI | During PCI |

### 2.6 Red Flags
- Cardiogenic shock (SBP <90, cool extremities, altered mental status)
- Mechanical complications (papillary muscle rupture, VSD, free wall rupture)
- Malignant arrhythmias (VT, VF)
- Recurrent ischemia despite therapy
- Acute heart failure/Killip class >II

## 3. Stroke Emergency {#3-stroke}

### 3.1 Clinical Overview

Stroke is the 5th leading cause of death and leading cause of disability. Management focuses on rapid recognition, imaging-based triage, and timely reperfusion therapy [^190^][^191^].

### 3.2 Ischemic Stroke

**Time-Critical Management:**
- "Time is brain": 1.9 million neurons lost per minute
- Target door-to-needle <60 minutes, door-to-imaging <25 minutes
- Bypass ED straight to brain imaging when possible [^571^]

**IV Thrombolysis:**
- **Tenecteplase:** 0.25 mg/kg IV bolus (max 25mg) - Class 1 recommendation; preferred over alteplase
  - Advantages: Single bolus, no infusion, easier transfer
- **Alteplase (tPA):** 0.9 mg/kg IV (max 90mg); 10% bolus, remainder over 60 min
- **Window:** Within 4.5 hours of last known well (Class 1)
- **Extended window (4.5-24h):** May be reasonable with advanced imaging showing salvageable tissue (Class 2a/2b); useful for wake-up stroke with perfusion mismatch [^190^]

**BP Management Before Thrombolysis:**
- Must be <185/110 mmHg before lysis
- Maintain <180/105 mmHg for 24 hours after
- Labetalol 10-20mg IV, nicardipine infusion, or clevidipine
- Class III recommendation against lowering SBP <140 mmHg post-tPA in mild-moderate stroke [^190^]

**NIHSS (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale):**
- Used for severity assessment and treatment decisions
- 0 = no stroke symptoms; 1-4 = minor; 5-15 = moderate; 16-20 = moderate-severe; 21-42 = severe

### 3.3 Mechanical Thrombectomy

**Indications (Class 1 Recommendations):**
- Anterior circulation LVO (ICA, M1) within 6 hours: Definitely recommended
- **6-24 hours:** Selected patients based on DAWN/DEFUSE-3 criteria
  - Age <80, NIHSS >=10, infarct core <70mL (RAPID software) with clinical-imaging mismatch
- **Large core infarcts (6-24h):** Class 1 for ASPECTS 3-5, NIHSS >=6, age <80 (SELECT2, ANGEL-ASPECT trials) [^190^]
- **Posterior circulation (basilar artery):** Class 1 within 24h for NIHSS >=10 with mild ischemic damage (ATTENTION, BAOCHE trials) [^190^]

**Criteria:**
- Pre-stroke mRS 0-1
- Causative occlusion of ICA or MCA M1
- Age >18
- NIHSS >=6
- ASPECTS >=6 (for early window)

### 3.4 Hemorrhagic Stroke

**Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH):**
- BP management: SBP target 140-160 mmHg (INTERACT-2, ATACH-2)
- Reverse anticoagulation immediately
- Vitamin K 10mg IV + PCC or FFP for warfarin
- Idarucizumab for dabigatran; andexanet alfa for factor Xa inhibitors
- 4-factor PCC for DOAC reversal if specific antidote unavailable
- Manage ICP: head of bed 30 degrees, osmotherapy (mannitol 0.25-1g/kg or hypertonic saline)
- Neurosurgical consultation for hematoma evacuation if indicated

**Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH):**
- CT head (sensitivity >95% within 6 hours); LP if CT negative but high suspicion
- CTA to identify aneurysm
- Secure aneurysm (coiling vs clipping)
- Nimodipine 60mg PO/NG q4h for 21 days (prevents vasospasm)
- Monitor for vasospasm, hyponatremia, hydrocephalus

### 3.5 Post-tPA Monitoring

- Frequent neuro checks and BP monitoring
- No antiplatelet or anticoagulation for 24 hours
- Repeat CT at 24 hours before starting antithrombotics
- Watch for:
  - Symptomatic ICH (sudden neuro decline, HA, N/V, increased BP)
  - Orolingual angioedema (incidence ~1.3-5.1%; more common with ACE inhibitor use)
  - Extracranial bleeding

### 3.6 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing Features |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Hypoglycemia | Check glucose immediately |
| Seizure with Todd's paralysis | History of seizure, encephalopathy |
| Migraine with aura | Gradual onset, prior history |
| Bell's palsy | Facial weakness only, no extremity involvement |
| Conversion disorder | Inconsistent exam, non-anatomic findings |
| Brain tumor | Subacute onset, progressive symptoms |
| Wernicke encephalopathy | Ataxia, ophthalmoplegia, confusion |

### 3.7 Red Flags
- Time-sensitive: Every minute counts
- Rapidly deteriorating neuro exam
- Signs of herniation (unequal pupils, posturing, Cushing's triad)
- ICH after thrombolysis
- Mass effect from large territory stroke

## 4. Sepsis and Septic Shock {#4-sepsis}

### 4.1 Clinical Overview

Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysregulated host response to infection. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 provides the most current evidence-based management recommendations [^5^][^579^].

### 4.2 Sepsis-3 Definitions

**Sepsis:** Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysregulated host response to infection; identified by acute change in total SOFA score >=2 points [^579^]

**qSOFA (Quick SOFA) - Screening Tool:**
- Respiratory rate >=22/min
- Altered mentation (GCS <15)
- SBP <=100 mmHg
- >=2 criteria suggests high risk of poor outcome

**Septic Shock:** Sepsis + persistent hypotension (MAP <65 mmHg) requiring vasopressors + serum lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation [^579^]

### 4.3 Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021: Hour-1 Bundle

Initiate bundle upon recognition of sepsis/septic shock. All elements should begin within 1 hour [^579^]:

1. **Measure lactate level** (remeasure if initial >=2 mmol/L)
2. **Obtain blood cultures** before antibiotics (do not delay antibiotics >45 min)
3. **Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics** within 1 hour
4. **Rapid administration of 30 mL/kg crystalloid** for hypotension or lactate >=4 mmol/L
5. **Apply vasopressors** if hypotensive during/after fluid resuscitation (maintain MAP >=65)

### 4.4 Fluid Resuscitation

- **Crystalloids preferred** over colloids [^5^]
- **Balanced crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's, Plasma-Lyte) preferred** over normal saline (2021 recommendation) [^579^]
- Initial: 30 mL/kg within first 3 hours (weak recommendation, low quality evidence)
- **Dynamic assessments** to guide further fluids:
  - Passive leg raise (PLR) test
  - Pulse pressure variation (PPV)
  - Stroke volume variation
  - Capillary refill time
- Avoid excessive fluid administration; potential U-shaped mortality curve [^576^]

### 4.5 Vasopressors

| Agent | Dosing | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| **Norepinephrine** | Start 0.01-3 mcg/kg/min | **First-line**; preferred over dopamine |
| Vasopressin | 0.03 units/min | Add if NE inadequate; may allow NE dose reduction |
| Epinephrine | 0.1-0.5 mcg/kg/min | Alternative/addition to NE |
| Dopamine | 5-20 mcg/kg/min | NOT recommended; use only if NE unavailable |
| Phenylephrine | 40-180 mcg/min | NOT recommended (reduces stroke volume) |

- Central access preferred but may start peripherally if needed (short period)
- Target MAP >=65 mmHg (individualize for chronic hypertension patients)
- Consider arterial line for BP monitoring [^5^][^579^]

### 4.6 Antibiotic Therapy

**Principles:**
- Broad-spectrum coverage based on suspected source
- Within 1 hour of recognition
- Combination therapy for septic shock (beta-lactam + aminoglycoside or fluoroquinolone)
- Source control as soon as feasible (drain abscesses, remove infected devices, debride necrotic tissue)
- Daily reassessment for de-escalation based on cultures
- Duration: typically 7-10 days (shorter for uncomplicated infections; longer for immunocompromised or inadequate source control)

**Common Empiric Regimens:**
| Suspected Source | Empiric Antibiotics |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Community-acquired pneumonia | Ceftriaxone + azithromycin or respiratory fluoroquinolone |
| Intra-abdominal | Piperacillin-tazobactam or cefepime + metronidazole |
| Urinary (healthcare-associated) | Piperacillin-tazobactam or ceftolozane-tazobactam |
| Skin/soft tissue (necrotizing fasciitis) | Vancomycin + piperacillin-tazobactam + clindamycin |
| Unknown source (septic shock) | Vancomycin + piperacillin-tazobactam |

### 4.7 Lactate Clearance and Perfusion Targets

- Initial lactate measurement required
- Remeasure within 2-4 hours if elevated
- Target clearance >10% from baseline
- Capillary refill time <3 seconds may be alternative to lactate
- Consider central venous O2 saturation (ScvO2) monitoring
- Parenteral fluid bolus with or without inotropes/vasopressors to maintain BP

### 4.8 Corticosteroids

- **Hydrocortisone 200mg/day** for adults with septic shock and ongoing vasopressor requirement (norepinephrine >=0.25 mcg/kg/min for at least 4 hours)
- May be given as 50mg IV q6h or continuous infusion
- Weak recommendation; moderate quality evidence [^5^][^579^]

### 4.9 ARDS Management

See Section 9.10 for full ARDS management details.

**Key Points in Sepsis-Related ARDS:**
- Lung-protective ventilation: tidal volume 6 mL/kg ideal body weight
- Plateau pressure <30 cmH2O
- Prone positioning for moderate-severe ARDS (PaO2/FiO2 <150)
- Consider ECMO for refractory hypoxemia in experienced centers [^5^]

### 4.10 Red Flags
- MAP <65 despite fluids and vasopressors
- Lactate >4 mmol/L = severe shock
- Acute respiratory failure requiring intubation
- Acute kidney injury (oliguria, rising creatinine)
- Altered mental status with sepsis
- Source not controlled (necrotizing fasciitis, undrained abscess)

## 5. Anaphylaxis {#5-anaphylaxis}

### 5.1 Clinical Overview

Anaphylaxis is a serious, rapid-onset, potentially fatal systemic allergic reaction. It requires immediate epinephrine administration and careful monitoring for biphasic reactions [^605^].

### 5.2 Definition and Recognition

**Anaphylaxis is likely when ANY ONE of the following criteria is met:**
1. Acute onset with skin/mucosal involvement AND respiratory compromise OR hypotension
2. Two or more systems after likely allergen exposure: skin, respiratory, GI, cardiovascular
3. Hypotension after known allergen: SBP <90 mmHg or >30% decrease from baseline

### 5.3 Epinephrine: First-Line Treatment

**Intramuscular (IM) Epinephrine:**
- **Dose:** 0.3-0.5 mg of 1:1000 (1 mg/mL) concentration
- **Route:** IM into lateral thigh (vastus lateralis)
- **Repeat:** Every 5-15 minutes as needed
- **Autoinjectors:** 0.3mg (EpiPen), 0.15mg (EpiPen Jr), 0.1mg (Auvi-Q)
- **Pediatrics:** 0.01 mg/kg (max 0.5mg) IM

**Intravenous Epinephrine:**
- For refractory anaphylaxis or patients in shock
- Start 0.1-1 mcg/min; titrate to effect
- Requires continuous monitoring

### 5.4 Adjunctive Therapy

| Medication | Role | Notes |
|------------|------|-------|
| H1 antihistamine (diphenhydramine) | Relieves urticaria, pruritus | NOT life-saving; adjunct only |
| H2 antihistamine (ranitidine/famotidine) | Adjunct for skin symptoms | Evidence limited |
| Corticosteroids (methylprednisolone) | May prevent biphasic reaction | Slow onset; not for acute rescue |
| Bronchodilators (albuterol) | Bronchospasm refractory to epinephrine | Adjunct only |
| Glucagon | For patients on beta-blockers | 1-5mg IV; may precipitate hyperglycemia |
| IV Fluids | Volume resuscitation | 1-2L normal saline rapidly if hypotensive |

### 5.5 Biphasic Reactions

- Occur in up to 20% of patients (studies vary 3-20%)
- Typically within 4-10 hours; can occur up to 33 hours later
- Risk factors: Severe initial reaction, wide pulse pressure, repeated epinephrine doses
- **Observation period: Minimum 4-6 hours** for mild-moderate reactions; **12-24 hours** for severe reactions [^605^]
- Some evidence supports transitioning to personalized approach for low-risk patients

### 5.6 Discharge Planning
- Epinephrine auto-injector prescription (2 devices)
- Allergy referral for testing and immunotherapy
- Written anaphylaxis action plan
- Education on avoidance strategies
- Medical alert identification

### 5.7 Red Flags
- Respiratory compromise (stridor, wheezing, hypoxemia)
- Hypotension or syncope
- Rapid progression of symptoms
- Prior severe anaphylaxis
- Asthma (increases risk of fatal anaphylaxis)
- Delayed epinephrine administration

### 5.8 Sources
- WAO/AAAAI Anaphylaxis Guidelines
- Evaluating Practice Patterns of Observation Periods Following Epinephrine for Anaphylaxis [^605^]

## 6. Toxicology and Overdose Management {#6-toxicology}

### 6.1 Approach to the Poisoned Patient

**ABCs First:**
1. Airway protection (intubate if needed)
2. Breathing and ventilation
3. Circulation and hemodynamic support
4. Dextrose (rule out hypoglycemia), thiamine, naloxone
5. Decontamination (when appropriate)

**Toxidrome Recognition:**

| Toxidrome | Key Features | Common Agents |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Sympathomimetic | Tachycardia, HTN, hyperthermia, agitation, mydriasis | Cocaine, amphetamines, bath salts |
| Anticholinergic | Hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter | Diphenhydramine, TCA, atropine |
| Cholinergic | SLUDGE/BLUDGE | Organophosphates, carbamates |
| Opioid | Respiratory depression, miosis, decreased LOC | Heroin, fentanyl, oxycodone |
| Sedative-hypnotic | Sedation, respiratory depression, ataxia | Benzodiazepines, barbiturates, EtOH |
| Serotonin | AMS, hyperthermia, clonus, hyperreflexia | SSRIs, MAOIs, tramadol |

### 6.2 Acetaminophen Overdose

**Mechanism:** NAPQI (toxic metabolite) depletes glutathione, causes centrilobular hepatic necrosis.

**Rumack-Matthew Nomogram:**
- Plot serum acetaminophen at 4-24 hours post-ingestion
- Treatment line at 150 mcg/mL at 4 hours
- High-risk line at 300 mcg/mL (high-risk ingestion: >30g or concentration above high-risk line) [^574^][^585^]

**N-Acetylcysteine (NAC) Protocol:**
- **IV (Acetadote):** Loading dose 150 mg/kg in 200mL D5W over 60 min, then 50 mg/kg in 500mL over 4 hours, then 100 mg/kg in 1000mL over 16 hours (total 300 mg/kg over 21 hours)
- **2-bag protocol (preferred):** Similar effectiveness, fewer reactions, fewer errors [^578^]
- **Oral:** 140 mg/kg loading, then 70 mg/kg q4h x 17 doses
- Start within 8 hours for nearly 100% hepatoprotection; beneficial even beyond 8 hours [^574^][^581^][^584^]

**NAC Discontinuation Criteria:**
- Patient clinically well
- Acetaminophen level <10 mcg/mL
- INR <2
- AST normal or down-trending
- If peak AST >=1000 IU/L: stop when AST decreased >25% from peak [^578^]

**High-Risk Ingestion:**
- >30g ingested or concentration above high-risk line
- Consider activated charcoal even >4 hours (prolonged absorption)
- Consult toxicology; may need high-dose NAC or fomepizole [^574^]

### 6.3 Opioid Overdose

**Recognition:** Respiratory depression, pinpoint pupils, altered mental status.

**Naloxone Dosing:**
- **Goal:** Restore adequate breathing (not full consciousness)
- **Intranasal:** 2-4mg per nostril (total 4-8mg); may repeat every 2-3 minutes
- **IV:** Start 0.04-0.4mg; titrate up to 2mg; repeat q2-3min as needed
- **IM/SC:** 0.4-2mg; onset 3-10 minutes
- High doses may be needed for fentanyl/analogues (up to 10mg total) [^575^][^582^]

**Fentanyl Considerations:**
- Higher potency requires larger naloxone doses
- Recurrent sedation common (fentanyl longer-acting than naloxone)
- Observe for minimum 2-3 hours; longer for long-acting opioids
- May need naloxone infusion (0.4-0.8x effective bolus dose/hour) [^582^]

### 6.4 Benzodiazepine Overdose

**General Management:** Supportive care is usually sufficient; isolated BDZ overdose rarely fatal [^659^].

**Flumazenil:**
- **Contraindications:** Unknown/mixed overdose, benzodiazepine tolerance/dependence, seizure disorders, prolonged QRS interval (suggests TCA co-ingestion), signs of TCA overdose [^659^][^663^]
- **Serious adverse events:** Seizures, arrhythmias (RR 3.81 vs placebo) [^659^]
- **Dose:** 0.2mg IV over 30 seconds; repeat 0.3-0.5mg q1min to max 3mg
- **When to consider:** Accidental pediatric ingestion, reversal of iatrogenic oversedation, isolated BDZ/Z-drug overdose with respiratory depression [^662^]
- Requires monitoring for at least 2 hours; re-sedation may occur

### 6.5 Tricyclic Antidepressant Overdose

**Mechanism:** Sodium channel blockade (QRS widening), anticholinergic effects, alpha-blockade, seizure risk.

**Key Management:**
- **Sodium Bicarbonate:** Antidote of choice for QRS widening [^619^]
  - Bolus: 50-100 mEq (1-2 ampules of 8.4%)
  - Infusion: 150 mEq in 1L D5W at 150 mL/hr; target serum pH 7.45-7.55
  - Repeat bolus for QRS >100ms or ventricular arrhythmias
  - Monitor for hypokalemia
- Seizures: Benzodiazepines first-line
- Hypotension: Norepinephrine (fluid-resistant)
- Avoid class IA and IC antiarrhythmics; lidocaine may be used
- Intubation if altered mental status or hemodynamic instability

### 6.6 Salicylate Overdose

**Mechanism:** Direct stimulation of respiratory center, uncoupling oxidative phosphorylation, interference with Krebs cycle.

**Management:**
- Activated charcoal (within 1-2 hours)
- **Urinary alkalinization:** Sodium bicarbonate to maintain urine pH 7.5-8.0
- Potassium supplementation (hypokalemia prevents alkalinization)
- Glucose management (even if normal, CNS glucose may be low)
- **Hemodialysis indications:**
  - Salicylate level >100 mg/dL (acute) or >60 mg/dL (chronic)
  - Altered mental status, seizures, cerebral edema
  - Pulmonary edema
  - Renal failure
  - Severe acid-base disturbance refractory to therapy
  - Clinical deterioration despite supportive care

### 6.7 Beta-Blocker / Calcium Channel Blocker Overdose

**Presentation:** Bradycardia, hypotension, AV block, cardiogenic shock; hyperglycemia (CCB), hypoglycemia (BB).

**Management:**

| Intervention | Dosing | Notes |
|-------------|--------|-------|
| Atropine | 0.5-1mg IV; may repeat | Usually ineffective |
| Calcium salts | Calcium chloride 1g or calcium gluconate 3g IV | For CCB; repeat q10-20min |
| Glucagon | 3-10mg IV bolus; 3-5mg/hr infusion | For BB; hyperglycemia side effect |
| High-dose insulin | 1 U/kg bolus; 1-10 U/kg/hr infusion | With dextrose to maintain euglycemia |
| Lipid emulsion | 1.5 mL/kg 20% bolus; 0.25-0.5 mL/kg/min infusion | "Lipid sink" for lipophilic agents |
| Vasopressors | Norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin | High doses often needed |

**High-Dose Insulin (HDI) Protocol:** [^618^]
- 1 U/kg IV bolus + 0.5 g/kg IV dextrose
- Infusion: Start 1 U/kg/hr; titrate to 10 U/kg/hr
- Dextrose: 0.5 g/kg/hr; titrate to maintain glucose 100-200 mg/dL
- Monitor K+ every 30 min; replace as needed
- Onset of hemodynamic effect: up to 60 minutes

**Lipid Emulsion Therapy:**
- 20% Intralipid emulsion
- Bolus: 1.5 mL/kg over 2-3 minutes
- Infusion: 0.25-0.5 mL/kg/min
- May repeat bolus q3-5min for persistent arrest

### 6.8 Digoxin Toxicity

**Presentation:** Nausea, vomiting, visual changes (yellow/green halos), arrhythmias (any arrhythmia possible).

**Digoxin Immune Fab (DigiFab):** [^620^][^621^]
- Indicated for life-threatening arrhythmias, hyperkalemia >5-6.5, hemodynamic instability
- **Acute ingestion (unknown amount):** 20 vials (may start with 10, then give 10 more)
- **Acute (known amount):** Dose (vials) = Amount ingested (mg) / 0.5 mg/vial
- **Chronic toxicity:** Dose (vials) = (Serum digoxin ng/mL x weight kg) / 100
- **Cardiac arrest:** 5 vials immediately; repeat if needed after 30 min
- Each vial contains 40mg digoxin immune Fab
- Watch for hypokalemia after administration; Fab fragments cause rapid K+ shift into cells
- Standard digoxin assays become unreliable after Fab administration [^620^]

### 6.9 Carbon Monoxide Poisoning

**Mechanism:** CO binds hemoglobin with 240x greater affinity than O2, forming carboxyhemoglobin (COHb).

**Management:**
- Remove from source immediately
- **100% oxygen via non-rebreather** (reduces half-life from 4-6h to 40-80 min)
- **Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) indications:** [^622^]
  - Loss of consciousness
  - COHb >25% (>15-20% in pregnancy)
  - Neurologic deficits
  - Cardiac ischemia/arrhythmias
  - Metabolic acidosis (pH <7.1-7.25)
  - End-organ ischemia
  - Best outcomes when initiated within 6 hours
- Pregnant patients: Lower threshold for HBOT; fetal hemoglobin has higher CO affinity

### 6.10 Toxic Alcohols (Methanol, Ethylene Glycol)

**Recognition:** Anion gap metabolic acidosis, osmolar gap >10, visual symptoms (methanol), calcium oxalate crystalluria (ethylene glycol), renal failure (ethylene glycol).

**Fomepizole (Antizol):** First-line antidote [^623^]
- **Loading dose:** 15 mg/kg IV
- **Maintenance:** 10 mg/kg IV q12h x 4 doses; then 15 mg/kg q12h
- **During dialysis:** 1-1.5 mg/kg/hr continuous infusion
- Continue until toxic alcohol <20 mg/dL AND pH normal

**Indications for fomepizole:**
- Documented ingestion with osmolar gap >10
- Toxic alcohol concentration >=20 mg/dL
- Suspected ingestion with: pH <7.3, bicarbonate <20, osmolar gap >10, oxalate crystalluria [^623^]

**Ethanol Alternative:** Loading dose to achieve serum ethanol 100-150 mg/dL; requires monitoring.

**Adjunctive Therapy:**
- Sodium bicarbonate for acidosis (pH <7.3)
- Folinic acid (leucovorin) 1mg/kg (max 50mg) IV q4h for methanol
- Pyridoxine 50mg IV q6h, thiamine 100mg IV q6h for ethylene glycol
- Hemodialysis: Indicated for severe acidosis (pH <7.1), renal failure, visual impairment, deteriorating vitals, methanol >=50 mg/dL [^623^]

### 6.11 Decontamination

| Method | Indications | Contraindications/Limitations |
|--------|------------|------------------------------|
| **Activated charcoal** | Within 1-2 hours of ingestion; extended-release formulations up to 4h | Unprotected airway, GI obstruction, caustic ingestion |
| **Whole bowel irrigation** | Body packers, sustained-release ingestions, iron, lithium | Bowel obstruction, ileus, hemodynamic instability |
| **Gastric lavage** | Very early (<1 hour), life-threatening ingestions | Unprotected airway, caustics, hydrocarbons |
| **Urine alkalinization** | Salicylates, phenobarbital | Renal failure, fluid overload |

- Gastric lavage rarely indicated; charcoal generally preferred
- Do not delay charcoal for imaging or other procedures in serious ingestions

### 6.12 Red Flags
- Multi-organ system involvement
- Widened QRS or prolonged QT
- Severe metabolic acidosis
- Hemodynamic instability
- Seizures
- Coma with unknown ingestion

## 7. Trauma Assessment {#7-trauma}

### 7.1 Clinical Overview

Trauma remains a leading cause of death worldwide. Modern trauma care follows ATLS principles with emphasis on rapid primary survey, damage control resuscitation, and early hemorrhage control [^606^][^607^][^613^].

### 7.2 Primary Survey (ABCDE Approach)

**xABCDE Approach (Updated 10th Edition ATLS):**

**x - Exsanguination Control (First):**
- Direct pressure, tourniquet, wound packing, pelvic binder
- If life-threatening external bleeding, control it BEFORE airway [^606^]

**A - Airway:**
- Assess patency, ability to talk
- Clear airway, chin lift/jaw thrust (no head tilt in trauma)
- Oropharyngeal/nasopharyngeal airway if needed
- Definitive airway: Endotracheal intubation if GCS <8, facial fractures, airway compromise
- C-spine immobilization during airway management
- Video laryngoscopy as default first-attempt device

**B - Breathing:**
- Inspect, palpate, auscultate chest
- Supplemental O2
- Tension pneumothorax: Clinical diagnosis, immediate needle decompression (do not wait for CXR)
- Open pneumothorax: Three-sided occlusive dressing
- Flail chest: Internal fixation if severe, analgesia
- Target SpO2 >94%

**C - Circulation:**
- Assess pulse, hemorrhage sources (chest, abdomen, pelvis, long bones)
- **Two large-bore IVs (14-16 gauge)** or intraosseous access
- **No more than 1 liter crystalloid** before blood products
- Activate massive transfusion protocol if indicated
- FAST exam
- Pelvic binder if unstable pelvis
- Tourniquet for extremity hemorrhage

**D - Disability:**
- GCS (documented formally), pupillary exam
- Blood glucose check
- Naloxone if opioid overdose suspected
- AVPU (Alert, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive)

**E - Exposure:**
- Completely undress patient
- Log-roll with C-spine precautions
- Prevent hypothermia (warm blankets, warmed fluids, raise room temperature) [^606^][^613^]

### 7.3 FAST Exam (Focused Assessment with Sonography for Trauma)

**Views:**
1. Pericardial (subxiphoid or parasternal)
2. Right upper quadrant (hepatorenal/Morison's pouch)
3. Left upper quadrant (splenorenal)
4. Suprapubic (pelvic)

**Positive FAST:** Free fluid in any view
- Indications for emergent laparotomy in hemodynamically unstable patient
- If FAST negative but high suspicion: Consider CT, repeat FAST, or DPL

**Extended FAST (eFAST):** Adds bilateral anterior thoracic views for pneumothorax detection.

### 7.4 Trauma Team Activation Criteria

**Criteria vary by center but typically include:**
- SBP <90 mmHg at any point
- GCS <8 with mechanism of injury
- Intubated patient
- Respiratory rate <10 or >29
- Penetrating injury to head, neck, torso
- Transfer patient receiving blood to maintain vitals
- EMS provider judgment

### 7.5 Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP)

**Activation Criteria:**
- SBP <90 despite initial resuscitation
- Ongoing hemorrhage with anticipated need for >4 units PRBC in 1 hour
- Clinical picture of hemorrhagic shock not responding to initial measures [^606^]

**Blood Products: 1:1:1 Ratio (PROPPR Trial):**
- 1 unit PRBC : 1 unit FFP : 1 unit Platelets
- Evidence shows improved survival compared to 1:1:2 ratio [^606^][^617^]

**Additional Interventions:**
- **Tranexamic acid (TXA):** 1g IV bolus over 10 minutes + 1g infusion over 8 hours
  - Must be given **within 3 hours of injury** (CRASH-2 trial); no benefit after 3 hours
  - 2g bolus for TBI patients [^606^][^617^]
- **Calcium:** Monitor and replete (citrate in blood products binds calcium)
- **Goal:** Permissive hypotension (SBP 80-90) in penetrating trauma WITHOUT head injury
- **Avoid:** Excessive crystalloid (worsens coagulopathy, hypothermia) [^606^]

### 7.6 Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)

**Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS):**
| Component | Score |
|-----------|-------|
| Eye opening | 4-spontaneous, 3-to voice, 2-to pain, 1-none |
| Verbal | 5-oriented, 4-confused, 3-inappropriate, 2-incomprehensible, 1-none |
| Motor | 6-obeys, 5-localizes, 4-withdraws, 3-decorticate, 2-decerebrate, 1-none |

**TBI Severity:**
- Mild: GCS 13-15
- Moderate: GCS 9-12
- Severe: GCS 3-8

**CT Head Criteria (Indications for Imaging):**
- GCS <15 at 2 hours after injury
- Suspected open/depressed skull fracture
- Any sign of basal skull fracture (raccoon eyes, Battle's sign, CSF leak)
- Vomiting >1 episode
- Age >65 years
- Post-traumatic seizure
- Focal neurologic deficit
- Dangerous mechanism (pedestrian struck, fall >3 feet/5 stairs, ejection from vehicle)
- Anticoagulation use

**ICP Management (Brain Trauma Foundation 4th Edition, 2020):** [^653^][^658^]
- ICP monitoring recommended for salvageable severe TBI (GCS 3-8) with abnormal CT
- Target ICP <22 mmHg
- CPP target 60-70 mmHg
- **Tiered approach:**
  - Tier 0: Head of bed 30 degrees, midline, sedation, analgesia
  - Tier 1: Mannitol 0.25-1 g/kg or hypertonic saline (3% 250mL bolus)
  - Tier 2: CSF drainage via EVD, brief hyperventilation (PaCO2 30-35)
  - Tier 3: Decompressive craniectomy (RESCUEicp trial showed mortality benefit but worse functional outcomes), barbiturate coma, hypothermia
- Anticonvulsants: Levetiracetam preferred for early post-traumatic seizures
- Avoid hypotension (SBP <100 in severe TBI) and hypoxia (SpO2 <90) [^653^]

### 7.7 Spinal Immobilization and Clearance

**NEXUS Criteria (Imaging Not Required if ALL met):** [^628^][^633^][^636^]
1. Normal alertness (GCS 15)
2. No intoxication
3. No painful distracting injury
4. No posterior midline cervical tenderness
5. No focal neurologic deficit
- Sensitivity: 99.6% for clinically significant injuries

**Canadian C-Spine Rule (CCR):** [^633^]
- Age 16-65, stable vitals, alert
- Exclusion: Neurologic deficit, dangerous mechanism, known vertebral disease
- Low-risk factors (simple rear-end MVC, sitting in ED, ambulatory at scene, delayed neck pain, no midline tenderness) + able to rotate neck 45 degrees = no imaging needed
- Sensitivity: 100% for significant injuries

**Clearance in Unconscious Patients:**
- CT cervical spine is standard
- If CT negative: MRI for ligamentous injury if concern
- Remove collar when CT negative AND patient awake for exam OR MRI clears

### 7.8 Penetrating Trauma

**Chest:**
- ABCs; immediate thoracotomy if tamponade physiology or exsanguination
- "Tube thoracostomy first" for most hemothoraces
- Pericardial window or ED thoracotomy for cardiac injury

**Abdomen:**
- Hemodynamically unstable with positive FAST = immediate laparotomy
- Stable patients: CT imaging, selective non-operative management for solid organ injuries
- Bowel injury often requires operative repair

### 7.9 Red Flags
- Hemodynamic instability (SBP <90)
- GCS declining
- Unequal pupils
- Open skull fracture
- Signs of raised ICP
- Expanding neck hematoma
- Tension pneumothorax
- Peritonitis

## 8. Environmental Emergencies {#8-environmental}

### 8.1 Heat-Related Illness

**Classification:**
| Condition | Features | Management |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| Heat cramps | Muscle cramps, sweating preserved | Rest, oral fluids, salt replacement |
| Heat exhaustion | Temp <40C, heavy sweating, fatigue, nausea | Cool environment, oral fluids, active cooling |
| **Heat stroke** | **Temp >40C, CNS dysfunction (delirium, seizures, coma)** | **Aggressive active cooling; mortality increases with delay** |

**Heat Stroke Management:** [^610^][^612^][^616^]
- **Most important step:** Rapid cooling at rate of 1C every 10 minutes until 38C (100.4F) reached
- **Cold water immersion:** Gold standard for exertional heat stroke; cooling rates up to 0.15-0.2C/min
- **Evaporative cooling:** Spray water (15C) + fan; remove clothing
- **Conductive cooling:** Ice packs to groin, axillae, neck; massage skin to prevent vasoconstriction
- **IV fluids:** Cooled crystalloids
- **No role for antipyretics** (acetaminophen, aspirin) or dantrolene
- Monitor for complications: seizures, rhabdomyolysis, DIC, AKI, liver injury, ARDS
- Admit for at least 48 hours [^616^]

**Cooling Methods Comparison:**
| Method | Cooling Rate | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Cold water immersion | Fastest (0.15-0.35C/min) | Exertional heat stroke |
| Evaporative + convective | Moderate | Classic heat stroke, hospital setting |
| Ice packs | Slow | Adjunctive |
| Cooled IV fluids | Minimal | Adjunctive |

### 8.2 Hypothermia

**Classification (Swiss Staging System):**
| Stage | Core Temp | Consciousness | Cardiac Activity |
|-------|-----------|---------------|------------------|
| HT I | 35-32C | Conscious | Normal |
| HT II | 32-28C | Impaired consciousness | Risk of arrhythmias |
| HT III | 28-24C | Unconscious | Osborn J waves, risk of VF |
| HT IV | 24-13.7C | Apparent death | VF, asystole, vital signs may be undetectable |

**ECG Findings:**
- Osborn (J) waves: Positive deflection at end of QRS; most prominent in V3-V6
- Bradycardia, prolonged intervals, AF common
- VF risk increases <28C; cardiac arrest may occur with rough handling

**Management:** [^609^]
- **Mild (HT I):** Passive rewarming (warm blankets, warm drinks if alert)
- **Moderate (HT II):** Active external rewarming (warm air, Bair Hugger); warm IV fluids
- **Severe (HT III-IV):** Active internal rewarming required
  - Warm IV fluids (40-42C)
  - Warm humidified O2 (42-46C)
  - Bladder/gastric lavage with warm fluid
  - Thoracic lavage (bilateral chest tubes)
  - **Extracorporeal rewarming (ECMO/CPB):** Most effective; preferred for cardiac arrest
- CPR: Handle gently; may use intermittent CPR if continuous not possible during rescue
- Defibrillation: 3 shocks if VF/VT at <30C; withhold further shocks until >30C
- Medications: Withhold until core temp >30C; double intervals until normothermia [^609^]

### 8.3 Drowning

**Definition:** Process of experiencing respiratory impairment from submersion/immersion in liquid.

**Resuscitation:** [^609^]
- Start with 5 ventilations before compressions (oxygenation priority)
- Then standard CPR protocol
- Do NOT attempt to drain water (delays CPR, risks aspiration)
- C-spine immobilization should NOT delay rescue from water
- All drowning victims need hospital evaluation (even if asymptomatic)
- Monitor for delayed ARDS (24-72 hours)

**Prognostic Factors:**
- Submersion duration <5 minutes = better outcome
- Time to effective CPR <10 minutes
- GCS >13 at presentation
- Pupillary response present
- Salty vs fresh water distinction NOT clinically relevant

### 8.4 Altitude Illness

**Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS):**
- Headache + GI (anorexia, nausea) or fatigue/dizziness
- Lake Louise Score: Mild (1-2 symptoms), moderate (3-5), severe (>5)

**High-Altitude Cerebral Edema (HACE):**
- End-stage AMS; ataxia, altered mental status, can progress to coma
- Can occur <4300m but rare

**High-Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE):**
- Most common cause of death from altitude illness
- Dyspnea at rest, cough, frothy sputum, cyanosis
- Tachycardia, tachypnea

**Management:** [^660^][^661^]

| Condition | Primary Treatment | Adjuncts |
|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| Mild AMS | Stop ascent; rest; symptomatic treatment | Acetazolamide 250mg BID |
| Moderate-severe AMS | Descent 300-1000m | Dexamethasone 4mg q6h; supplemental O2 |
| HACE | **Immediate descent** | Dexamethasone 8mg load, then 4mg q6h; O2; hyperbaric chamber |
| HAPE | **Immediate descent** + rest | Nifedipine 30mg SR BID; O2; PDE-5 inhibitors if nifedipine unavailable |

**Prevention:** [^661^]
- Gradual ascent (no more than 500m sleeping altitude increase/day above 3000m)
- Acetazolamide 125mg BID starting day before ascent (250mg if >100kg)
- Dexamethasone 2mg q6h or 4mg q12h (alternative if acetazolamide contraindicated)
- Nifedipine 20-30mg SR q8-12h for HAPE prevention (prior HAPE history)
- Avoid alcohol first 48 hours

**Medication Dosing Summary:** [^661^]
| Medication | Indication | Dose |
|------------|-----------|------|
| Acetazolamide | AMS/HACE prevention | 125mg BID PO; 250mg BID if >100kg |
| Acetazolamide | AMS treatment | 250mg BID PO |
| Dexamethasone | AMS/HACE prevention | 2mg q6h or 4mg q12h |
| Dexamethasone | HACE treatment | 8mg IV/IM/PO, then 4mg q6h |
| Nifedipine | HAPE prevention | 20mg SR q8h or 30mg SR q12h |
| Nifedipine | HAPE treatment | 30mg SR q12h |

### 8.5 Electrical Injuries

**AC vs DC Current:**
- **AC (alternating current):** More dangerous; causes tetany ("let-go" threshold ~6mA); continuous muscle contraction
- **DC (direct current):** Single intense contraction; often throws victim away

**Lightning Strikes:**
- Direct strike, side flash, or ground current
- Cardiac: Immediate asystole (primary arrhythmia); respiratory arrest may persist
- keraunoparalysis: Transient lower extremity paralysis
- Lichtenberg figures (ferning): Pathognomonic skin pattern
- **Reverse triage:** Attend to those in cardiac arrest first (may survive vs typical cardiac arrest)

**Management:**
- ABCs; CPR for cardiac arrest (prolonged CPR may be successful)
- Fluid resuscitation: Rhabdomyolysis common; urine output goal 1-2 mL/kg/hr
- Alkalinize urine for rhabdomyolysis
- Monitor for compartment syndrome
- ECG for all patients; troponins if high-voltage or symptoms
- Fasciotomy if compartment pressures elevated
- Secondary survey for exit wounds (worse than entry wounds)

### 8.6 Envenomation (Snakebite)

**General Principles:** [^650^][^651^][^655^][^656^][^657^]

**First Aid:**
- Immobilize patient and bitten limb
- Remove rings/jewelry from affected limb
- Pressure immobilization with elastic bandage (controversial; recommended for neurotoxic elapid bites in some regions)
- **Do NOT:** Apply tourniquet, cut/suck wound, use electric shock, ice

**Assessment:**
- ABCs; 20-minute whole blood clotting test (20WBCT) for coagulopathy
- Monitor for at least 8 hours (asymptomatic) or 12-24 hours (if envenomated)

**Antivenom:**
- IV administration; same dose adults and children
- Indications: Shock, spontaneous systemic bleeding, uncoagulable blood, neurotoxicity, AKI, rapidly progressive local swelling
- Monitor for anaphylaxis first 2 hours; treat with epinephrine IM
- Repeat if bleeding persists or worsens within 2 hours

**Discharge:**
- Asymptomatic patients with negative 20WBCT at 24 hours may be discharged
- 2 weeks of bleeding precautions after hemotoxic envenomation
- Follow-up at 3-4 days

### 8.7 Red Flags
- Heat stroke: CNS dysfunction + temp >40C
- Hypothermia: Core temp <28C (risk of VF); cardiac arrest
- Drowning: Submersion >10 minutes, no CPR within 10 minutes
- HACE: Ataxia, altered mental status at altitude
- HAPE: Dyspnea at rest, pink frothy sputum
- Lightning: Cardiac arrest, keraunoparalysis
- Snakebite: Respiratory paralysis, coagulopathy, AKI

## 9. Acute Respiratory Failure {#9-respiratory}

### 9.1 Clinical Overview

Acute respiratory failure is the inability of the respiratory system to maintain adequate gas exchange, defined as PaO2 <60 mmHg with FiO2 >0.6 or PaCO2 >50 mmHg with pH <7.35.

### 9.2 Classification

| Type | Mechanism | PaO2 | PaCO2 | Common Causes |
|------|-----------|------|-------|---------------|
| **Type I (Hypoxemic)** | V/Q mismatch, shunt, diffusion impairment | Low | Normal/Low | Pneumonia, pulmonary edema, ARDS, PE |
| **Type II (Hypercapnic)** | Alveolar hypoventilation | May be normal/Low | High | COPD exacerbation, neuromuscular disease, opioid overdose |

### 9.3 NIV: CPAP vs BiPAP

**CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure):**
- Single pressure level
- Indications: Hypoxemic respiratory failure (cardiogenic pulmonary edema, chest wall trauma, OSA, selected pneumonia/ARDS) [^639^]
- Reduces afterload, improves oxygenation by alveolar recruitment

**BiPAP (Bilevel Positive Airway Pressure):**
- Two pressure levels: IPAP and EPAP
- Indications: Hypercapnic respiratory failure (COPD exacerbation, obesity hypoventilation, neuromuscular disease) [^639^][^641^]
- IPAP reduces work of breathing; EPAP maintains alveolar recruitment

**Starting Settings:**
| Parameter | Initial Setting | Titration |
|-----------|----------------|-----------|
| CPAP | 5-10 cmH2O | Up to 15 based on O2 and tolerance |
| BiPAP IPAP | 10-15 cmH2O | Increase to target tidal volume; max 30 without specialist input |
| BiPAP EPAP | 3-5 cmH2O | Increase for hypoxemia; max 8 without specialist input |
| Backup rate | 12-16 breaths/min | Adjust for patient comfort |

[^639^][^641^][^643^]

### 9.4 NIV Indications by Condition

| Condition | Evidence | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------|----------------|
| AECOPD | Strong | BiPAP; pH <7.35 + PaCO2 >49 mmHg |
| Cardiogenic pulmonary edema | Strong | CPAP or BiPAP both effective |
| Acute asthma | Weak/Not recommended | ERS/ATS guidelines do NOT recommend [^642^] |
| AHRF (pneumonia/ARDS) | Moderate | Select patients; CPAP or BiPAP |
| Post-extubation (high-risk) | Strong | Preventive BiPAP reduces re-intubation |
| Immunocompromised | Moderate | May avoid intubation |

[^641^][^642^]

### 9.5 NIV Contraindications

**Absolute:**
- Cardiac/respiratory arrest
- Inability to protect airway
- Coma (GCS <8)
- Severe facial deformity/burns
- Fixed upper airway obstruction
- Undrained pneumothorax [^639^][^641^][^645^]

**Relative:**
- Hemodynamic instability
- Confusion/agitation
- Copious secretions/vomiting
- Severe acidosis (pH <7.15)
- Recent upper GI/upper airway surgery
- Bowel obstruction [^641^][^643^]

**NIV Failure Indicators:**
- pH not improving after 1-2 hours
- Worsening ABGs after 1 hour
- Worsening mental status
- Respiratory rate >35/min
- Hemodynamic instability
- Inability to maintain SpO2 [^641^][^643^]

### 9.6 Intubation Criteria

- Failure of NIV after 1-2 hour trial
- GCS <8 with inability to protect airway
- Severe acidosis (pH <7.25) with worsening
- Severe hypoxemia (PaO2/FiO2 <100) refractory to O2
- Hemodynamic instability
- Cardiac/respiratory arrest

### 9.7 Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI)

**Steps:** [^646^][^648^][^649^]

1. **Preparation:** Suction, oxygen, airway equipment, drugs, monitoring, ETCO2
2. **Preoxygenation:** 100% FiO2 for 3-5 min; NRM >40 L/min; consider apneic O2 via nasal cannula
3. **Pretreatment:** Lidocaine 1-1.5 mg/kg (if time); fentanyl for sympathetic response
4. **Induction + Paralysis:**

| Agent | Dose | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|
| **Ketamine** | 1.5-2 mg/kg IV | Preferred for hemodynamically unstable; bronchodilation |
| **Etomidate** | 0.3-0.4 mg/kg | Hemodynamically neutral; adrenal suppression concern |
| **Propofol** | 1.5-2.5 mg/kg | For stable patients; hypotension risk |
| **Rocuronium** | 1-1.2 mg/kg | Preferred NMBA; 60 sec onset; 40-90 min duration |
| **Succinylcholine** | 1.5 mg/kg | 45 sec onset; contraindications: hyperkalemia risk, MH |

[^646^][^649^]

5. **Positioning:** Head-elevated; video laryngoscopy preferred
6. **Placement confirmation:** Direct visualization, ETCO2, chest rise, auscultation
7. **Post-intubation:** CXR, sedation, analgesia, lung-protective ventilation

### 9.8 Ventilator Settings: Lung-Protective Strategy

| Parameter | Setting | Rationale |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| Mode | Volume or pressure control | Either acceptable |
| Tidal volume | 6 mL/kg predicted body weight | Prevents volutrauma |
| Plateau pressure | <30 cmH2O | Prevents barotrauma |
| Driving pressure | <15 cmH2O | Strong mortality predictor |
| PEEP | 5-15 cmH2O (titrated) | Alveolar recruitment |
| FiO2 | Titrate to SpO2 88-95% (PaO2 55-80) | Avoid hyperoxia |
| Respiratory rate | 20-35/min | Maintain pH >7.25 |
| I:E ratio | 1:1 to 1:3 | Prolonged expiration for COPD |

### 9.9 ARDS Berlin Definition [^611^]

| Timing | Oxygenation | Chest Imaging | Origin of Edema |
|--------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Acute onset (<1 week) | Mild: 200 < PaO2/FiO2 <= 300 | Bilateral opacities | Not fully explained by cardiac failure/fluid overload |
| Known risk factor or new/worsening respiratory symptoms | Moderate: 100 < PaO2/FiO2 <= 200 | Not fully explained by effusions, nodules, lobar/lung collapse | Requires objective assessment (echo) if no risk factor |
| | Severe: PaO2/FiO2 <= 100 | | |

### 9.10 ARDS Management

**Evidence-Based Interventions:**

1. **Lung-protective ventilation:** TV 4-8 mL/kg PBW, plateau <30 cmH2O [^604^][^611^]
2. **Prone positioning:** For moderate-severe ARDS (PaO2/FiO2 <150); >=16 hours/session; shown mortality benefit (PROSEVA trial) [^604^][^614^]
3. **Conservative fluid management:** After initial resuscitation; diurese to even or negative balance
4. **Neuromuscular blockade:** Intermittent boluses preferred over continuous infusion for PaO2/FiO2 <150
5. **ECMO:** For refractory hypoxemia when conventional management fails; experienced centers only

**PEEP Strategy:**
- Titrated to oxygenation and compliance
- Higher PEEP for moderate-severe ARDS
- Monitor hemodynamics (higher PEEP can reduce preload)
- Driving pressure (plateau - PEEP) most predictive of survival [^615^]

**Refractory Hypoxemia:**
- Inhaled vasodilators (epoprostenol, nitric oxide) - temporary bridge
- Prone positioning
- ECMO consideration
- High PEEP strategy
- Recruitment maneuvers (controversial)

### 9.11 Red Flags
- PaO2/FiO2 <100 (severe ARDS)
- Refractory hypoxemia despite maximal ventilator settings
- Need for ECMO
- Ventilator-induced lung injury
- Barotrauma (pneumothorax)

## 10. Acute Renal Failure and Electrolyte Emergencies {#10-renal}

### 10.1 Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

**KDIGO Staging:**
| Stage | Creatinine | Urine Output |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| 1 | 1.5-1.9x baseline or >=0.3 mg/dL increase | <0.5 mL/kg/hr for 6-12 hours |
| 2 | 2.0-2.9x baseline | <0.5 mL/kg/hr for >=12 hours |
| 3 | >=3.0x baseline or >=4.0 mg/dL or RRT | <0.3 mL/kg/hr for >=24 hours or anuria >=12 hours |

**Causes:**
- **Prerenal:** Hypovolemia, CHF, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, renovascular disease
- **Intrinsic:** ATN (ischemic/nephrotoxic), AIN, glomerulonephritis, rhabdomyolysis
- **Postrenal:** BPH, ureteral obstruction, neurogenic bladder, nephrolithiasis

**Management:**
- Treat underlying cause
- Fluid management: Resuscitate if volume-depleted; restrict if volume-overloaded
- Avoid nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, contrast when possible)
- Dose-adjust medications
- Indications for dialysis: AEIOU
  - **A**cidosis (refractory)
  - **E**lectrolyte abnormalities (refractory hyperkalemia)
  - **I**ntoxications (dialyzable toxins)
  - **O**verload (volume overload refractory to diuretics)
  - **U**remia (pericarditis, encephalopathy, bleeding)

### 10.2 Hyperkalemia

**ECG Changes (progressive):**
- Peaked T waves (earliest)
- PR prolongation, P wave flattening
- QRS widening
- Sine wave pattern (pre-arrest)
- VF/asystole

**Management (3-Step Approach):** [^625^][^626^]

**Step 1: Stabilize Myocardium (Minutes)**
- **Calcium gluconate:** 30 mL of 10% IV over 10 minutes (via large bore IV with cardiac monitoring)
  - Onset: 3 minutes; Duration: 30-60 minutes
  - May repeat in 5-10 minutes if ECG changes persist
  - Alternative: Calcium chloride 10 mL of 10% (3x more calcium; preferred if hemodynamically unstable)
  - **Do NOT give via line containing bicarbonate** (precipitation risk)

**Step 2: Shift K+ into Cells (Minutes to Hours)**
- **Insulin/Glucose:** 10 units regular insulin + 50 mL 50% glucose IV over 30 minutes
  - Onset: 15-30 min; Duration: 2-6 hours
  - Monitor glucose q30-60 min; recheck K+ at 60 min [^625^]
- **Beta-agonists:** Salbutamol 10-20mg nebulized; onset 30 min
- **Sodium bicarbonate:** 50-100 mEq IV; especially if pH <7.2

**Step 3: Remove K+ from Body (Hours)**
- **Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate (SZC):** 10g PO TID; onset 1-2 hours [^625^]
- **Sodium polystyrene sulfonate (Kayexalate):** 15-30g PO/PR; onset 2-6 hours (slower)
- **Loop diuretics:** Furosemide 40-80mg IV (if euvolemic/hypervolemic)
- **Hemodialysis:** Definitive for severe/refractory hyperkalemia; immediate if life-threatening ECG changes

### 10.3 Hyponatremia

**Symptomatic (Severe):** Seizures, altered mental status, coma
- **3% hypertonic saline:** 100 mL IV bolus over 10 minutes; may repeat x2
- Goal: Raise Na by 4-6 mEq/L acutely to stop seizures
- Then limit correction rate

**Rate of Correction (Critical):**
- **Chronic hyponatremia (>48h):**
  - Limit correction to **<8-12 mEq/L in first 24 hours**
  - Limit to **<18 mEq/L in first 48 hours**
  - **High-risk patients** (Na <115, alcohol use, malnutrition, liver disease, hypokalemia): Limit to **<8 mEq/L in 24 hours** [^624^][^627^]

**Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS):**
- Can occur even with correction <=10 mEq/L/24h if risk factors present
- Risk greatest when Na <115 mEq/L
- Risk factors: Alcohol use disorder, liver disease, malnutrition, hypokalemia, Na <=105
- Can cause locked-in syndrome, quadriplegia, cranial nerve palsies
- **Prevention:** Slow correction; consider desmopressin if over-correction occurring; thiamine supplementation

**Fluid Restriction:**
- First-line for SIADH: <800-1200 mL/day
- Salt tablets, urea, tolvaptan (vasopressin antagonist) for refractory

### 10.4 Severe Metabolic Acidosis

**Sodium Bicarbonate Indications:**
- pH <7.1 with hemodynamic compromise
- Bicarbonate <5 mEq/L
- Cardiac arrest with hyperkalemia
- Salicylate overdose
- Tricyclic antidepressant overdose
- Methanol/ethylene glycol poisoning

**Dosing:**
- 1-2 mEq/kg IV bolus; then infusion titrated to pH
- Target pH 7.2 (not normalization)
- Watch for volume overload, hypokalemia, hypocalcemia
- Hypertonic bicarbonate (8.4%) preferred in fluid-overloaded patients

### 10.5 Red Flags
- Hyperkalemia with ECG changes = emergency
- Hyponatremia with seizures = emergency
- Oliguria <0.3 mL/kg/hr
- Refractory acidosis (pH <7.1)
- Uremic pericarditis or encephalopathy

## 11. Psychiatric Emergencies {#11-psychiatric}

### 11.1 Suicidal Ideation Assessment

**Risk Factors:**
- Prior suicide attempt (strongest predictor)
- Psychiatric illness (depression, bipolar, schizophrenia, substance use, borderline PD)
- Access to lethal means
- Recent loss or stressful life event
- Family history of suicide
- Male gender, age >65 or 15-24
- Social isolation
- Chronic medical illness, pain
- Recent discharge from psychiatric hospitalization

**Assessment Tools:**
- **Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS):** Screening and severity assessment
- **SAFE-T framework:** Ideation, plan, intent, means, protective factors
- **PAPERS:** Plan, Access, Protective factors, Earlier attempts, Resources/Support, Substance use

**Safety Planning:**
1. Recognize warning signs
2. Employ internal coping strategies
3. Socialize with people who may offer support
4. Contact family members or friends who may help
5. Contact professionals and agencies
6. Reduce access to lethal means (means restriction counseling)
7. Reasons for living

**Disposition:**
- Voluntary or involuntary admission if high risk
- Contract for safety NOT considered adequate protection
- Remove firearms, medications, sharp objects
- Ensure close follow-up if discharged

### 11.2 Agitated/Delirious Patient

**De-escalation First (Verbal):**
- Ensure safety of patient and staff
- Calm environment, limit stimuli
- Respect personal space (2 arm lengths)
- Non-threatening posture, open hands
- Active listening, empathetic response
- Offer choices, set clear limits
- Do not: crowd, touch without warning, argue, make threats

**Chemical Sedation (if de-escalation fails):**

| Agent | Dose | Onset | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|-------|
| **Midazolam** | 2-5mg IM/IV | 3-5 min (IV) | Good for alcohol withdrawal; respiratory depression |
| **Haloperidol** | 5-10mg IM/IV | 15-30 min | EPS risk; add lorazepam or benztropine |
| **Olanzapine** | 5-10mg IM | 15-30 min | Less EPS; avoid with benzodiazepines |
| **Ketamine** | 4-5mg/kg IM | 3-5 min | For severe agitation; dissociative sedation |
| **Lorazepam** | 1-2mg IM/IV | 15-30 min (IM) | Good for alcohol/BZD withdrawal |

- Monitor for oversedation and respiratory depression
- Consider restraints as last resort; never as punishment
- Reassess q15 min; offer food, fluids, toileting
- Search for underlying cause (infection, metabolic, substance, pain)

### 11.3 Serotonin Syndrome vs Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome

| Feature | Serotonin Syndrome | Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Cause** | Serotonergic agents (SSRIs, MAOIs, TCAs, fentanyl, tramadol, MDMA, lithium) | Dopamine antagonists (antipsychotics, metoclopramide) |
| **Onset** | Hours (<24h) | Days (gradual) |
| **Mental status** | Agitation, delirium, coma | Stupor, mutism, fluctuating consciousness |
| **Muscles** | Hyperreflexia, clonus, tremor, myoclonus | "Lead-pipe" rigidity (severe) |
| **Reflexes** | Hyperreflexia | Hyporeflexia |
| **Pupils** | Dilated (mydriasis) | Normal |
| **Bowel sounds** | Hyperactive | Decreased/absent |
| **Skin** | Diaphoresis, flushing | Pallor, diaphoresis |
| **Temperature** | Usually <40C, but can be >40C | >40C common |
| **CK** | Mildly elevated or normal | Markedly elevated (often >1000) |
| **WBC** | Normal/mildly elevated | Elevated |
| **Iron** | Normal | Decreased |

[^629^][^630^][^631^][^632^][^635^]

**Hunter Serotonin Toxicity Criteria:**
- Patient taking serotonergic agent PLUS:
  - Spontaneous clonus, OR
  - Inducible clonus + agitation or diaphoresis, OR
  - Ocular clonus + agitation or diaphoresis, OR
  - Tremor + hyperreflexia, OR
  - Hypertonia + temperature >38C + ocular or inducible clonus

**NMS Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5/Consensus):**
- Exposure to dopamine antagonist within 72 hours
- Hyperthermia (>38C on 2 occasions)
- Muscle rigidity
- Altered mental status
- Plus 5 of: tachycardia, BP elevation/lability, diaphoresis/sialorrhea, tremor, incontinence, CK >4x ULN, leukocytosis, metabolic acidosis

**Management:**

| | Serotonin Syndrome | NMS |
|---|---|---|
| **First-line** | Discontinue serotonergic agents | Discontinue dopamine antagonist; supportive care |
| **Supportive** | Cooling, IV fluids, benzodiazepines | Cooling, IV fluids, dantrolene |
| **Specific** | Cyproheptadine 12mg PO, then 2mg q2h | Bromocriptine 2.5mg PO/NG TID; dantrolene 1-2.5mg/kg IV |
| **Benzodiazepines** | For agitation, tremor, hyperreflexia | For rigidity, autonomic symptoms |
| **Resolution** | 24-72 hours | Days to 2 weeks |

[^629^][^630^][^635^]

### 11.4 Alcohol Withdrawal

**CIWA-Ar (Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment for Alcohol):**
- 10-item scale; max 67
- Score 0-8: Minimal; 9-14: Mild; 15-20: Moderate; >20: Severe
- Monitor q1-4h depending on severity

**Benzodiazepine Protocol:** [^634^]
- **Symptom-triggered** preferred over fixed-schedule (shorter duration, less sedation)
- CIWA >=20: Diazepam 10-20mg (or lorazepam 2-4mg) q1h PRN
- CIWA 15-19: Recheck in 1 hour; medicate if persists
- CIWA 10-14: Recheck q2h
- CIWA <10: Recheck q4h
- Continue for minimum 48 hours after last dose
- **Phenobarbital** alternative/addition for refractory cases
- Thiamine 100mg IV before glucose (prevent Wernicke's)
- Multivitamin (banana bag) supplementation
- Haloperidol for hallucinations only (does NOT treat withdrawal)

**Delirium Tremens (DTs):**
- Onset 48-96 hours after last drink
- Characterized by: Delirium, hallucinations, autonomic hyperactivity, agitation, fever
- Mortality 1-5% with treatment; up to 20% without
- ICU monitoring required
- High-dose benzodiazepines; may need propofol or phenobarbital infusion

### 11.5 Opioid Withdrawal

**Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale (COWS):**
- 11-item scale; score 5-12 mild, 13-24 moderate, 25-36 moderately severe, >36 severe
- Resting pulse rate, sweating, restlessness, pupil size, bone/joint aches, runny nose, GI upset, tremor, yawning, anxiety, gooseflesh

**Buprenorphine Microinduction:** [^654^]
- **Traditional induction:** Wait 12-24 hours after last short-acting opioid (COWS >=12), then give buprenorphine; risk of precipitated withdrawal
- **Microinduction (for inpatients):** Gradual overlapping transition from full agonist to buprenorphine
  - Continue full agonist PRN throughout
  - Step 1: 225 mcg buccal Belbuca q6h x3 doses
  - Step 2: 450 mcg buccal q3h x6 doses
  - Step 3: 2mg SL buprenorphine q6h x4 doses
  - Step 4: 4mg SL q6h x4 doses (total 16mg)
  - Step 5: Wean full agonist; consolidate to daily dosing
  - Few patients need >24mg/day
  - Advantages: Avoids withdrawal period; suitable for patients on methadone or fentanyl
- **Macrodosing:** Start >=16mg; may reduce precipitated withdrawal by rapidly stimulating receptors

**Alternative Treatments:**
- Methadone (in controlled settings)
- Clonidine (alpha-2 agonist) for autonomic symptoms: 0.1-0.2mg q2-4h PRN; watch for hypotension
- Lofexidine (FDA-approved for opioid withdrawal)
- Symptomatic: Loperamide, ondansetron, NSAIDs, sleep aids

### 11.6 Red Flags
- Active suicidal intent with plan and means
- Psychosis with command hallucinations
- Severe serotonin syndrome (temperature >40C, severe hypertonia)
- NMS with CK >10,000, rhabdomyolysis, acute kidney injury
- Delirium tremens (autonomic instability, altered mental status)
- Severe alcohol withdrawal seizure
- Excited delirium with hyperthermia (risk of sudden death)
