## 1. HEADACHE

### 1.1 Clinical Overview

Headache is one of the most common presenting complaints in clinical practice. The International Classification of Headache Disorders (ICHD-3) categorizes headaches into primary (tension-type, migraine, cluster, trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias) and secondary (due to underlying pathology) [^1^].

### 1.2 Tension-Type Headache (TTH)

**Definition:** Bilateral, pressing or tightening pain of mild-to-moderate intensity, not aggravated by routine physical activity. No nausea or vomiting; photophobia or phonophobia may be present but not both [^1^].

**Management:**
- Acute: Simple analgesics (acetaminophen, ibuprofen, naproxen); avoid combination analgesics and opioids
- Preventive (≥10 headache days/month): Amitriptyline 10-75 mg at bedtime; venlafaxine 150 mg for chronic TTH; mirtazapine; acupuncture may be effective
- Lifestyle: Regular sleep, exercise, stress management, hydration

### 1.3 Migraine

**Definition:** Recurrent headache disorder with attacks lasting 4-72 hours. Characteristic features: unilateral location, pulsating quality, moderate-to-severe pain intensity, aggravation by routine physical activity, and association with nausea/vomiting and/or photophobia and phonophobia [^1^].

**With Aura:** Recurrent fully reversible visual, sensory, or other CNS symptoms developing gradually over ≥5 minutes, each symptom lasting 5-60 minutes.

**Acute Treatment (Stepwise):**
1. **Mild-to-Moderate:** NSAIDs (ibuprofen 400-800 mg, naproxen 500 mg), acetaminophen (1000 mg), or aspirin (900-1000 mg) [^2^]
2. **Moderate-to-Severe:** Triptans (sumatriptan 50-100 mg PO; 6 mg SC; zolmitriptan 2.5-5 mg; rizatriptan 10 mg) [^2^]
3. **Severe/Refractory:** SC sumatriptan 6 mg, DHE IV/IM, antiemetics (metoclopramide 10 mg, prochlorperazine 10 mg) [^2^]

**CGRP-Targeted Therapies (Novel):**
- **Gepants (acute):** Rimegepant 75 mg ODT; ubrogepant 50-100 mg; zavegepant intranasal — do NOT cause vasoconstriction; safe in cardiovascular disease [^3^]
- **Ditans:** Lasmiditan 50-200 mg — no vasoconstriction; caution with driving [^3^]

**Preventive Therapy:**

Indications: ≥4 migraine days/month, disabling attacks, medication overuse, contraindication to acute therapy [^2^].

| Class | Medication | Evidence Level |
|-------|-----------|----------------|
| **CGRP mAbs** | Erenumab 70-140 mg/month SC; fremanezumab 225 mg/month; galcanezumab 120 mg/month; eptinezumab 100-300 mg IV q3mo | A |
| **Gepants** | Atogepant 10-60 mg daily; rimegepant 75 mg every other day | A |
| **Beta-blockers** | Propranolol 80-240 mg/day; metoprolol; timolol | A |
| **Anticonvulsants** | Topiramate 25-100 mg/day; valproate 500-1500 mg/day | A |
| **OnabotulinumtoxinA** | 155-195 units IM every 12 weeks (chronic migraine ≥15 days/month) | A |
| **TCAs** | Amitriptyline 10-50 mg; venlafaxine 150 mg | B |
| **ARBs** | Candesartan 4-16 mg | B |

**Contraindications:**
- Triptans: Ischemic heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, hemiplegic migraine, basilar migraine, pregnancy
- Beta-blockers: Asthma, bradycardia, heart block, peripheral vascular disease
- Topiramate: Pregnancy (teratogenic), kidney stones, glaucoma
- Valproate: Pregnancy (neural tube defects), liver disease

### 1.4 Cluster Headache

**Definition:** Severe unilateral orbital, supraorbital, or temporal pain lasting 15-180 minutes, occurring up to 8 times daily, with ipsilateral autonomic features (lacrimation, conjunctival injection, nasal congestion, ptosis/miosis). Excruciating intensity; patients often pace/rock [^4^].

**Acute (Abortive) Treatment:**
- **100% Oxygen:** 12-15 L/min via non-rebreather mask for 15-20 minutes — first-line; most effective and safest [^4^][^5^]
- **Sumatriptan 6 mg SC:** Most effective pharmacologic abortive; repeat once after 1 hour if needed [^4^]
- **Zolmitriptan 5-10 mg intranasal:** Alternative for patients without IV access [^4^]
- **DHE IV/IM:** Effective alternative

**Preventive Therapy:**
- **Verapamil:** First-line; start 80 mg TID, titrate to 240-480 mg/day; ECG before each dose increase; monitor for bradycardia, heart block [^4^][^5^]
- **Lithium:** 300-1200 mg/day; serum levels 0.4-1.2 mEq/L; monitor thyroid/renal function
- **Topiramate:** Start 25 mg daily, titrate to 100-400 mg/day
- **Galcanezumab:** 300 mg/month SC — FDA-approved for episodic cluster headache [^5^]
- **Greater occipital nerve block:** Transitional therapy [^5^]

**Transitional (Bridge) Therapy:**
- Prednisone 50-80 mg/day tapered over 10-12 days; suboccipital steroid injection

**SUICIDE RISK:** Screen all patients — cluster headache has one of the highest associations with suicidal ideation of any pain condition [^4^].

### 1.5 Medication Overuse Headache (MOH)

**Definition:** Headache occurring ≥15 days/month in a patient with pre-existing headache disorder, associated with regular overuse of acute headache medication for >3 months [^1^].

**Thresholds for Overuse:**
- Triptans, ergot alkaloids, combination analgesics, opioids: ≥10 days/month
- Simple analgesics (acetaminophen, NSAIDs): ≥15 days/month

**Management:**
- Patient education; gradual withdrawal of overused medication; may require bridge therapy
- Preventive therapy should be initiated or optimized
- Opioids should NEVER be used for headache

### 1.6 Thunderclap Headache (SAH)

**Definition:** Severe headache reaching maximum intensity within seconds to a minute ("worst headache of life"). Primary thunderclap headache is a diagnosis of exclusion [^6^].

**Differential Diagnosis (Red Flags):**
- Subarachnoid hemorrhage (most feared — 10-25% of thunderclap headaches)
- Cervical artery dissection
- Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis
- Reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome (RCVS)
- Hypertensive emergency
- Acute angle-closure glaucoma
- Meningoencephalitis
- Spontaneous intracranial hypotension

**Workup:**
- **Immediate non-contrast CT head:** Sensitivity for SAH >95% within 6 hours of symptom onset [^6^]
- **If CT negative and <6 hours:** CT angiography or lumbar puncture (xanthochromia)
- **If CT negative and >6 hours:** Lumbar puncture mandatory [^6^]

### 1.7 Headache Red Flags ("SNOOP4")

| Mnemonic | Feature | Possible Etiology |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| **S**ystemic | Fever, weight loss, malignancy, immunosuppression | Infection, malignancy, temporal arteritis |
| **N**eurologic | Abnormal exam, confusion, seizures, focal deficits | Mass lesion, stroke, infection |
| **O**nset | Sudden onset ("thunderclap") | SAH, dissection, venous thrombosis |
| **O**lder age | New onset after age 50 | Temporal arteritis, mass lesion |
| **P**rior history | Change in pattern, progressive worsening | Subdural hematoma, mass lesion |
| **P**osture dependent | Worse with Valsalva, standing, or lying | Chiari, mass lesion, CSF leak |
| **P**recipitated by exertion | Exercise, sexual activity | SAH, RCVS |
| **P**regnancy/postpartum | New headache in pregnancy | Preeclampsia, CVT, PRES |

**Imaging Indications:** New headache with abnormal neurologic examination, onset after age 50 with new headache, sudden onset thunderclap, headache with fever/meningismus, headache with known cancer or immunosuppression, progressive headache, post-traumatic headache with worsening [^6^].

**Sources:** [^1^] ICHD-3 Classification; [^2^] Medscape Migraine Guidelines 2024; [^3^] Australian Prescriber CGRP-Therapies 2025; [^4^] Medscape Cluster Headache 2025; [^5^] EAN Cluster Headache Guidelines 2023; [^6^] AAFP Acute Headache 2022

## 2. SEIZURES AND EPILEPSY

### 2.1 Clinical Overview

Epilepsy is defined as a disorder of the brain characterized by an enduring predisposition to generate epileptic seizures and by the neurobiologic, cognitive, psychological, and social consequences of this condition [^7^].

**ILAE 2017 Classification:**
1. **Focal onset:** Originating within networks limited to one hemisphere
   - With/without impaired awareness
   - Motor onset (tonic, clonic, atonic, myoclonic, hyperkinetic, automatisms)
   - Non-motor onset (sensory, emotional, autonomic, behavior arrest)
2. **Generalized onset:** Originating within and rapidly engaging bilaterally distributed networks
   - Motor (tonic-clonic, tonic, clonic, myoclonic, myoclonic-tonic-clonic, atonic)
   - Non-motor (absence — typical, atypical)
3. **Unknown onset:** Spasms, behavior arrest seizures [^7^]

### 2.2 First Seizure Workup

**History:** Characterize the event (witness description), prior unrecognized seizures, family history, febrile seizures, sleep deprivation, alcohol/drug use, medications, prior head trauma, CNS infection, developmental history.

**Investigations:**
- **EEG:** Within 24-48 hours increases yield; sleep-deprived EEG if initial normal
- **Brain MRI:** Preferred over CT; identify structural lesions (mesial temporal sclerosis, tumors, malformations, vascular lesions)
- **Laboratory:** CBC, CMP, Mg, glucose, LFTs, toxicology screen, pregnancy test
- **Lumbar puncture:** If fever/meningismus, immunocompromised, or concern for encephalitis
- **ECG:** To rule out arrhythmia causing syncope vs seizure

**Recurrence Risk:** ~40-50% over 2 years after first unprovoked seizure. Risk factors: abnormal EEG, prior brain injury, abnormal neuroimaging, nocturnal seizure, Todd's paralysis [^7^].

### 2.3 AED Selection by Seizure Type

| Seizure Type | First-Line Options | Second-Line |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Focal seizures** | Lamotrigine, levetiracetam, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine | Lacosamide, brivaracetam, zonisamide, topiramate, valproate |
| **Generalized tonic-clonic** | Valproate, lamotrigine, levetiracetam | Topiramate, zonisamide, perampanel |
| **Absence** | Ethosuximide, valproate, lamotrigine | Zonisamide, clonazepam |
| **Myoclonic** | Valproate, levetiracetam | Topiramate, zonisamide, clonazepam |
| **Juvenile myoclonic** | Valproate, lamotrigine, levetiracetam | Topiramate, zonisamide |

**Important Notes:**
- **Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenytoin:** May worsen absence and myoclonic seizures
- **Valproate:** Contraindicated in women of childbearing potential unless no alternative
- **Lamotrigine:** Slow titration required (risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome)
- **Levetiracetam:** Psychiatric side effects (irritability, aggression); renal dosing

### 2.4 Status Epilepticus (SE)

**Definition:** Seizure lasting >5 minutes, or recurrent seizures without recovery to baseline between attacks. Types: convulsive (tonic-clonic), non-convulsive, focal [^8^].

**Management Algorithm:**

| Phase | Timeframe | Treatment |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **Early/Pre-hospital** | 0-5 min | Position patient, protect airway, oxygen, check glucose, establish IV |
| **Established SE** | 5-20 min | **Benzodiazepines:** IV lorazepam 4 mg (repeat x1 in 5 min) OR IM midazolam 10 mg (preferred if no IV) OR buccal midazolam 10 mg [^8^][^9^] |
| **Refractory SE** | 20-40 min | **Second-line (ESETT trial — all equivalent):** Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg IV (max 4500 mg) over 10 min OR phenytoin 20 mg/kg at 50 mg/min OR valproate 40 mg/kg IV over 5 min [^9^] |
| **Refractory SE** | 40-60 min | **Third-line (anesthesia):** Propofol or midazolam infusion; continuous EEG monitoring |
| **Super-refractory** | >24h anesthesia | Ketamine, barbiturate coma, immunotherapy (if autoimmune), hypothermia, surgery |

**Key Points:**
- Do NOT delay benzodiazepines — time-dependent response
- ESETT trial showed levetiracetam, fosphenytoin, and valproate have similar efficacy and safety [^9^]
- Address underlying cause: electrolytes, infection, stroke, metabolic, trauma

### 2.5 Pregnancy and Epilepsy

**Key Recommendations (2024 AAN/AES/SMFM Guideline):** [^10^]
- **Preconception counseling:** Optimize seizure control; folic acid 0.4-5 mg daily beginning before conception
- **Preferred AEDs:** Lamotrigine, levetiracetam, oxcarbazepine — lowest risk of major congenital malformations
- **Avoid if possible:** Valproic acid (neural tube defects, neurodevelopmental impairment), phenobarbital, topiramate
- **Monotherapy preferred** over polytherapy whenever possible
- **Vitamin K:** 10 mg orally daily in last month of pregnancy for women on enzyme-inducing AEDs; 1 mg IM to newborn at birth
- **AED level monitoring:** Recommended for lamotrigine (levels decrease significantly in pregnancy)
- **Breastfeeding:** Generally encouraged; most AEDs compatible

**Teratogenic Risks by AED:**
- Valproate: ~10% major malformations; dose-dependent neurodevelopmental effects
- Carbamazepine: ~2-3% major malformations; neural tube defects
- Lamotrigine: ~2-3% (dose-dependent; >200 mg/day higher risk)
- Levetiracetam: ~2-2.5% (lowest risk)

**Sources:** [^7^] ILAE Classification 2025; [^8^] NCBI Modern Treatment of SE 2022; [^9^] NHS Scotland SE Guidelines 2024; [^10^] AAN/AES Pregnancy Guideline 2024

## 3. STROKE

### 3.1 Clinical Overview

Stroke is a clinical syndrome characterized by rapidly developing signs of focal (or global) disturbance of cerebral function lasting >24 hours or leading to death, with no apparent cause other than vascular origin [^11^].

### 3.2 Ischemic Stroke

**Definition:** Cerebral infarction due to arterial occlusion (thrombosis or embolism). Accounts for ~85% of all strokes.

**IV Thrombolysis (Updated 2024-2025 Guidelines):** [^11^][^12^]

| Parameter | Standard Window | Extended Window |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Time** | ≤4.5 hours from LKN | 4.5-24 hours (with imaging) |
| **Agent** | Tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg IV bolus OR Alteplase 0.9 mg/kg (10% bolus, 90% over 1h) | Same |
| **Eligibility** | Disabling deficit, age ≥18, measurable deficit | Advanced imaging (CTP/MRI) showing salvageable tissue |

**Tenecteplase preferred:** Class 1 recommendation; single bolus, easier administration, non-inferior outcomes [^12^].

**Contraindications to IV tPA:**
- Absolute: Active bleeding, intracranial hemorrhage, recent intracranial surgery, severe uncontrolled hypertension (>185/110 mmHg despite treatment), platelets <100,000, INR >1.7 on warfarin, prior stroke with diabetes within 3 months, major surgery/trauma within 14 days [^11^]
- Extended window (>3-4.5 hours): Age >80, NIHSS >25, combination of diabetes + prior stroke, any anticoagulant use [^11^]

**Mechanical Thrombectomy (2024 Updated Guidelines):** [^12^][^13^]

| Window | Criteria | Evidence |
|--------|----------|----------|
| **0-6 hours** | LVO (ICA, M1, basilar), age ≥18, NIHSS ≥6, ASPECTS ≥6, prestroke mRS 0-1 | Class 1, Level A |
| **6-24 hours** | DAWN/DEFUSE-3 criteria: Clinical-imaging mismatch | Class 1, Level A |
| **Large core (ASPECTS 3-5)** | Age <80, NIHSS ≥6, no significant mass effect | Class 1 (SELECT2, ANGEL-ASPECT) |
| **Basilar artery occlusion** | NIHSS ≥10, mild ischemic damage, within 24h | Class 1 (ATTENTION, BAOCHE) |

**Post-Thrombectomy BP Targets:**
- Pre-thrombectomy: SBP <185, DBP <110 for tPA candidates
- Post-recanalization: Avoid SBP <140 mmHg (class III harm from ENCHANTED2/MT)
- Goal: <180/105 for first 24 hours, then individualized

### 3.3 Hemorrhagic Stroke

**Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH):**
- Urgent CT to diagnose; reverse anticoagulation immediately
- BP management: SBP 140-160 mmHg (INTERACT-2, ATACH-2)
- Coagulopathy reversal: Vitamin K + PCC (warfarin); idarucizumab (dabigatran); andexanet alfa (Xa inhibitors)
- Neurosurgical consultation for cerebellar hemorrhage >3 cm, hematoma evacuation for large supratentorial bleeds with mass effect

**Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH):**
- CT head: >95% sensitive within 6 hours
- CT angiography to identify aneurysm
- Nimodipine 60 mg PO/NG q4h for 21 days (prevents vasospasm/DIND)
- Secure aneurysm: Coiling (endovascular) vs clipping (surgical) — coiling preferred for most ruptured aneurysms

### 3.4 TIA and ABCD2 Score

**Definition:** Transient episode of neurologic dysfunction caused by focal brain, spinal cord, or retinal ischemia without acute infarction. Symptoms resolve within 24 hours (most within 1 hour).

**ABCD2 Score:**

| Factor | Points |
|--------|--------|
| Age ≥60 years | 1 |
| BP ≥140/90 mmHg | 1 |
| Clinical features: Unilateral weakness | 2 |
| Speech disturbance without weakness | 1 |
| Duration: ≥60 minutes | 2 |
| Duration: 10-59 minutes | 1 |
| Diabetes | 1 |

**Risk Stratification:**
- 0-3 points: Low risk; outpatient workup within 48 hours
- 4-5 points: Moderate risk; consider hospitalization
- 6-7 points: High risk; hospitalization for urgent workup

**TIA Workup:**
- MRI with DWI (rule out silent infarcts)
- Vascular imaging: CTA head/neck or MRA
- Echocardiography
- ECG and 24-48h cardiac monitoring (rule out AF)
- Labs: Lipids, HbA1c, CBC, CMP

### 3.5 Secondary Prevention

| Modality | Recommendation |
|----------|---------------|
| **Antiplatelet** | Aspirin 81 mg daily (unless contraindicated); clopidogrel 75 mg if aspirin allergy |
| **Dual antiplatelet** | Aspirin + clopidogrel for 21-90 days (CHANCE, POINT trials) for minor stroke/TIA, then single agent |
| **Anticoagulation** | DOACs preferred over warfarin for AF-related stroke (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, edoxaban) |
| **Statins** | High-intensity (atorvastatin 40-80 mg or rosuvastatin 20-40 mg) regardless of baseline LDL |
| **BP control** | Goal <130/80 mmHg (per AHA/ACC guidelines) |
| **Diabetes** | Metformin; GLP-1 agonists or SGLT2-i may have added cerebrovascular benefit |
| **Lifestyle** | Mediterranean diet, exercise 150 min/week, weight loss, smoking cessation, alcohol moderation |
| **Carotid revascularization** | CEA or stenting for symptomatic 50-99% stenosis (within 2 weeks for high-grade) |

**Sources:** [^11^] AHA/ASA 2026 AIS Guideline; [^12^] TCTMD AHA/ASA Guideline Summary 2026; [^13^] Medscape Ischemic Stroke Guidelines 2025

## 4. DIZZINESS AND VERTIGO

### 4.1 Clinical Overview

Dizziness is a nonspecific term; accurate diagnosis requires distinguishing vertigo (illusion of movement), presyncope (lightheadedness), disequilibrium (imbalance), and nonspecific dizziness [^14^].

### 4.2 Peripheral vs Central Vertigo

| Feature | Peripheral (Vestibular) | Central (CNS) |
|---------|------------------------|---------------|
| **Onset** | Sudden, severe | Gradual or sudden |
| **Severity** | Intense | Mild-to-moderate |
| **Duration** | Episodic (seconds-days) | Continuous |
| **Direction** | Unidirectional nystagmus | Bidirectional, gaze-evoked |
| **Associated** | Hearing loss, tinnitus | Diplopia, dysarthria, weakness, ataxia |
| **Head impulse** | Abnormal (corrective saccade) | Normal |
| **Skew deviation** | Absent | May be present |

**HINTS Exam (for acute vestibular syndrome):** Head Impulse (abnormal = peripheral, normal = central), Nystagmus (direction-changing = central), Test of Skew (skew = central). HINTS has >99% sensitivity and >97% specificity for stroke — more sensitive than MRI in first 24-48 hours [^14^].

### 4.3 BPPV (Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo)

**Definition:** Most common cause of recurrent vertigo; brief episodes of spinning triggered by head position changes. Due to otoconia dislodged into semicircular canals [^14^][^15^].

**Diagnosis:** Dix-Hallpike maneuver — positive when: latency of several seconds, upbeating torsional nystagmus with fast phase toward affected ear, fatigability within 30-60 seconds [^15^].

**Treatment:**
- **Epley (Canalith Repositioning) Maneuver:** First-line; effective in 50-90%; can be self-administered; repeat 3 times/day until symptom-free for 24 hours [^14^][^15^]
- **Brandt-Daroff exercises:** Alternative if affected side unclear
- **Surgery:** Posterior canal occlusion or singular neurectomy for refractory cases
- **Medications:** NOT recommended as primary treatment; meclizine 25-100 mg may be used briefly for severe symptoms [^15^]

### 4.4 Vestibular Neuritis

**Definition:** Acute onset sustained vertigo due to inflammation of the vestibular nerve (likely viral), lasting days to weeks. No hearing loss (unlike labyrinthitis) [^14^].

**Management:**
- **Corticosteroids:** If initiated within 3 days of onset, may improve outcomes (methylprednisolone 100 mg or 500 mg IV x 5 days, then taper) [^14^]
- **Symptomatic:** Betahistine (Serc) 16 mg TID; antiemetics (ondansetron, prochlorperazine)
- **Vestibular rehabilitation:** Begin as early as possible; reduces symptoms and improves gait stability [^14^]

### 4.5 Meniere's Disease

**Definition:** Episodic vertigo (20 min - 12 hours) with fluctuating sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus, and aural fullness. Endolymphatic hydrops on histopathology [^14^].

**Management:**
- Dietary sodium restriction (<1.5-2 g/day); avoid caffeine, alcohol, tobacco
- Betahistine 16-48 mg/day (may reduce vertigo frequency)
- Diuretics (e.g., hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg)
- Intratympanic gentamicin or dexamethasone for refractory cases
- Surgery (endolymphatic sac decompression, vestibular nerve section, labyrinthectomy) for severe refractory disease

### 4.6 Posterior Circulation Stroke

**Critical Consideration:** Vertigo with ANY of the following suggests posterior circulation stroke: vertical nystagmus, skew deviation, severe gait ataxia (unable to sit/stand unassisted), new headache, diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, focal weakness, altered mental status [^14^].

**RED FLAGS requiring immediate imaging (CTA/CT):**
- Acute vertigo with new severe headache or neck pain
- Acute vertigo with any focal neurologic deficit
- Vertigo with inability to walk (severe gait ataxia)
- Acute vertigo in patient with vascular risk factors + HINTS exam suggesting central cause

**Sources:** [^14^] NCBI Vertigo in Clinical Practice 2025; [^15^] StatPearls BPPV 2025; Specialist Link Vertigo Pathway

## 5. PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHY

### 5.1 Clinical Overview

Peripheral neuropathy refers to disorders of the peripheral nervous system. Clinical presentation depends on fiber type involvement (large fiber: numbness, tingling, imbalance; small fiber: pain, burning, autonomic symptoms) [^16^].

**Pattern Recognition:**
- Symmetric distal "stocking-glove" pattern: Length-dependent (DM, B12, alcohol, toxins)
- Asymmetric/mononeuropathy: Focal compression (CTS), vasculitis, leprosy, HNPP
- Mononeuritis multiplex: Multiple non-contiguous nerves (vasculitis, DM, Lyme)
- Radiculopathy: Nerve root pattern (radiculopathy)

### 5.2 Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy (DPN)

**Definition:** Most common cause of peripheral neuropathy in developed countries; present in ~50% of patients with long-standing diabetes [^16^].

**Screening:**
- T2DM: Screen at diagnosis, then annually
- T1DM: Screen 5 years after diagnosis, then annually
- 10-g monofilament, vibration perception (128 Hz tuning fork), pinprick, ankle reflexes [^16^]

**Management (AAN/ADA Guidelines):** [^16^]

**Prevention:** Tight glucose control (most effective in T1DM); lifestyle modifications

**Symptomatic Treatment (Painful DPN):**

| Class | Agent | Dosing | Notes |
|-------|-------|--------|-------|
| **SNRIs** | Duloxetine | 30-120 mg daily | FDA-approved; start 30 mg |
| **Gabapentinoids** | Pregabalin | 25-600 mg/day | FDA-approved; titrate slowly |
| | Gabapentin | 300-3600 mg/day | Renal dosing required |
| **TCAs** | Amitriptyline | 10-100 mg qHS | Most evidence; anticholinergic SE |
| | Nortriptyline | 10-100 mg qHS | Better tolerated |
| **Topical** | Capsaicin 8% | One 60-min application q3mo | FDA-approved; apply in clinic |
| **Sodium channel blockers** | Carbamazepine | 200-1200 mg/day | AAN guideline-supported |

**Avoid:** Opioids (including tramadol, tapentadol ER) — not recommended due to lack of long-term efficacy data and risk of abuse/complications [^16^].

**Refractory Pain:** Spinal cord stimulation (FDA-approved); combination therapy from different classes; CBT, physical therapy [^16^].

### 5.3 B12 Deficiency Neuropathy

**Clinical Features:** Dorsal column involvement (paresthesias, loss of vibration/proprioception, sensory ataxia, Romberg sign), may include optic neuropathy and cognitive changes (megaloblastic madness).

**Diagnosis:** Serum B12 <200 pg/mL (with symptoms); elevated methylmalonic acid (MMA) and homocysteine; macrocytosis may be absent (30% have normal MCV).

**Treatment:** B12 1000 mcg IM daily x 1 week, then weekly x 1 month, then monthly indefinitely; or high-dose oral (1000-2000 mcg/day) if malabsorption not present.

**Important:** Metformin, PPIs, H2-blockers cause B12 deficiency; check in all patients with peripheral neuropathy.

### 5.4 Guillain-Barre Syndrome (GBS)

**Definition:** Acute inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy; ascending symmetric weakness, areflexia, albuminocytologic dissociation in CSF [^17^][^18^].

**Subtypes:**
- AIDP (acute inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy): Most common in North America/Europe
- AMAN (acute motor axonal neuropathy): Associated with Campylobacter jejuni; more common in Asia
- Miller Fisher syndrome: Ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, areflexia; anti-GQ1b antibody

**Diagnostic Criteria:** Progressive weakness of both legs and arms; areflexia; elevated CSF protein with cell count <10; nerve conduction studies showing demyelination.

**Treatment (Equally Effective):** [^17^][^18^]
- **IVIG:** 2 g/kg divided over 2-5 days — preferred (easier, fewer complications)
- **Plasma Exchange (PE):** 4-6 exchanges over 7-14 days
- **Corticosteroids:** NOT effective
- **Monitor:** FVC, MIP, MEP; ICU admission if FVC <15 mL/kg or rapid progression

**Prognosis:** Recovery begins 2-4 weeks after nadir; 80% walk independently at 6 months; 5-10% mortality.

### 5.5 Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy (CIDP)

**Definition:** Chronic counterpart of GBS; progressive or relapsing weakness for >8 weeks [^18^].

**Treatment:**
- IVIG: First-line; loading then maintenance doses
- Plasma exchange: Effective but temporary
- Corticosteroids: Prednisone or pulsed dexamethasone
- Steroid-sparing: Azathioprine, mycophenolate, rituximab

### 5.6 Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS)

**Definition:** Compression of median nerve at the wrist; most common entrapment neuropathy.

**Symptoms:** Numbness/tingling in thumb, index, middle, and radial half of ring finger; worse at night; thenar weakness in severe cases.

**Physical Exam:** Tinel sign (tapping over carpal tunnel), Phalen maneuver (wrist flexion for 60 seconds), thenar atrophy (late).

**Diagnosis:** Nerve conduction studies/EMG; ultrasound showing enlarged median nerve.

**Management:**
- Conservative: Wrist splinting at night (neutral position), activity modification, NSAIDs
- Injection: Corticosteroid injection into carpal tunnel
- Surgical: Carpal tunnel release (open or endoscopic) for failed conservative therapy or severe disease

**Sources:** [^16^] PMC Update on Painful DPN; [^17^] WHO GBS Fact Sheet; [^18^] NINDS GBS Information

## 6. COGNITIVE IMPAIRMENT

### 6.1 MCI vs Dementia

**Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI):**
- Objective cognitive decline in one or more domains
- Preserved functional independence
- Not meeting dementia criteria
- Annual progression rate to dementia: ~10-15%

**Dementia:**
- Cognitive deficits in ≥2 domains (memory, language, executive, visuospatial, behavior)
- Interferes with daily functioning and independence
- NOT due exclusively to delirium or psychiatric disorder

### 6.2 Delirium vs Dementia

| Feature | Delirium | Dementia |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Onset | Hours to days | Months to years |
| Course | Fluctuating | Progressive |
| Attention | Impaired (inattentive, easily distracted) | Normal (early); impaired (late) |
| Consciousness | Altered (hyper/hypoactive) | Clear until end-stage |
| Memory | Immediate/recent impaired | Recent > remote (early) |
| Hallucinations | Common (visual) | Uncommon (except LBD) |
| Reversibility | Often reversible | Irreversible |

**Delirium is a medical emergency.** Screen with CAM (Confusion Assessment Method) or 4AT. Common causes: infection, medications, metabolic, pain, urinary retention, constipation, sleep deprivation, sensory impairment [^19^][^20^].

### 6.3 Alzheimer's Disease (AD)

**Definition:** Most common cause of dementia (~60-70%); characterized by amyloid plaques and neurofibrillary tau tangles; insidious onset with progressive memory impairment [^21^].

**Clinical Features:**
- Episodic memory impairment (early)
- Language difficulties (word-finding)
- Executive dysfunction, impaired judgment
- Behavioral changes (apathy, depression, agitation in later stages)
- Preserved motor function until late

**Biomarkers:** Amyloid PET (decreased), CSF (low Aβ42, high p-tau, high t-tau), MRI (hippocampal/medial temporal atrophy), plasma p-tau217/p-tau181 (emerging)

**FDA-Approved Disease-Modifying Therapies (2024):** [^21^][^22^][^23^]

| Agent | Mechanism | Indication | Administration | Key Risks |
|-------|-----------|------------|---------------|-----------|
| **Lecanemab** (Leqembi) | Anti-amyloid mAb | Early AD (MCI/mild dementia) | 10 mg/kg IV q2 weeks | ARIA-E, ARIA-H (esp. APOE ε4 carriers); infusion reactions |
| **Donanemab** (Kisunla) | Anti-amyloid mAb (N3pG) | Early AD (MCI/mild dementia) | 700 mg → 1400 mg IV q4 weeks | ARIA-E, ARIA-H; infusion reactions; can stop when amyloid negative |

**ARIA (Amyloid-Related Imaging Abnormalities):** Radiographic finding of edema (ARIA-E) or hemosiderin deposition (ARIA-H); more common in APOE ε4 carriers; requires MRI monitoring.

**Symptomatic Treatments:**
- **Cholinesterase inhibitors:** Donepezil 5-10 mg daily; rivastigmine 3-12 mg/day; galantamine 8-24 mg/day — modest benefit in cognition/function; common side effects: GI (nausea, diarrhea), bradycardia
- **Memantine:** 5-20 mg/day (NMDA antagonist) — moderate-to-severe AD; may be combined with cholinesterase inhibitor

**Important Considerations:**
- These therapies slow decline by ~25-35% over 18 months — they do NOT reverse or cure AD
- Requires confirmed amyloid pathology (PET or CSF) before initiation
- MRI monitoring required (baseline, before infusions 5, 9, 13, then every 6 months or with symptoms)
- APOE ε4 genotyping recommended; homozygotes at highest ARIA risk [^21^][^22^]

### 6.4 Vascular Dementia

**Features:** Stepwise decline, prominent executive dysfunction, gait disturbance ("marche a petits pas"), urinary urgency/incontinence, pseudobulbar palsy (emotional lability), history of strokes/TIAs. MRI shows significant white matter disease and/or lacunar infarcts.

**Management:** Aggressive vascular risk factor modification (BP, lipids, diabetes, smoking).

### 6.5 Lewy Body Dementia (LBD)

**Core Clinical Features (must have 2 for probable, 1 for possible):**
- Fluctuating cognition with pronounced variations in attention/alertness
- Recurrent visual hallucinations (well-formed, detailed)
- REM sleep behavior disorder
- Parkinsonism (spontaneous, after cognitive decline)

**Suggestive Features:** Severe neuroleptic sensitivity, postural instability, syncope, autonomic dysfunction (orthostatic hypotension, constipation), hyposmia.

**CRITICAL:** Avoid antipsychotics (severe sensitivity; may cause neuroleptic malignant syndrome); if absolutely necessary, use quetiapine at lowest dose or pimavanserin.

### 6.6 Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD)

**Variants:**
- **Behavioral variant:** Personality changes, disinhibition, apathy, loss of empathy, compulsive behaviors, hyperorality; MRI shows frontal/anterior temporal atrophy
- **Nonfluent/agrammatic variant PPA:** Effortful speech, agrammatism
- **Semantic variant PPA:** Loss of word meaning, impaired naming with preserved fluency

**Treatment:** No disease-modifying therapy; supportive care; SSRIs may help behavioral symptoms; avoid cholinesterase inhibitors (generally not beneficial and may worsen behavior).

### 6.7 Prevention

- Physical exercise (most robust evidence)
- Cognitive engagement and social activity
- Control vascular risk factors (BP, diabetes, lipids)
- Mediterranean diet, DASH or MIND diet
- Adequate sleep, treat sleep apnea
- Hearing aids for hearing loss
- Limit alcohol, avoid smoking
- Manage depression

**Sources:** [^19^] Mayo Clinic Delirium; [^20^] NHS Inform Delirium; [^21^] FDA Donanemab Approval 2024; [^22^] JAMA FDA Green-Lights Donanemab 2024; [^23^] FDA Briefing Document Donanemab 2024

## 1. DEPRESSION

### 1.1 Clinical Overview

Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is characterized by ≥5 of the following for ≥2 weeks (including at least one of depressed mood or anhedonia): depressed mood, anhedonia, weight/appetite change, insomnia/hypersomnia, psychomotor agitation/retardation, fatigue, worthlessness/guilt, impaired concentration, suicidal ideation.

### 1.2 Screening: PHQ-9

The Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) is the most widely used depression screening tool in primary care.

| Score | Interpretation | Action |
|-------|---------------|--------|
| 1-4 | Minimal depression | Monitor |
| 5-9 | Mild depression | Watchful waiting; consider counseling |
| 10-14 | Moderate depression | Initiate treatment (medication ± therapy) |
| 15-19 | Moderately severe | Initiate treatment; consider referral |
| 20-27 | Severe depression | Active treatment; referral strongly recommended |

**Question 9 (suicidal ideation):** Any score >0 requires clinical assessment of suicidal risk.

### 1.3 Pharmacologic Treatment

**First-Line: SSRIs and SNRIs**

| Class | Agent | Starting Dose | Therapeutic Range | Notes |
|-------|-------|--------------|-------------------|-------|
| **SSRIs** | Sertraline | 25-50 mg | 50-200 mg | Favorable profile; less sedation |
| | Escitalopram | 10 mg | 10-20 mg | Most selective; good tolerability |
| | Fluoxetine | 20 mg | 20-80 mg | Long half-life; activating; CYP interactions |
| | Citalopram | 20 mg | 20-40 mg | QTc prolongation risk (max 40 mg) |
| | Paroxetine | 20 mg | 20-60 mg | Most anticholinergic; weight gain; avoid in elderly |
| **SNRIs** | Venlafaxine | 37.5-75 mg | 75-375 mg | HTN at higher doses; withdrawal syndrome |
| | Duloxetine | 30-60 mg | 60-120 mg | Also indicated for neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia |
| | Desvenlafaxine | 50 mg | 50-100 mg | Active metabolite of venlafaxine |

**Treatment Principles:**
- Start at half normal dose in elderly and anxiety patients (to minimize activation)
- Titrate every 2-4 weeks based on response and tolerability
- Adequate trial: 4-6 weeks at therapeutic dose
- Continue at full therapeutic dose for 6-12 months after remission (first episode); longer for recurrent episodes
- Taper gradually to avoid discontinuation syndrome (especially paroxetine, venlafaxine)

**Second-Line/Augmentation:**
- Bupropion XL 150-450 mg (add if SSRI causes sexual dysfunction, fatigue, weight gain; activating; avoid in anxiety, seizure disorders, eating disorders)
- Mirtazapine 7.5-45 mg qHS (good for insomnia, appetite loss, anxiety; weight gain)
- Atypical antipsychotics: Aripiprazole 2-15 mg, quetiapine XR 150-300 mg (augmentation for TRD)
- Lithium (augmentation; target 0.6-1.0 mEq/L)
- Thyroid hormone (T3 25-50 mcg)

### 1.4 Treatment-Resistant Depression (TRD)

**Definition:** Failure to respond to ≥2 adequate antidepressant trials of different classes.

**Stepwise Approach:** [^24^]

| Step | Intervention |
|------|-------------|
| 1 | Optimize current treatment (dose, duration, adherence) |
| 2 | Switch antidepressant (different mechanism: SSRI → SNRI, bupropion, mirtazapine) |
| 3 | Augmentation (lithium, aripiprazole, thyroid hormone) |
| 4 | Combination therapy (two antidepressants from different classes) |
| 5 | Neuromodulation: ECT (gold standard for severe/suicidal), rTMS |
| 6 | Novel agents: Esketamine intranasal (Spravato) — approved for TRD; requires REMS monitoring; ketamine IV off-label; dextromethorphan-bupropion (Auvelity) |

### 1.5 Suicide Risk Assessment

**Risk Factors (SAD PERSONS):** Sex (male), Age (15-24, >65), Depression, Previous attempt, Ethanol/substance abuse, Rational thinking loss (psychosis), Social support lacking, Organized plan, No spouse, Sickness/chronic illness

**Protective Factors:** Strong therapeutic alliance, family/children, religious/spiritual beliefs, pregnancy, future orientation, responsibility for others, fear of death

**Management of Active Suicidal Ideation:**
- Safety assessment (lethal means, intent, plan, access)
- Reduce access to lethal means (firearms, medications)
- Do NOT leave patient alone
- Consider involuntary hospitalization if imminent risk
- Psychiatric emergency services if active plan with intent

### 1.6 Perinatal Depression

**Screening:** All pregnant and postpartum patients using EPDS (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale) or PHQ-9 at least once during pregnancy and postpartum.

**Treatment:**
- **Psychotherapy:** CBT, IPT — first-line for mild-to-moderate
- **SSRIs:** Sertraline preferred (lowest breast milk levels); paroxetine for preconception/pregnancy; avoid fluoxetine if breastfeeding
- **Other:** Bupropion (lower milk levels); avoid benzodiazepines
- **Risks of untreated depression:** Preterm birth, low birth weight, impaired maternal-infant bonding, developmental effects

**Sources:** [^24^] PMC Clinical Practice Guideline Depression India; PHQ-9 Depression Screening Tools; APA Treatment Guidelines

## 2. ANXIETY DISORDERS

### 2.1 Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)

**Diagnosis:** Excessive anxiety/worry occurring more days than not for ≥6 months, difficult to control, with ≥3: restlessness, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance.

**Screening:** GAD-7 questionnaire; score ≥10 indicates clinically significant anxiety.

**Treatment:** [^25^][^26^]

| Severity | First-Line |
|----------|-----------|
| Mild | CBT, self-help, watchful waiting |
| Moderate | CBT or SSRI/SNRI |
| Severe | SSRI/SNRI + CBT |

**SSRIs for GAD:** Sertraline 25-200 mg; escitalopram 10-20 mg; fluoxetine 20-80 mg
**SNRIs:** Venlafaxine XR 75-225 mg; duloxetine 60-120 mg
**Pregabalin:** 75-300 mg BID (Schedule V controlled substance; risk of abuse)

**NOT recommended:** Benzodiazepines (except short-term crisis), beta-blockers, antipsychotics [^25^][^26^].

**Duration:** Treat for at least 12 months after response; high relapse rate if discontinued early.

### 2.2 Panic Disorder

**Diagnosis:** Recurrent unexpected panic attacks with ≥1 month of worry about additional attacks or maladaptive behavior change.

**Panic Attack Symptoms:** Palpitations, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, choking, chest pain, nausea, dizziness, derealization, fear of losing control, fear of dying, paresthesias, chills/hot flashes.

**Treatment:** [^25^][^26^]
- First-line: SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine, fluoxetine, escitalopram) or venlafaxine XR
- CBT (panic-focused): Psychoeducation, interoceptive exposure, cognitive restructuring — as effective as medication, more durable
- Combination: Best outcomes for severe panic disorder
- Acute attacks: Benzodiazepines short-term only (avoid long-term)
- Continue treatment ≥6-12 months after remission

### 2.3 Social Anxiety Disorder (SAD)

**Diagnosis:** Marked fear/anxiety about social situations involving possible scrutiny; fear of acting in a way that will be negatively evaluated; avoidance or endured with intense anxiety for ≥6 months.

**Treatment:** [^26^]
- First-line: CBT (exposure-based) or SSRI/SNRI
- Sertraline, paroxetine, venlafaxine XR — all FDA-approved
- Gabapentin/pregabalin may be helpful
- Beta-blockers: Useful for performance anxiety only (propranolol 10-40 mg 30-60 min before event)
- Continue treatment ≥12 months after response

### 2.4 Specific Phobias

**Treatment:** CBT with systematic desensitization and graded exposure — treatment of choice; SSRIs generally not effective as monotherapy.

### 2.5 Key Principles Across Anxiety Disorders

- Start SSRIs at half dose (to minimize initial activation/jitteriness)
- Therapeutic effect may take 4-8 weeks (longer than for depression)
- Warn patients about initial increase in anxiety for first 1-2 weeks
- CBT skills are durable and prevent relapse
- Avoid benzodiazepines for long-term management

**Sources:** [^25^] NICE CG113 GAD/Panic Guidelines; [^26^] RANZCP Anxiety Guidelines; PMC Clinical Practice Guidelines Anxiety Disorders

## 3. BIPOLAR DISORDER

### 3.1 Clinical Overview

Bipolar disorder is characterized by episodes of mania/hypomania alternating with depression. Lifetime prevalence ~1-2% for bipolar I, ~0.5-1% for bipolar II [^27^].

**Mania vs Hypomania:**

| Feature | Mania | Hypomania |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| Duration | ≥7 days (or any if hospitalized) | ≥4 days |
| Severity | Severe functional impairment | Noticeable change, but functioning maintained |
| Psychosis | May be present | Absent |
| Hospitalization | May be required | Not required by definition |

**Diagnostic Criteria (Mania):** Elevated, expansive, or irritable mood + increased energy/activity + ≥3 (4 if mood is only irritable): inflated self-esteem, decreased sleep need, pressured speech, flight of ideas, distractibility, increased goal-directed activity, excessive risky behavior.

### 3.2 Acute Mania Management

- **Hospitalization:** If severe, psychotic features, suicidal/homicidal, unable to care for self, substance use
- **First-line:** Atypical antipsychotic (olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine, aripiprazole, ziprasidone) OR valproate OR lithium
- **Severe:** Combination therapy (lithium or valproate + antipsychotic)
- **Benzodiazepines:** Short-term for agitation, insomnia (lorazepam, clonazepam)
- **Avoid antidepressants:** May worsen mania

### 3.3 Bipolar Depression

- **First-line:** Quetiapine (monotherapy); olanzapine-fluoxetine combination (OFC); lurasidone (with lithium or valproate)
- **Lamotrigine:** Effective for preventing depressive episodes; slow titration required (rash risk)
- **Lithium:** Gold standard for maintenance; may reduce suicide risk
- **Caution with antidepressants:** Risk of mood switch; only with mood stabilizer, for non-rapid cycling patients

### 3.4 Maintenance Therapy

| Agent | Indication | Monitoring |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **Lithium** | First-line maintenance; anti-suicidal | Serum levels (0.6-1.0 mEq/L), renal function, thyroid (TSH), calcium, weight |
| **Valproate** | Alternative to lithium; rapid cycling | LFTs, platelets, weight, ammonia; teratogenic |
| **Lamotrigine** | Bipolar depression prevention | Slow titration (SJS risk); no blood levels needed |
| **Atypical antipsychotics** | Aripiprazole, olanzapine, quetiapine, risperidone | Metabolic monitoring (weight, glucose, lipids) |

**Rapid Cycling (≥4 episodes/year):**
- Discontinue antidepressants
- Consider lithium + valproate combination
- Lamotrigine + mood stabilizer
- Check thyroid function; treat hypothyroidism

### 3.5 Physical Health Monitoring

All patients on maintenance therapy require annual monitoring: fasting glucose, lipid profile, BP, weight/BMI, smoking status, alcohol use [^27^].

**Sources:** [^27^] NHS Lanarkshire Bipolar Guidelines 2024; NICE Bipolar Guidelines

## 4. PTSD (POST-TRAUMATIC STRESS DISORDER)

### 4.1 Diagnostic Criteria

Exposure to actual/threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence + intrusion symptoms (flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories) + avoidance + negative alterations in cognition/mood + marked alterations in arousal/reactivity; duration >1 month; causes significant distress/impairment [^28^][^29^].

### 4.2 Treatment

**First-Line (VA/DoD, APA Guidelines):** [^28^][^29^]

**Trauma-Focused Psychotherapy (preferred when available):**
- Prolonged Exposure (PE)
- Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT)
- Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR)
- Trauma-focused CBT

**Pharmacotherapy (alternative or adjunct):**
- SSRIs: Sertraline 50-200 mg; paroxetine 20-60 mg; fluoxetine 20-60 mg — only FDA-approved medications for PTSD
- SNRI: Venlafaxine XR 75-300 mg
- Continue for ≥6-12 months after response

### 4.3 Prazosin for Nightmares

**Role:** Alpha-1 adrenergic antagonist; reduces PTSD-related nightmares and sleep disturbance [^28^][^29^][^30^].

**Dosing:** Start 1 mg at bedtime; titrate by 1 mg every 2 weeks to 3-15 mg based on response [^28^].

**Evidence:** Mixed results — initial trials positive; larger VA Cooperative Study (N=304) negative. Current VA/DoD guidelines: insufficient evidence to recommend for or against [^29^]. AASM continues to recommend as first-line option [^29^].

**Clinical approach:** Trial may be reasonable; discontinue if no benefit after 4-8 weeks at therapeutic dose.

### 4.4 Additional Considerations

- **Benzodiazepines:** Contraindicated — may interfere with fear extinction, worsen outcomes, high abuse potential [^29^]
- **Sleep disturbance:** Prazosin, trazodone, mirtazapine, CBT-I
- **Avoid:** Cannabis, alcohol as self-medication
- **Duration:** Treat for ≥12 months; high relapse rate

**Sources:** [^28^] UpToDate PTSD Treatment; [^29^] PMC Treatment of PTSD 2019; [^30^] PMC Prazosin Systematic Review 2021; NHS Highland Prazosin Guidelines

## 5. SUBSTANCE USE DISORDERS

### 5.1 Alcohol Use Disorder (AUD)

**Screening:** AUDIT-C (3-item); AUDIT (10-item, score ≥8 indicates hazardous drinking); single-item screening: "How many times in the past year have you had ≥5 (men) or ≥4 (women) drinks in a day?"

**Diagnosis:** DSM-5 criteria (mild: 2-3, moderate: 4-5, severe: ≥6 criteria)

**Treatment:**
- Brief intervention (motivational interviewing)
- Pharmacotherapy: Naltrexone 50 mg daily (reduces cravings; opioid-dependent patients must be opioid-free 7-10 days), acamprosate 666 mg TID (maintains abstinence), disulfiram 250-500 mg daily (aversive therapy; hepatotoxic)
- Psychosocial support: AA, SMART Recovery, CBT, contingency management

### 5.2 Opioid Use Disorder (OUD)

**2024 Updated Guidelines:** [^31^][^32^][^33^]

**First-Line Medications for OUD (MOUD):**

| Medication | Mechanism | Key Features |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Buprenorphine** | Partial μ-agonist | Can prescribe in office (DEA registration); lower overdose risk; ceiling effect on respiratory depression |
| **Methadone** | Full μ-agonist | OTP (opioid treatment program) required; higher retention; overdose risk in first month |
| **Naltrexone XR** | μ-antagonist | Monthly IM injection; requires 7-10 days opioid-free; prevents relapse |

**Key Recommendations:**
- Buprenorphine and methadone are BOTH first-line options (2024 update — methadone elevated to equal status) [^31^]
- Both reduce mortality by ~50% compared to no treatment
- Psychosocial treatment should be offered as adjunct but should NOT be mandatory [^31^]
- Withdrawal management alone is NOT recommended (high relapse/overdose risk)
- Harm reduction: Naloxone distribution, syringe services, fentanyl test strips [^32^][^33^]

**Buprenorphine Induction:**
- Start when patient in moderate withdrawal (COWS ≥8)
- Initial dose: 2-4 mg SL; may repeat in 1-2 hours; total day 1: 4-8 mg
- Target maintenance: 8-16 mg/day (max 24 mg/day)
- Warn about precipitated withdrawal if inducted too early

### 5.3 Tobacco Cessation

**2024 WHO Guideline:** [^34^][^35^]

**First-Line Pharmacotherapy:**
- **Varenicline:** Most effective (NNT ~8); partial agonist; 1 mg BID for 12 weeks; start 1 week before quit date
- **Combination NRT:** Patch (long-acting) + gum/lozenge (short-acting) — most effective NRT approach
- **Bupropion SR:** 150 mg daily x 3 days, then 150 mg BID; start 1-2 weeks before quit date; contraindicated in seizure disorders, eating disorders
- **Cytisine:** Plant-derived partial agonist; moderate certainty evidence

**All medications:** More effective with behavioral support; combining pharmacotherapy with counseling increases quit rates 2-3x [^34^][^35^].

**E-cigarettes:** Not recommended as first-line; may be less harmful than combustible cigarettes but not harmless.

**Sources:** [^31^] PMC OUD Management 2024 Update; [^32^] WHO Opioid Dependence Guidelines 2025; [^33^] CDC OUD Treatment 2024; [^34^] WHO Tobacco Cessation Guideline 2024; [^35^] NCBI WHO Tobacco Cessation

## 6. SLEEP DISORDERS

### 6.1 Insomnia

**Definition:** Difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep despite adequate opportunity/circumstances, with daytime impairment, ≥3 nights/week for ≥3 months [^36^][^37^].

**Treatment (AASM Guidelines):** [^36^][^37^][^38^]

**CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia):** First-line, strongly recommended [^37^]
- Stimulus control therapy
- Sleep restriction therapy
- Cognitive restructuring
- Relaxation training
- Sleep hygiene education (NOT effective as standalone)

**Pharmacotherapy (when CBT-I unavailable or insufficient):**

| Class | Agent | Indication | Notes |
|-------|-------|-----------|-------|
| **Z-drugs** | Zolpidem 5-10 mg | Sleep onset | Complex sleep behaviors (black box); avoid in elderly |
| | Eszopiclone 1-3 mg | Onset + maintenance | Metallic taste; avoid in elderly |
| **Benzodiazepines** | Temazepam 7.5-30 mg | Onset + maintenance | Avoid in elderly; tolerance/dependence |
| **Orexin antagonists** | Suvorexant 10-20 mg | Onset + maintenance | No tolerance; may cause next-day somnolence |
| | Lemborexant 5-10 mg | Onset + maintenance | Orexin receptor antagonist |
| | Daridorexant 25-50 mg | Onset + maintenance | Dual orexin antagonist |
| | Doxepin 3-6 mg | Sleep maintenance | Low-dose; anticholinergic at higher doses |
| **Melatonin** | 1-5 mg | Circadian rhythm, jet lag | Not strongly recommended by AASM for chronic insomnia |
| **Ramelteon** | 8 mg | Sleep onset | Melatonin receptor agonist; no abuse potential |

**Combination Therapy:** AASM suggests CBT-I + medication over medication alone; CBT-I alone preferred over combination [^36^][^38^].

**Avoid:** Trazodone, diphenhydramine, doxylamine (insufficient evidence, anticholinergic), benzodiazepines in elderly [^37^].

### 6.2 Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA)

**Screening:** STOP-BANG Questionnaire [^39^]
- **STOP:** Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnea, Pressure (BP)
- **BANG:** BMI >35, Age >50, Neck circumference >16 inches, Gender (male)
- Score ≥3: High risk for OSA; ≥5-6: Very high risk

**Diagnosis:** Polysomnography (gold standard); home sleep apnea testing for uncomplicated cases [^39^].

**Treatment:** [^39^]
- **CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure):** First-line for moderate-severe OSA; most effective treatment
- **Oral appliances:** Mandibular advancement devices for mild-moderate OSA or CPAP-intolerant
- **Weight loss:** ≥10% weight loss can significantly reduce AHI
- **Positional therapy:** For positional OSA
- **Myofunctional therapy:** Upper airway exercises
- **Surgery:** UPPP, maxillomandibular advancement, hypoglossal nerve stimulation for refractory cases [^39^]

**CPAP adherence:** ≥4 hours/night for ≥70% of nights; support and close follow-up improve adherence.

### 6.3 Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS)

**2024 AASM Updated Guidelines:** [^40^][^41^]

**Diagnostic Criteria (URGE):** Urge to move legs; Rest-induced; Gets better with movement; Evening/night worsening.

**First-Line Treatment (NEW — dopaminergic agents no longer recommended as first-line):** [^40^][^41^]
1. **Gabapentinoids (strong recommendation):**
   - Gabapentin enacarbil 600-1200 mg evening
   - Pregabalin 75-450 mg/day
   - Gabapentin 300-2400 mg/day
2. **IV Iron:** If ferritin <75-100 μg/L — IV ferric carboxymaltose strongly recommended; rule out iron deficiency [^41^]

**Avoid/Address First:** Alcohol, caffeine, antihistamines, serotonergic medications, untreated OSA [^40^].

**Pregnancy:** RLS common in pregnancy; consider safety profile; iron repletion if deficient.

### 6.4 Narcolepsy

**Symptoms:** Excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy (sudden loss of muscle tone with emotions), sleep paralysis, hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations, disrupted nocturnal sleep.

**Treatment:**
- **Excessive daytime sleepiness:** Modafinil 100-400 mg; armodafinil 150-250 mg; pitolisant (H3 antagonist); solriamfetol (DNRI); methylphenidate
- **Cataplexy:** Sodium oxybate (sodium oxybate is also FDA-approved for EDS); venlafaxine; pitolisant
- **Support:** Scheduled naps (15-20 minutes), good sleep hygiene, safety (driving restrictions)

**Sources:** [^36^] PMC Combination Treatment Insomnia 2025; [^37^] Medscape Insomnia 2025; [^38^] AASM Combination Therapy Guideline; [^39^] Mayo Clinic Sleep Apnea; [^40^] PubMed RLS AASM Guideline 2025; [^41^] Chest Physician RLS Guidelines 2025

## 7. EATING DISORDERS

### 7.1 Anorexia Nervosa (AN)

**Diagnostic Criteria:** Restriction of energy intake leading to significantly low body weight, intense fear of gaining weight, disturbance in self-perceived weight/shape, amenorrhea (DSM-5 no longer required).

**Medical Complications:** Bradycardia, hypotension, orthostasis, electrolyte abnormalities (hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia, hypomagnesemia), QTc prolongation, osteopenia/osteoporosis, refeeding syndrome, leukopenia, elevated LFTs, constipation, hypothyroidism.

**REFeeding Syndrome Risk:** Start nutrition slowly (1000-1200 kcal/day, increase by 200-300 kcal every 3-5 days); monitor phosphorus, potassium, magnesium daily for first week; supplement prophylactically.

**Treatment:** [^42^][^43^]
- Outpatient: Eating-disorder-focused CBT, family-based treatment (FBT) for adolescents, Maudsley Anorexia Nervosa Treatment for Adults (MANTRA), SSCM (Specialist Supportive Clinical Management)
- Inpatient: BMI <13, rapid weight loss, hemodynamic instability, ECG abnormalities, electrolyte disturbances, suicidality, failed outpatient treatment
- **Medication:** Generally NOT effective for weight restoration; may treat comorbid depression/anxiety
- **Monitor:** Weight, vitals (orthostatics), ECG, electrolytes, bone density

### 7.2 Bulimia Nervosa (BN)

**Diagnostic Criteria:** Recurrent episodes of binge eating + inappropriate compensatory behaviors (vomiting, laxatives, diuretics, fasting, excessive exercise) ≥1x/week for 3 months; self-evaluation unduly influenced by body shape/weight.

**Medical Complications:** Electrolyte disturbances (hypokalemia, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis from vomiting), dental erosion, parotid enlargement, Russell sign (knuckle calluses), esophageal tears (Mallory-Weiss), arrhythmias.

**Treatment:** [^42^][^43^]
- **CBT-BN:** First-line; 16-20 sessions over 4-5 months; most effective for reducing binge/purge behaviors [^42^]
- **Guided self-help:** CBT-based self-help with brief supportive sessions
- **Fluoxetine:** Only FDA-approved medication for BN; 60 mg daily (higher than depression dose); reduces binge/purge frequency [^42^]
- **Interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT):** Alternative to CBT; slower onset but comparable at 12 months

### 7.3 Binge Eating Disorder (BED)

**Diagnostic Criteria:** Recurrent episodes of binge eating (loss of control, larger than normal amount) ≥1x/week for 3 months; NO regular compensatory behaviors; associated with distress.

**Treatment:** [^42^][^43^]
- **CBT-BED:** First-line; individual or group format; 16-20 sessions [^43^]
- **Guided self-help:** CBT-based materials with brief supportive sessions
- **Lisdexamfetamine:** FDA-approved for moderate-severe BED; 50-70 mg daily
- **Topiramate:** 100-400 mg/day; effective but cognitive side effects, weight loss
- **SSRIs:** May reduce binge frequency; less robust evidence
- **Note:** Psychological treatments have limited effect on body weight; address obesity separately [^43^]

**Sources:** [^42^] NICE Eating Disorders NG69; [^43^] NCBI NICE Eating Disorders Book

## 8. ADHD (ATTENTION-DEFICIT/HYPERACTIVITY DISORDER)

### 8.1 Adult ADHD Assessment

**Diagnosis:** Requires ≥5 symptoms of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity (compared to ≥6 in children) present before age 12, in ≥2 settings, causing functional impairment. Symptoms: inattention (careless mistakes, difficulty sustaining attention, poor organization, avoiding mental effort, losing things, easily distracted, forgetful), hyperactivity-impulsivity (fidgeting, leaving seat, running/climbing, unable to play quietly, on the go, talking excessively, blurting out, difficulty waiting turn, interrupting) [^44^][^45^].

**Assessment Tools:** Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS-v1.1); DIVA-5 diagnostic interview; collateral history from family/partner; school records if available.

**Differential:** Anxiety, depression, bipolar disorder, substance use, sleep disorders, thyroid dysfunction, autism spectrum disorder, personality disorders.

### 8.2 Pharmacologic Treatment

**First-Line: Stimulants** [^44^][^45^][^46^]

| Agent | Starting Dose | Typical Range | Notes |
|-------|--------------|---------------|-------|
| **Methylphenidate IR** | 5 mg BID-TID | 10-60 mg/day | Short-acting; multiple doses |
| **Methylphenidate ER** | 18-36 mg daily | 18-72 mg daily | Concerta, Ritalin LA |
| **Lisdexamfetamine** | 30 mg daily | 30-70 mg daily | Prodrug; lower abuse potential; once daily |
| **Amphetamine/Dextroamphetamine** | 5 mg BID | 5-40 mg/day | Mixed salts (Adderall) |
| **Dexmethylphenidate** | 5 mg BID | 5-20 mg/day | Focalin |

**Second-Line: Non-Stimulants** [^44^][^45^][^46^]

| Agent | Dosing | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| **Atomoxetine** | 40 mg → 80-100 mg daily | SNRI; takes 2-4 weeks; once daily; no abuse potential |
| **Guanfacine ER** | 1-4 mg daily | Alpha-2A agonist; may help with emotional dysregulation |
| **Clonidine ER** | 0.1-0.4 mg daily | Similar to guanfacine |
| **Bupropion** | 150-450 mg/day | Off-label; may help if comorbid depression |

**Treatment Principles:**
- Stimulants effective in ~70% of patients; trial both methylphenidate and amphetamine classes if needed
- Start low, titrate every 1-2 weeks based on response and side effects
- Monitor BP, HR, weight; assess for misuse/diversion
- Assess benefit vs. risk in patients with substance use disorders
- Cardiovascular screening: History of sudden cardiac death, structural heart disease, arrhythmias; ECG if indicated [^44^]

**Special Populations:**
- **Pregnancy:** Generally avoid stimulants; if needed, lowest effective dose
- **Elderly:** Start lower doses; monitor for cardiovascular effects
- **Substance use:** Non-stimulants preferred; if stimulant needed, lisdexamfetamine (lower abuse potential) with close monitoring [^44^]

### 8.3 Non-Pharmacologic Treatment

- CBT adapted for ADHD (organization, time management, planning skills)
- Coaching and skills training
- Workplace/school accommodations
- Exercise
- Mindfulness-based interventions
- Support groups
