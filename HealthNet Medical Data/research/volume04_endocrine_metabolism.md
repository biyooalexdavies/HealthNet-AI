# 1. Diabetes Mellitus - Type 1

## 1.1 Clinical Overview

Type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) is an autoimmune disease characterized by the destruction of pancreatic beta cells, resulting in absolute insulin deficiency. It accounts for approximately 5-10% of all diabetes cases. The pathophysiology involves T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on insulin-producing beta cells, often triggered by genetic and environmental factors in susceptible individuals (HLA-DR3/DR4, DQ2/DQ8). [^1^]

**Key diagnostic features:**
- Hyperglycemia with classic symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss)
- Low/absent C-peptide levels
- Presence of autoantibodies (GAD65, IA-2, ZnT8, insulin autoantibodies, IAA)
- Typically presents in childhood/adolescence but can occur at any age (LADA)

## 1.2 Guidelines & Evidence

### ADA Standards of Care 2024 [^2^]
- **CGM Recommendation (7.16):** rtCGM (A) or isCGM (E) should be offered for youth with T1DM on MDI or CSII capable of using devices safely
- **AID Recommendation (7.27):** Automated insulin delivery systems should be offered to all youth and adults with T1DM (A)
- **Early CGM initiation (7.2):** CGM should be offered early in disease, even at time of diagnosis (A) - **NEW 2024**
- **Early pump/AID initiation (7.8):** CSII and/or AID early, even at diagnosis, can be beneficial (C) - **NEW 2024**

### Classification [^3^]
- **DKA Severity:**
  - Mild: beta-hydroxybutyrate <= 6 mmol/L, pH > 7.25, bicarbonate >= 15 mmol/L, normal mental status
  - Moderate: beta-hydroxybutyrate <= 6 mmol/L, pH 7.0-7.25, bicarbonate 10 to <15 mmol/L
  - Severe: beta-hydroxybutyrate > 6 mmol/L, pH < 7.0, bicarbonate < 10 mmol/L, stupor or coma

## 1.3 Insulin Therapy

### Basal-Bolus Therapy
- **Basal insulin:** Long-acting analogs (glargine, detemir, degludec) provide 24-hour background insulin
- **Bolus insulin:** Rapid-acting analogs (lispro, aspart, glulisine, faster aspart, ultra-rapid lispro) for meal coverage
- Starting total daily insulin (TDI): 0.5-0.6 units/kg/day (adolescents may need 0.8-1.0 units/kg/day)
- Basal:bolus ratio typically 40-50% basal, 50-60% bolus

### Insulin Pump (CSII) [^2^]
- Continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion
- Offers flexible basal rates, precise bolus dosing
- Compatible with CGM for sensor-augmented pump therapy
- 2024 ADA recommends early initiation, even at diagnosis (7.8)

### Automated Insulin Delivery (AID) [^2^]
- Hybrid closed-loop systems combine CGM + pump + algorithm
- 2024 ADA: AID should be offered to all T1DM patients capable of safe use (A)
- Reduces hypoglycemia and improves TIR (Time in Range 70-180 mg/dL)

## 1.4 CGM Technology

### Key Metrics (2024 ADA) [^2^]
- **Time in Range (TIR):** 70-180 mg/dL, target >70% of time
- **Time below Range (TBR):** <70 mg/dL, target <4% (<54 mg/dL <1%)
- **Time above Range (TAR):** >180 mg/dL, target <25% (>250 mg/dL <5%)
- **GMI (Glucose Management Indicator):** Estimated HbA1c from CGM data
- **Glycemic Variability (CV):** Target <36%

### CGM Types
- **rtCGM (real-time):** Dexcom G6/G7, Guardian, provides continuous data
- **isCGM (intermittently scanned):** FreeStyle Libre 2/3, requires scanning

## 1.5 DKA Prevention and Management

### Prevention
- Patient education on sick day rules
- Never omit insulin (especially basal) during illness
- Ketone monitoring (blood beta-hydroxybutyrate preferred over urine ketones)
- Early intervention when glucose >250 mg/dL with moderate/large ketones

### DKA Management (2024 Consensus Report) [^3^][^4^]
1. **Fluid resuscitation:** 0.9% saline 500-1000 mL/hr for first 2-4 hours (if no cardiac/renal compromise)
2. **Potassium assessment:** Must be >3.3 mEq/L before insulin initiation
3. **Insulin:** IV regular insulin 0.1 units/kg/hr (or 0.14 units/kg/hr) after fluid resuscitation
4. **Glucose monitoring:** Check capillary glucose every 1-2 hours
5. **Electrolytes:** Check electrolytes, beta-hydroxybutyrate, venous pH every 4 hours
6. **Dextrose:** Add D5% to IV fluids when glucose <200-250 mg/dL to permit continued insulin until ketosis resolves
7. **Resolution criteria:** Blood ketone <0.6 mmol/L AND venous pH >=7.3 or bicarbonate >=18 mmol/L

### Special Considerations [^4^]
- **Hypokalemia:** If K <3.5 mEq/L, delay insulin until corrected; if K 3.3-5.3 mEq/L, add 20-40 mEq/L to fluids
- **Bicarbonate:** Routine use not recommended; consider only if pH <6.9 with hemodynamic compromise
- **Cerebral edema:** Most common in children/adolescents; rare >28 years
- **SC insulin option:** Mild/moderate uncomplicated DKA may be treated with SC rapid-acting insulin q1-2h (2024 consensus)
- **Transition:** Give SC basal insulin 2-4 hours before stopping IV insulin

## 1.6 Hypoglycemia

### Classification
- **Level 1:** Glucose <70 mg/dL with or without symptoms
- **Level 2:** Glucose <54 mg/dL - threshold for neuroglycopenic symptoms
- **Level 3:** Severe event requiring third-party assistance

### Treatment
- **Rule of 15:** 15g fast-acting carbohydrate, recheck in 15 minutes
- **Glucagon:** IM/IN glucagon for severe hypoglycemia (nasal glucagon - BAQSIMI; autoinjector - Gvoke)
- CGM alerts for low glucose (Level 1: <70 mg/dL; Level 2: <54 mg/dL)

### Risk Factors
- Excess insulin, missed meals, exercise, alcohol, renal impairment
- Hypoglycemia unawareness (counterregulatory failure)
- Intensive glycemic control (HbA1c <6.5%)

## 1.7 Special Populations

### Pediatric T1DM
- Target HbA1c: <7.0% (individualize based on age/hypoglycemia risk)
- Insulin requirements: often 0.8-1.0 units/kg/day during puberty
- CGM strongly recommended (rtCGM A, isCGM E)
- AID recommended (A)
- Psychosocial support essential

### Pregnancy
- Tight glycemic targets: fasting <95 mg/dL, 1h <140 mg/dL, 2h <120 mg/dL
- Insulin is the only approved medication
- CGM use recommended

### Older Adults
- Relaxed targets if hypoglycemia risk or limited life expectancy
- HbA1c <8.0-8.5% may be appropriate
- Focus on hypoglycemia prevention

# 2. Diabetes Mellitus - Type 2

## 2.1 Clinical Overview

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a progressive metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency. It represents 90-95% of diabetes cases and is closely associated with obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and genetic predisposition. [^5^]

## 2.2 Diagnosis

### Criteria (ADA 2024) [^5^]
- **HbA1c:** >=6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- **Fasting plasma glucose:** >=126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) after at least 8 hours
- **2-hour OGTT:** >=200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during 75g glucose load
- **Random glucose:** >=200 mg/dL with classic symptoms
- **Prediabetes:** HbA1c 5.7-6.4%, FPG 100-125 mg/dL, 2h OGTT 140-199 mg/dL

### Screening Recommendations
- All adults >=35 years should be screened (USPSTF/ADA)
- Adults <35 with risk factors (BMI >=25, family history, high-risk ethnicity, history of GDM, PCOS, hypertension, dyslipidemia)
- Repeat every 3 years if normal

## 2.3 ADA/EASD Management Guidelines [^5^][^6^]

### Glycemic Targets
- **General target:** HbA1c <7% (53 mmol/mol) for most non-pregnant adults
- **Stringent:** <6.5% if achievable without hypoglycemia/weight gain (young, new diagnosis, long life expectancy)
- **Relaxed:** <8.0-8.5% (older, significant comorbidities, history of hypoglycemia, limited life expectancy)
- **Fasting glucose target:** 80-130 mg/dL
- **Postprandial glucose:** <180 mg/dL at 1-2 hours

### Person-Centered Approach (2022 ADA/EASD Consensus) [^6^]
1. Assess cardiovascular/renal risk factors
2. Manage comorbidities (weight, BP, lipids, NAFLD, sleep apnea)
3. Set glycemic targets
4. Choose glucose-lowering medication based on individual characteristics
5. Provide DSMES (Diabetes Self-Management Education and Support)

### Pharmacotherapy Algorithm [^6^]

#### First-Line: Metformin
- Unless contraindicated or not tolerated
- Start 500 mg daily or BID with meals; titrate to 1000-2000 mg/day
- Contraindications: eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m2; hold if eGFR 30-45
- Benefits: weight neutral, low hypoglycemia risk, extensive evidence

#### Add-On Based on Comorbidities

**With ASCVD or High Risk:**
- **GLP-1 RA** (liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide) - proven CV benefit (REDUCE-IT, LEADER, SUSTAIN-6)
- **SGLT2-i** (empagliflozin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin) - proven CV benefit (EMPA-REG, CANVAS, DECLARE)

**With Heart Failure (especially HFrEF):**
- **SGLT2-i** (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) - Class I indication for HF

**With CKD/UACR >=30 mg/g:**
- **SGLT2-i** - kidney protective (CREDENCE, DAPA-CKD)
- **GLP-1 RA** - if SGLT2-i not tolerated or needs additional lowering

**With Obesity/Need for Weight Loss:**
- **GLP-1 RA** (semaglutide) or **GIP/GLP-1 dual agonist** (tirzepatide)
- **SGLT2-i** (modest weight benefit ~2-4 kg)

**With Predominant Hyperglycemia (HbA1c >10%):**
- Consider early combination therapy
- If HbA1c >=1.5% above target at diagnosis, consider dual therapy from start

### Specific Agents

#### SGLT2 Inhibitors [^6^]
- **Agents:** Empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin
- **Mechanism:** Reduce glucose reabsorption in proximal tubule
- **HbA1c lowering:** ~0.5-1.0%
- **Benefits:** CV protection, HF reduction, kidney protection, weight loss (~2-4 kg), BP lowering
- **Side effects:** Genital mycotic infections, volume depletion, euglycemic DKA (rare), amputation risk (canagliflozin)
- **Contraindications:** Dialysis, eGFR thresholds vary by agent

#### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists [^6^]
- **Agents:** Liraglutide (daily), semaglutide (weekly), dulaglutide (weekly), exenatide (weekly), lixisenatide (daily)
- **Mechanism:** Enhance glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppress glucagon, delay gastric emptying, reduce appetite
- **HbA1c lowering:** ~1.0-1.8%
- **Benefits:** CV protection (liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide), weight loss (5-15 kg with semaglutide)
- **Side effects:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, potential gallbladder disease
- **Contraindications:** Personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, MEN2

#### Tirzepatide (GIP/GLP-1 Dual Agonist)
- **Dosing:** 2.5 mg weekly, titrate to 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5, 15 mg
- **HbA1c lowering:** Up to 2.4% (SURPASS program)
- **Weight loss:** Up to 20-22% (SURMOUNT program)
- **FDA approved:** T2DM (Mounjaro) and obesity (Zepbound)

#### Insulin Initiation
- When HbA1c persistently >10% or symptomatic hyperglycemia
- Basal insulin: 0.1-0.2 units/kg/day (NPH, glargine, detemir, degludec)
- Titration: Increase by 2 units every 3 days until fasting target reached
- GLP-1 RA should be considered prior to insulin initiation [^6^]
- Continue organ-protective medications when insulin is started

## 2.4 Complications Screening

### Microvascular
- **Retinopathy:** Dilated eye exam at diagnosis, then annually (or every 2 years if normal with well-controlled)
- **Nephropathy:** Annual UACR; eGFR monitoring
- **Neuropathy:** Annual 10g monofilament + vibration testing

### Macrovascular
- **BP target:** <130/80 mmHg (if tolerated)
- **Lipid management:** Moderate-high intensity statin for most; add ezetimibe/PCSK9 if needed
- **Aspirin:** Consider for secondary prevention; primary prevention individualized

### Foot Care
- Annual comprehensive foot exam
- Risk stratification: low, moderate, high risk
- Patient education on daily foot inspection

## 2.5 Red Flags
- New diagnosis with HbA1c >10% or symptomatic - consider insulin
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (check for T1DM)
- Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS) - glucose >600 mg/dL
- Foot ulcer with infection signs
- Sudden vision changes
- Chest pain suggestive of ASCVD

# 3. Thyroid Disorders

## 3.1 Hypothyroidism

### Clinical Overview
Hypothyroidism is the clinical syndrome resulting from insufficient thyroid hormone production. The most common cause in iodine-sufficient regions is chronic autoimmune thyroiditis (Hashimoto's thyroiditis), characterized by lymphocytic infiltration and destruction of thyroid tissue. [^7^]

### Causes
- **Primary (95%):** Hashimoto's thyroiditis, post-ablative (radioactive iodine, surgery), post-thyroiditis, drug-induced (amiodarone, lithium, interferon), iodine deficiency/excess, congenital
- **Central (5%):** Pituitary/hypothalamic disease (TSH deficiency, TRH deficiency)
- **Peripheral:** Thyroid hormone resistance (rare)

### Diagnosis
- **TSH:** Elevated in primary hypothyroidism
- **Free T4:** Low in overt hypothyroidism; normal in subclinical
- **Anti-TPO antibodies:** Present in 90-95% of Hashimoto's thyroiditis
- **Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies:** Present in 60-80%

### Treatment - Levothyroxine (L-T4) [^7^]
- **Dosing:** 1.6 mcg/kg/day (young, healthy); 0.8-1.0 mcg/kg/day (elderly, CAD)
- **Starting dose:** 25-50 mcg/day in elderly/cardiac patients; full replacement in young healthy
- **Administration:** On empty stomach, 30-60 minutes before breakfast
- **Drug interactions:** Separate from calcium, iron, PPIs, soy by 4 hours
- **Monitoring:** TSH 6-8 weeks after initiation or dose change, then every 6-12 months
- **Target:** TSH within reference range (0.5-4.0 mIU/L typically; some evidence for 0.5-2.5 in pregnancy)

### Subclinical Hypothyroidism
- Elevated TSH with normal free T4
- Consider treatment if: TSH >10 mIU/L, positive anti-TPO antibodies, symptoms, pregnancy plans, goiter, dyslipidemia
- If TSH 4.5-10: treatment individualized; monitor if untreated
- If TSH <10 with symptoms: trial of L-T4 may be reasonable

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy:** TSH target <2.5 in first trimester, <3.0 in second/third; increase L-T4 dose by 20-30% as soon as pregnancy confirmed
- **Elderly:** Start low (12.5-25 mcg), go slow; target may be higher (TSH 4-6)
- **Cardiac disease:** Start 12.5-25 mcg/day, increase every 4-6 weeks

## 3.2 Hyperthyroidism

### Clinical Overview
Hyperthyroidism results from excessive thyroid hormone production. Graves' disease (autoimmune stimulation of TSH receptor) is the most common cause, followed by toxic multinodular goiter and toxic adenoma. [^7^][^8^]

### Causes
- **Graves' disease (60-80%):** Autoantibodies stimulate TSH receptor
- **Toxic multinodular goiter (5-15%):** Autonomous hormone production in nodules
- **Toxic adenoma (2-10%):** Single autonomous functioning nodule
- **Thyroiditis:** Subacute (painful), painless/silent, postpartum
- **Iodine-induced (Jod-Basedow):** After iodine exposure
- **Factitious/exogenous:** Excessive thyroid hormone intake

### Diagnosis
- **TSH:** Suppressed (<0.01 mIU/L typically)
- **Free T4, T3:** Elevated (may have T3 toxicosis with normal T4)
- **Graves' disease:** Positive TSH receptor antibodies (TRAb/TSI)
- **Radioactive iodine uptake (RAIU):** High/diffuse in Graves'; patchy in toxic MNG; focal in adenoma; low in thyroiditis
- **Thyroid ultrasound:** Useful for nodular disease; decreased vascularity in thyroiditis

### Treatment

#### Graves' Disease
1. **Antithyroid drugs (ATDs):**
   - **Methimazole (MMI):** First-line (except first trimester pregnancy); 10-40 mg daily
   - **Propylthiouracil (PTU):** 100-150 mg q8h; preferred first trimester due to MMI teratogenicity risk
   - **Side effects:** Rash, arthralgia, agranulocytosis (0.2-0.5%), hepatotoxicity (PTU > MMI)
   - **Monitoring:** Baseline CBC, LFTs; check if fever/sore throat (agranulocytosis)
   - **Duration:** 12-18 months; assess for remission (40-50% remission rate)

2. **Radioactive iodine (RAI):**
   - Definitive treatment; destroys thyroid tissue
   - Requires pre-treatment with ATDs if severe hyperthyroidism
   - Contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding
   - Results in permanent hypothyroidism (requires L-T4)

3. **Surgery:**
   - Thyroidectomy for large goiters, suspicious nodules, failed ATD/RAI
   - Requires preoperative preparation with ATDs (euthyroid state)
   - Risks: Recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, hypoparathyroidism

#### Toxic Nodular Goiter
- RAI or surgery preferred (ATDs only for pre-treatment)
- Lower remission rate with ATDs alone

### Thyroid Storm [^8^]
- Life-threatening exacerbation of hyperthyroidism
- **Mortality:** 10-30% even with treatment
- **Precipitants:** Infection, surgery, trauma, RAI, parturition, iodine load, ATD withdrawal

#### Clinical Features
- Fever (>38.5 C/101.3 F), tachycardia (often >140), arrhythmias
- CNS dysfunction (agitation, confusion, coma)
- GI symptoms (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain)
- Cardiovascular collapse, CHF

#### Treatment (Burch-Wartofsky Score >45) [^8^]
1. **PTU** loading dose 600-1000 mg, then 200-250 mg q4h (preferred for first trimester and thyroid storm due to peripheral T4-to-T3 blocking)
   - OR **Methimazole** 60-80 mg load, then 20-25 mg q4h
2. **Iodine (SSKI/Lugol's):** Given 1 hour AFTER ATD (to prevent iodine use for hormone synthesis); 5 drops q6h
3. **Beta-blocker:** Propranolol 60-80 mg q4h (blocks T4 to T3 conversion); IV esmolol if needed
4. **Glucocorticoids:** Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV q8h (inhibits T4 to T3 conversion)
5. **Supportive:** IV fluids, cooling blankets, treat precipitating cause
6. **Bile acid sequestrant:** Cholestyramine (binds thyroid hormone in gut)
7. **Definitive therapy:** Once stable, consider RAI or surgery

## 3.3 Thyroid Nodules and Cancer

### Evaluation (ATA 2015 Guidelines) [^9^]
- **Prevalence:** 50-60% of adults have thyroid nodules (most benign)
- **Malignancy risk:** 5-10% of nodules

#### Diagnostic Approach
1. **TSH:** If low, radionuclide scan to assess for hot nodule (usually benign)
2. **Thyroid ultrasound:** All nodules >=1 cm or suspicious features
3. **FNA based on ultrasound risk stratification (ATA patterns):**

| Sonographic Pattern | Risk of Malignancy | FNA Cutoff |
|--------------------|-------------------|------------|
| High suspicion | >70-90% | >=1 cm |
| Intermediate suspicion | 10-20% | >=1 cm |
| Low suspicion | 5-10% | >=1.5 cm |
| Very low suspicion | <3% | >=2 cm |
| Benign (pure cyst) | <1% | No biopsy |

**Suspicious features:** Solid hypoechoic, irregular margins, microcalcifications, taller-than-wide, extrathyroidal extension, suspicious lymph nodes

#### Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology
- I: Non-diagnostic/unsatisfactory
- II: Benign
- III: AUS/FLUS (atypia of undetermined significance)
- IV: Follicular neoplasm/suspicious for follicular neoplasm
- V: Suspicious for malignancy
- VI: Malignant

### Differentiated Thyroid Cancer (Papillary/Follicular)
- **Treatment:** Total thyroidectomy + possible radioactive iodine + TSH suppression
- **Follow-up:** Thyroglobulin levels, neck ultrasound
- **TSH suppression:** 0.1-0.5 mIU/L for low risk; <0.1 for high risk

## 3.4 TSH Interpretation Guide

| TSH | Free T4 | Interpretation |
|-----|---------|----------------|
| High | Low | Primary hypothyroidism |
| High | Normal | Subclinical hypothyroidism |
| Low | High | Primary hyperthyroidism |
| Low | Normal | Subclinical hyperthyroidism |
| Normal | Normal | Euthyroid |
| Low | Low | Central hypothyroidism (pituitary/hypothalamic) |
| High | High | Thyroid hormone resistance (rare) |

### Important Notes
- **Biocytin interference:** High-dose biotin (>5 mg/day) can cause falsely LOW TSH, HIGH T3/T4; stop biotin 48 hours before testing
- **Acute illness:** Euthyroid sick syndrome - TSH may be low in acute illness
- **Pregnancy:** TSH reference ranges shift; trimester-specific ranges needed

# 4. Adrenal Disorders

## 4.1 Cushing's Syndrome

### Clinical Overview
Cushing's syndrome results from chronic exposure to excess glucocorticoids. Endogenous causes include ACTH-dependent (Cushing's disease from pituitary adenoma, ectopic ACTH) and ACTH-independent (adrenal adenoma, adrenal carcinoma, macronodular hyperplasia). [^10^]

### Diagnosis
1. **Screening tests (one of):**
   - 24-hour urinary free cortisol (x2-3 collections)
   - Late-night salivary cortisol (x2)
   - 1 mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test (DST)
2. **Confirmatory testing** if screening positive
3. **ACTH level:** Distinguishes ACTH-dependent vs independent
4. **Imaging:** Pituitary MRI (Cushing's disease), CT chest/abdomen (ectopic), CT adrenal (adrenal source)
5. **IPSS (Inferior Petrosal Sinus Sampling):** If ACTH-dependent with equivocal imaging

### Treatment (Endocrine Society Guidelines) [^10^]
- **First-line:** Surgical resection of causal lesion
  - Transsphenoidal selective adenomectomy (TSS) for Cushing's disease by experienced pituitary surgeon
  - Unilateral adrenalectomy for adrenal adenoma
  - Ectopic ACTH source resection if identified
- **Medical therapy:** If surgery contraindicated/unsuccessful
  - Ketoconazole, metyrapone, mitotane, osilodrostat
  - Mifepristone (glucocorticoid receptor antagonist)
- **Bilateral adrenalectomy:** For severe/recurrent disease
- **Postoperative:** Glucocorticoid replacement (HPA axis recovery 6-18 months)

### Long-term Follow-up
- Monitor for recurrence (lifelong, except adrenal adenoma with HU <10)
- Treat comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, osteoporosis, psychiatric symptoms)
- Screen for associated conditions (carney complex)

## 4.2 Addison's Disease (Primary Adrenal Insufficiency)

### Clinical Overview
Primary adrenal insufficiency results from destruction of the adrenal cortex, leading to deficiency of cortisol and aldosterone. Autoimmune adrenalitis accounts for 70-90% of cases in developed countries. [^11^][^12^]

### Causes
- **Autoimmune adrenalitis (70-90%):** Isolated or part of APS type 1 or 2
- **Infections:** TB (most common worldwide), HIV, fungal, CMV
- **Metastatic disease:** Lung, breast, melanoma, lymphoma
- **Adrenal hemorrhage:** Waterhouse-Friderichsen (meningococcemia), anticoagulation
- **Genetic:** CAH, AHC, ALD
- **Drugs:** Ketoconazole, etomidate, mitotane

### Diagnosis
- **Morning cortisol:** <3 mcg/dL strongly suggestive; >15 mcg/dL excludes
- **ACTH:** Elevated (>2-3x upper limit) in primary AI
- **ACTH stimulation test (250 mcg):** Gold standard - cortisol <18 mcg/dL at 30 or 60 minutes confirms
- **Renin and aldosterone:** Elevated renin, low aldosterone
- **Autoantibodies:** 21-hydroxylase antibodies
- **Imaging:** CT adrenals if non-autoimmune suspected
- **Electrolytes:** Hyponatremia, hyperkalemia (aldosterone deficiency)

### Treatment (Endocrine Society/NICE 2024) [^11^][^12^]

#### Glucocorticoid Replacement
- **Hydrocortisone:** 15-25 mg/day in 2-3 divided doses (larger dose morning)
- **Cortisone acetate:** 20-35 mg/day (requires conversion to cortisol)
- **Prednisolone:** 3-5 mg once daily (alternative if compliance issues with multiple doses)
- **Modified-release hydrocortisone:** Available (e.g., Plenadren) for once-daily dosing
- **Monitoring:** Clinical symptoms, weight, blood pressure; no routine lab monitoring

#### Mineralocorticoid Replacement
- **Fludrocortisone:** 50-300 mcg/day (start 100 mcg, titrate by BP, electrolytes, plasma renin)
- Not needed in secondary/tertiary AI

#### Adrenal Crisis Prevention and Management
- **Patient education:** Stress dosing, sick day rules, steroid emergency card, medical alert bracelet
- **Stress dosing:** Double or triple hydrocortisone during febrile illness, injury, surgery
- **Emergency injection kit:** 100 mg hydrocortisone for IM injection
- **Adrenal crisis treatment:**
  1. IV hydrocortisone 100 mg bolus, then 200 mg/24h infusion (or 50 mg q6h)
  2. IV fluids: 0.9% saline 1L over first hour, then as needed
  3. Treat precipitating cause
  4. Glucose management if hypoglycemic

### Pregnancy Considerations [^11^]
- Continue glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid replacement
- May need dose increase in third trimester
- Stress dose for labor: 100 mg hydrocortisone IM at onset of active labor, then 50 mg q6h until delivery
- Hyperemesis gravidarum: May need IV hydrocortisone; 100 mg IM if unable to take oral

## 4.3 Adrenal Incidentaloma

### Definition
Adrenal mass discovered incidentally on imaging performed for non-adrenal indications.
- **Prevalence:** 1-5% of abdominal CT scans (increases with age)
- **Key question:** Is it hormonally active or malignant?

### Evaluation
1. **Hormonal workup (all patients):**
   - Overnight 1 mg DST (screen for Cushing's) or late-night salivary cortisol
   - Plasma free metanephrines (screen for pheochromocytoma)
   - Aldosterone/renin ratio if hypertensive/hypokalemic (screen for Conn's)
2. **Imaging characteristics:**
   - HU >10 on unenhanced CT: Concerning for malignancy
   - Washout CT: >60% absolute, >40% relative washout suggests adenoma
   - Size: >4 cm increases malignancy risk
3. **Biopsy:** Only if suspected metastasis AND pheochromocytoma excluded

### Management
- **Functional tumors:** Surgery regardless of size
- **Non-functional, benign imaging, <4 cm:** Follow-up imaging in 6-12 months, then annually for 1-2 years
- **Indeterminate or >4 cm:** Consider adrenalectomy

## 4.4 Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's Syndrome)

### Clinical Features
- Resistant hypertension, hypokalemia (may be absent), metabolic alkalosis
- **Screening:** Aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) >20-40 ng/dL per ng/mL/h
- **Confirmatory testing:** Saline suppression test, oral sodium loading, fludrocortisone suppression
- **Subtype diagnosis:** CT adrenal, adrenal venous sampling (AVS) if surgical candidate

### Treatment
- **Unilateral:** Laparoscopic adrenalectomy (cure rate 30-60% for hypertension)
- **Bilateral/hypoplasia:** Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (spironolactone, eplerenone)
- **Spironolactone:** 25-400 mg/day; side effects: gynecomastia, sexual dysfunction
- **Eplerenone:** 50-200 mg/day; more selective, fewer sexual side effects

## 4.5 Pheochromocytoma

### Clinical Overview
Catecholamine-secreting tumor of adrenal medulla (pheochromocytoma) or extra-adrenal paraganglia (paraganglioma). Rule of 10: 10% bilateral, 10% malignant, 10% extra-adrenal, 10% familial, 10% pediatric. [^12^]

### Diagnosis
- **Plasma free metanephrines or urinary fractionated metanephrines:** Elevated
- **Imaging:** CT/MRI adrenal; MIBG or DOTATATE PET for localization
- **Genetic testing:** Consider for all (SDHx, VHL, MEN2, NF1)

### Treatment
- **Preoperative preparation (essential):**
  - Alpha-blockade: Phenoxybenzamine 10 mg BID, titrate to orthostatic hypotension; or doxazosin
  - Start 7-14 days preoperatively
  - Add beta-blocker only AFTER adequate alpha-blockade (to avoid hypertensive crisis)
  - High sodium diet and fluids to restore intravascular volume
- **Surgery:** Laparoscopic adrenalectomy (open if large/malignant)
- **Malignant:** 131I-MIBG, chemotherapy (CVD regimen), targeted therapy (sunitinib)

# 5. Pituitary Disorders

## 5.1 Prolactinoma

### Clinical Overview
Prolactin-secreting pituitary adenomas are the most common hormonally active pituitary tumors. Microprolactinomas (<1 cm) predominate in women; macroprolactinomas (>=1 cm) more common in men. [^13^][^14^]

### Clinical Features
- **Women:** Galactorrhea, amenorrhea/oligomenorrhea, infertility, decreased libido
- **Men:** Decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, infertility, gynecomastia (rarely galactorrhea)
- **Mass effects (macroadenoma):** Headache, visual field defects (bitemporal hemianopsia), hypopituitarism

### Diagnosis
- **Prolactin level:** Elevated (note: macroprolactinomas can cause "hook effect" - falsely low due to assay interference; request dilution if large tumor with mild elevation)
- **MRI pituitary:** Assess size and extension
- **Exclude other causes:** Medications (antipsychotics, metoclopramide, verapamil), pregnancy, hypothyroidism, CKD, chest wall lesions

### Treatment (Pituitary Society Consensus 2023) [^14^]
- **Cabergoline:** First-line dopamine agonist
  - Dosing: 0.25-0.5 mg/week initially, titrate to 0.5-3.5 mg/week
  - Normalize prolactin in 80-90%, tumor shrinkage in 70-90%
  - Better tolerated than bromocriptine
- **Bromocriptine:** Alternative (especially pregnancy first-line)
  - Dosing: 1.25-2.5 mg/day, titrate to 10-15 mg/day
  - Side effects: Nausea, orthostatic hypotension, nasal congestion
- **Surgery:** Transsphenoidal resection if DA intolerance/resistance, or for fertility in microadenoma
- **Withdrawal:** 40-50% of patients with normalized prolactin and tumor shrinkage can taper after 2+ years

### Pregnancy Management [^14^]
- Microprolactinoma: Generally uneventful; stop DA when pregnancy confirmed; monitor clinically
- Macroprolactinoma: Risk of tumor expansion (25-35%); monthly follow-up; MRI without gadolinium if symptoms; may continue DA (cabergoline preferred)
- Prolactin levels NOT useful for monitoring during pregnancy

## 5.2 Acromegaly

### Clinical Overview
Acromegaly results from excess growth hormone (GH) secretion, usually from a pituitary somatotroph adenoma. If occurs before epiphyseal fusion, causes gigantism. [^15^]

### Clinical Features
- Acral enlargement (hands, feet, jaw, forehead)
- Coarsening facial features, prognathism, macroglossia
- Organomegaly, arthropathy, carpal tunnel syndrome
- Hypertension, diabetes, sleep apnea, cardiomyopathy, colonic polyps
- Headache, visual field defects

### Diagnosis
- **IGF-1:** Elevated for age (screening test)
- **OGTT with GH suppression:** GH nadir >0.4 mcg/L (or >1.0 mcg/L depending on assay) confirms
- **MRI pituitary:** Localize tumor
- **Pituitary function panel:** Assess for hypopituitarism

### Treatment
- **Surgery:** Transsphenoidal resection is first-line; high cure rate with microadenomas
- **Medical therapy:**
  - **Somatostatin analogues:** Octreotide LAR 20-30 mg IM monthly, lanreotide autogel 60-120 mg SC monthly, pasireotide
  - **GH receptor antagonist:** Pegvisomant (for SSA-resistant cases)
  - **Dopamine agonist:** Cabergoline (mild cases or adjuvant)
- **Radiation therapy:** For surgical failures
- **Treatment goal:** Normalize IGF-1, control tumor size

### Long-term Monitoring
- Annual colonoscopy (increased polyp/cancer risk)
- Echocardiogram, sleep study as needed
- Monitor for hypopituitarism
- GH/IGF-1 levels every 3-6 months

## 5.3 Hypopituitarism

### Clinical Overview
Deficiency of one or more anterior pituitary hormones (ACTH, TSH, LH/FSH, GH, prolactin) or posterior pituitary (ADH). [^16^]

### Causes
- Pituitary adenoma or treatment (surgery, radiation)
- Pituitary apoplexy (hemorrhagic infarction)
- Traumatic brain injury, subarachnoid hemorrhage
- Infiltrative (hemochromatosis, sarcoidosis, histiocytosis)
- Infectious, autoimmune (hypophysitis), congenital
- Medications (high-dose glucocorticoids suppress ACTH)

### Diagnosis
- **ACTH deficiency:** Low morning cortisol (<3 mcg/dL), low ACTH, failed ACTH stimulation test
- **TSH deficiency:** Low free T4 with inappropriately normal/low TSH
- **LH/FSH deficiency:** Low testosterone/estradiol with low/normal LH/FSH
- **GH deficiency:** Low IGF-1 (not reliable in adults), GH stimulation testing
- **Prolactin deficiency:** Inability to lactate postpartum

### Treatment [^16^]
- **ACTH (adrenal insufficiency):** Hydrocortisone 15-25 mg/day in 2-3 doses
- **TSH (secondary hypothyroidism):** L-thyroxine (do NOT treat based on TSH - use free T4 target upper-normal)
- **LH/FSH:** Testosterone replacement in men; estrogen/progesterone in women
- **GH deficiency:** GH replacement (0.2-0.6 mg/day SC) - improves body composition, QoL
- **ADH (diabetes insipidus):** Desmopressin

## 5.4 Diabetes Insipidus (DI)

### Clinical Overview
DI is characterized by hypotonic polyuria (>3 L/day) and polydipsia due to impaired ADH secretion (central DI) or renal resistance to ADH (nephrogenic DI). [^17^][^18^]

### Causes
- **Central DI:** Pituitary surgery, tumors (craniopharyngioma, germinoma), trauma, infiltrative (Langerhans cell histiocytosis), idiopathic, genetic
- **Nephrogenic DI:** Lithium (most common), congenital (AVPR2/AQP2 mutations), hypercalcemia, hypokalemia, post-obstructive

### Diagnosis
- **Water deprivation test:** Gold standard
  - Deprive water until orthostatic hypotension, >3% weight loss, or urine osmolality plateaus
  - Then administer desmopressin
  - Central DI: Urine osmolality increases >50% after desmopressin
  - Nephrogenic DI: Minimal response to desmopressin
- **Plasma copeptin:** Emerging biomarker for ADH
- **MRI:** Assess pituitary/hypothalamus (absent posterior pituitary bright spot suggests central DI)

### Treatment [^17^][^18^]
- **Central DI:**
  - **Desmopressin (DDAVP):** 0.1-0.2 mg oral BID-TID; or 10-40 mcg intranasal daily; or sublingual
  - Monitor for hyponatremia (allow periodic aquaresis - skip dose once or twice weekly)
  - Maintain free access to water
- **Nephrogenic DI:**
  - Treat underlying cause (e.g., stop lithium if possible)
  - **Thiazide diuretics:** Paradoxically reduce urine output
  - **Low-solute diet:** Restrict protein and sodium
  - **Amiloride:** For lithium-induced DI (blocks lithium entry into collecting duct)
  - **NSAIDs:** Indomethacin (reduce prostaglandins)
  - Partial NDI: May respond to high-dose desmopressin

## 5.5 SIADH (Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone)

### Clinical Overview
SIADH is the most common cause of euvolemic hyponatremia, characterized by inappropriate ADH secretion leading to water retention and dilutional hyponatremia. [^19^]

### Causes
- Malignancy (small cell lung cancer most common)
- CNS disorders (stroke, infection, trauma, subarachnoid hemorrhage)
- Pulmonary disease (pneumonia, TB, positive pressure ventilation)
- Medications (SSRIs, antipsychotics, carbamazepine, MDMA, NSAIDs)
- Pain, nausea, stress
- Idiopathic

### Diagnosis
- Hyponatremia (<135 mEq/L) with inappropriately concentrated urine (>100 mOsm/kg)
- Euvolemic on exam (no edema, normal skin turgor)
- Normal thyroid, adrenal, renal function
- Effective arterial blood volume normal
- Urine sodium >30 mEq/L (on normal salt intake)

### Treatment [^19^][^20^]
- **Mild/asymptomatic (Na >125):** Fluid restriction 500-1000 mL/day (first-line)
- **Moderate/severe:**
  - **Oral urea:** 15-30 g/day (highly effective, safe) [^20^]
  - **Tolvaptan:** Vasopressin V2 receptor antagonist; 7.5-15 mg initially; monitor for overly rapid correction
  - **Loop diuretics + salt tablets:** Alternative
  - **Demeclocycline:** Inhibits ADH action on collecting duct (less commonly used due to toxicity)
- **Severe/symptomatic (seizures, altered mental status):**
  - 3% hypertonic saline 100-150 mL bolus (may repeat 1-2 times)
  - Goal: Increase Na by 4-6 mEq/L acutely to stop symptoms, then slow correction
  - **Max correction rate:** 8-10 mEq/L in first 24 hours, 18 mEq/L in 48 hours (to avoid ODS)
- **Overcorrection management:** If overcorrecting >8-10 mEq/L/24h, give D5W +/- desmopressin
- **Treat underlying cause**

### ODS (Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome) Prevention
- Risk factors: Na <120, malnutrition, alcoholism, liver disease, hypokalemia
- Max correction: 8-10 mEq/L first 24h, 18 mEq/L in 48h
- If overcorrection occurs: Give D5W, consider desmopressin to re-lower sodium

# 6. Metabolic Syndrome

## 6.1 Clinical Overview

Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of interrelated metabolic risk factors that increase the risk of cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes. Insulin resistance and central obesity are central pathophysiologic features. [^21^]

## 6.2 Diagnostic Criteria

### NCEP ATP III (most widely used) [^21^]
- **Requires >=3 of 5:**

| Component | Cutoff Value |
|-----------|-------------|
| Elevated waist circumference | Men: >102 cm (40 in); Women: >88 cm (35 in) |
| Elevated triglycerides | >=150 mg/dL or on therapy |
| Reduced HDL cholesterol | Men: <40 mg/dL; Women: <50 mg/dL or on therapy |
| Elevated blood pressure | >=130/85 mmHg or on therapy |
| Elevated fasting glucose | >=100 mg/dL or on therapy |

### IDF Criteria [^21^]
- **Central obesity required** (ethnicity-specific waist circumference: Europid men >=94 cm, women >=80 cm; South Asian/Chinese men >=90 cm, women >=80 cm)
- **Plus >=2 of 4:** Elevated TG, reduced HDL, elevated BP, elevated glucose

### AACE Diagnostic Criteria (2020)
- Diagnose "insulin resistance syndrome" rather than metabolic syndrome
- Includes additional risk factors (non-HDL cholesterol, LDL particle size, apoB)

## 6.3 Cardiovascular Risk
- Metabolic syndrome confers 2-fold increased risk of CVD and 5-fold increased risk of T2DM
- CVD risk greater than individual components alone
- Associated with pro-inflammatory and pro-thrombotic states
- NCEP ATP III considered metabolic syndrome a secondary target of therapy

## 6.4 Management Strategies

### Lifestyle Modification (First-Line)
- **Weight loss:** 7-10% body weight reduction
- **Physical activity:** >=150 min/week moderate-intensity aerobic exercise
- **Diet:** Mediterranean, DASH, or low-carbohydrate; reduce saturated fat, trans fat, refined carbohydrates; increase fiber
- **Smoking cessation**

### Pharmacologic Treatment
- **Hypertension:** Target <130/80; ACE-I or ARB preferred (insulin-sensitizing properties)
- **Dyslipidemia:** Statins are primary therapy; consider fibrates for high TG
- **Hyperglycemia:** Metformin if prediabetic (prevents/delay T2DM); treat diabetes per guidelines
- **Aspirin:** Consider for primary prevention in high 10-year ASCVD risk

### Monitoring
- Annual assessment of all components
- Periodic HbA1c if prediabetic
- ASCVD risk calculation (PREVENT calculator 2024)

# 7. Obesity Management

## 7.1 BMI Classification

| Classification | BMI (kg/m2) |
|---------------|-------------|
| Underweight | <18.5 |
| Normal weight | 18.5-24.9 |
| Overweight | 25.0-29.9 |
| Class I obesity | 30.0-34.9 |
| Class II obesity | 35.0-39.9 |
| Class III obesity | >=40.0 |

### Asian Population Adjustments
- Overweight: >=23 kg/m2
- Obesity: >=25 kg/m2
- High risk: >=27.5 kg/m2 [^22^]

## 7.2 Lifestyle Intervention
- **Comprehensive lifestyle program:** 500-750 kcal/day deficit; 1-2 lb/week loss
- **Physical activity:** 150-300 min/week moderate intensity
- **Behavioral strategies:** Self-monitoring, goal setting, cognitive behavioral therapy
- **Expected outcome:** 5-10% weight loss at 6 months; maintenance is challenging

## 7.3 Anti-Obesity Medications [^23^][^24^]

### Criteria for Pharmacotherapy
- BMI >=30, OR
- BMI >=27 with obesity-related comorbidity (hypertension, T2DM, dyslipidemia, OSA)

### FDA-Approved Agents

| Drug | Mechanism | Typical Weight Loss | Key Side Effects |
|------|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Orlistat** | Lipase inhibitor | 3-5% at 1 year | GI (steatorrhea, fecal incontinence), fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption |
| **Naltrexone/Bupropion** | Opioid/dopamine reuptake inhibition | 5-8% | Nausea, headache, constipation, contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension |
| **Liraglutide 3.0 mg** | GLP-1 agonist | 10-12% | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea; thyroid C-cell risk |
| **Semaglutide 2.4 mg** | GLP-1 agonist | 15-17% (STEP trials) | GI side effects; thyroid C-cell risk |
| **Tirzepatide** | GIP/GLP-1 dual agonist | 20-22% (SURMOUNT trials) | GI side effects; thyroid C-cell risk |
| **Phentermine-Topiramate** | Sympathomimetic/anticonvulsant | 8-10% | Tachycardia, paresthesia, teratogenicity, cognitive effects |
| **Gelesis100 (Plenity)** | Hydrogel capsules | 6-8% | GI fullness, distension |

### Semaglutide vs Tirzepatide Comparison [^24^]
- Real-world data suggests tirzepatide produces greater weight loss than semaglutide
- Both are highly effective for weight loss and metabolic improvements
- Cardiovascular outcomes: SELECT trial showed semaglutide reduced MACE by 20% in overweight/obese patients with CVD; SURMOUNT-MC ongoing for tirzepatide

### Contraindications for GLP-1 RA/Tirzepatide
- Personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma
- MEN2
- Pregnancy/breastfeeding
- Active pancreatitis history
- Severe GI disease

## 7.4 Bariatric Surgery [^22^][^25^]

### Criteria (ASMBS/IFSO 2022 Updated Guidelines)
- **BMI >=35 kg/m2:** Recommended regardless of comorbidities
- **BMI 30-34.9 kg/m2:** Considered for metabolic disease (especially T2DM) or failure of nonsurgical methods
- **BMI >=27.5 kg/m2 (Asian population):** Should be offered MBS
- **Adolescents:** BMI >120% of 95th percentile with major comorbidity OR >140% of 95th percentile

### Procedure Types
- **Sleeve gastrectomy (SG):** Most common (~60%); restrictive; irreversible
- **Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB):** Combined restrictive/malabsorptive; gold standard for metabolic disease
- **One anastomosis gastric bypass (OAGB/mini-bypass):** Increasingly used
- **Biliopancreatic diversion with duodenal switch (BPD/DS):** Most effective for severe obesity; highest malabsorption

### Outcomes
- **Weight loss:** 25-35% total body weight loss at 1-2 years
- **T2DM remission:** 60-80% (varies by procedure and duration of diabetes)
- **Mortality:** <0.1-0.5% perioperative; 30-day mortality ~0.03-0.2%

### Long-term Follow-up
- Lifelong vitamin/mineral supplementation (B12, iron, calcium, vitamin D, folate)
- Annual metabolic monitoring
- Screen for dumping syndrome, marginal ulcers, strictures
- Alcohol/drug use counseling (transfer addiction risk)

## 7.5 Red Flags Requiring Immediate Referral
- BMI >40 with comorbidities
- Rapidly progressive obesity in childhood
- Suspicion of secondary obesity (Cushing's, hypothyroidism)
- Severe OSA, obesity hypoventilation syndrome
- New-onset diabetes with obesity

# 8. Dyslipidemia

## 8.1 Clinical Overview

Dyslipidemia encompasses abnormal lipid levels including elevated LDL-C, elevated triglycerides, low HDL-C, and elevated non-HDL-C or apolipoprotein B. It is a major modifiable risk factor for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). [^26^]

## 8.2 ACC/AHA 2026 Updated Cholesterol Guidelines [^26^]

### ASCVD Risk Assessment
- **PREVENT calculator (2024):** Replaces Pooled Cohort Equations; includes CKD, BMI, HbA1c, SDOH
- **Risk categories:**
  - Low risk: <5% 10-year risk
  - Borderline: 5-7.5%
  - Intermediate: >7.5-20%
  - High: >20%

### Treatment by Category

#### Severe Hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C >=190 mg/dL) [^26^]
- High-intensity statin regardless of 10-year risk
- Goal: LDL-C <100 mg/dL (no ASCVD); <70 mg/dL (with ASCVD or confirmed FH)
- Add ezetimibe, PCSK9 mAb, bempedoic acid, or inclisiran as needed
- Evaluate for familial hypercholesterolemia

#### Diabetes (Age 40-75) [^26^]
- **Without ASCVD:** Moderate-intensity statin; goal LDL-C <100, non-HDL-C <130
- **With multiple risk factors:** High-intensity statin; goal LDL-C <70, non-HDL-C <100
- **With ASCVD:** Very high-risk, LDL-C <55 mg/dL goal

#### Secondary Prevention (Clinical ASCVD) [^26^]
- High-intensity statin: Goal >=50% reduction, LDL-C <70 mg/dL
- If LDL-C >=70 on statin: Add ezetimibe
- If LDL-C >=70 on statin + ezetimibe: Add PCSK9 mAb or bempedoic acid
- Very high risk (recurrent events, polyvascular): LDL-C <55 mg/dL

#### Age >75 Years
- Continue statin if tolerating
- May initiate moderate-intensity after shared decision-making if life expectancy >2.5 years
- Consider life expectancy, frailty, comorbidities

### LDL-C Thresholds and Goals (ESC/EAS 2019) [^26^]
| Risk Category | LDL-C Goal |
|--------------|------------|
| Very high risk | <55 mg/dL (<1.4 mmol/L) |
| High risk | <70 mg/dL (<1.8 mmol/L) |
| Moderate risk | <100 mg/dL (<2.6 mmol/L) |
| Low risk | <116 mg/dL (<3.0 mmol/L) |

## 8.3 Statin Therapy

### High-Intensity Statins (Reduce LDL >=50%)
- **Atorvastatin:** 40-80 mg daily
- **Rosuvastatin:** 20-40 mg daily

### Moderate-Intensity Statins (Reduce LDL 30-49%)
- Atorvastatin 10-20 mg
- Rosuvastatin 5-10 mg
- Simvastatin 20-40 mg
- Pravastatin 40-80 mg
- Pitavastatin 2-4 mg

### Side Effects and Management
- **Myalgia:** 5-10%; check CK if severe; consider vitamin D deficiency, hypothyroidism
- **Myopathy:** CK >10x ULN with muscle symptoms; discontinue statin
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** Rare; dark urine, elevated CK, renal failure; emergency
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevated LFTs (>3x ULN); discontinue if persistent
- **New-onset diabetes:** Small risk; benefits outweigh risks
- **Drug interactions:** CYP3A4 (atorvastatin, simvastatin) - avoid with clarithromycin, azoles, HIV protease inhibitors, cyclosporine

### Statin Intolerance
- Rule out secondary causes (hypothyroidism, drug interactions)
- Try different statin (hydrophilic: pravastatin, rosuvastatin)
- Lower dose or alternate-day dosing
- Add non-statin (ezetimibe, bempedoic acid)
- Rechallenge once symptoms resolve (70% can tolerate)

## 8.4 Non-Statin LDL Therapies

### Ezetimibe
- **Mechanism:** Inhibits intestinal cholesterol absorption
- **Effect:** Additional ~18-25% LDL-C reduction
- **Dosing:** 10 mg daily
- **Evidence:** IMPROVE-IT (post-ACS) showed incremental benefit when added to statin
- **Side effects:** Generally well tolerated; myalgia, elevated LFTs (rare)

### PCSK9 Inhibitors
- **Alirocumab:** 75-150 mg SC every 2 weeks
- **Evolocumab:** 140 mg SC every 2 weeks or 420 mg monthly
- **Effect:** 50-60% additional LDL-C reduction
- **Evidence:** ODYSSEY, FOURIER trials demonstrated CV outcome benefits
- **Cost:** High; prior authorization often required
- **Side effects:** Injection site reactions, nasopharyngitis

### Inclisiran
- **Mechanism:** siRNA targeting PCSK9 synthesis
- **Dosing:** 284 mg SC on day 1, day 90, then every 6 months
- **Effect:** ~50% LDL-C reduction
- **Advantage:** Infrequent dosing (biannual)

### Bempedoic Acid
- **Mechanism:** ACL inhibitor (upstream from HMG-CoA reductase)
- **Dosing:** 180 mg daily
- **Effect:** ~15-20% LDL-C reduction
- **Evidence:** CLEAR Outcomes trial showed CV benefit in statin-intolerant patients
- **Side effects:** Hyperuricemia, gout, tendon rupture risk

## 8.5 Familial Hypercholesterolemia [^26^][^27^]

### Definition
- **HeFH (Heterozygous):** LDL-C >190 mg/dL, tendon xanthomas, premature ASCVD, genetic mutation in LDLR, APOB, or PCSK9
- **HoFH (Homozygous):** LDL-C >500 mg/dL (untreated), xanthomas by age 10, genetic mutations in both alleles

### Diagnosis (Simon Broome Criteria)
1. TC >290 mg/dL or LDL-C >190 in adult (TC >260 or LDL >155 in child)
2. Tendon xanthomas in patient/first-degree relative
3. DNA-based evidence of FH mutation
- **Definite FH:** 1 + 2; **Possible FH:** 1 + family history of premature ASCVD

### Treatment [^26^]
- **HeFH:** High-intensity statin + ezetimibe; add PCSK9 mAb if needed; goal >=50% reduction, LDL-C <70-100 depending on risk
- **HoFH:** Maximal oral therapy + PCSK9 mAb + evinacumab (ANGPTL3 mAb); lomitapide (MTP inhibitor); lipoprotein apheresis
- **Cascade screening:** Test first-degree relatives

## 8.6 Hypertriglyceridemia Management [^26^]

### Classification
- **Mild:** 150-199 mg/dL
- **Moderate:** 200-499 mg/dL
- **Severe:** 500-999 mg/dL
- **Very severe:** >=1000 mg/dL (pancreatitis risk)

### Management
- **150-499 mg/dL:** Lifestyle modification, statin if indicated; consider icosapent ethyl (EPA) if ASCVD/diabetes with risk factors (REDUCE-IT trial)
- **500-999 mg/dL:** Very low-fat diet, eliminate alcohol, add fibrate or omega-3 fatty acids
- **>=1000 mg/dL:** Fibrate + omega-3; evaluate for familial chylomicronemia; olezarsen for familial chylomicronemia syndrome
- **Acute pancreatitis prevention:** Maintain TG <500 mg/dL

# 9. Osteoporosis

## 9.1 Clinical Overview

Osteoporosis is a skeletal disorder characterized by compromised bone strength predisposing to increased fracture risk. It is defined by BMD T-score <=-2.5 at the lumbar spine, total hip, or femoral neck on DEXA. [^28^][^29^]

## 9.2 Diagnosis

### FRAX Score [^28^]
- WHO fracture risk assessment tool
- Estimates 10-year probability of major osteoporotic fracture and hip fracture
- Inputs: Age, sex, weight, height, prior fracture, parent hip fracture, smoking, alcohol, glucocorticoid use, rheumatoid arthritis, secondary osteoporosis, femoral neck BMD
- Used to guide treatment decisions when BMD in osteopenic range

### DEXA Interpretation (WHO Criteria)
| Category | T-Score |
|----------|---------|
| Normal | >= -1.0 |
| Osteopenia (low bone mass) | -1.0 to -2.5 |
| Osteoporosis | <= -2.5 |
| Severe osteoporosis | <= -2.5 + fragility fracture |

### Indications for DEXA
- All women >=65 years and men >=70 years
- Younger postmenopausal women and men 50-69 with risk factors
- Adults with fragility fracture
- Adults with conditions or medications associated with bone loss
- Anyone being considered for pharmacologic therapy
- Anyone receiving long-term glucocorticoid therapy

## 9.3 Treatment Guidelines (UK 2024 / ACP 2023) [^28^][^29^]

### Indications for Treatment
- T-score <=-2.5 (osteoporosis)
- T-score -1.0 to -2.5 with FRAX above intervention threshold
- Prior fragility fracture (hip or vertebral)
- Long-term glucocorticoids (>=7.5 mg prednisone equivalent for >=3 months)

### First-Line Therapy: Bisphosphonates [^28^]
- **Alendronate:** 70 mg orally once weekly
- **Risedronate:** 35 mg orally once weekly (or 150 mg monthly)
- **Ibandronate:** 150 mg orally monthly or 3 mg IV every 3 months
- **Zoledronic acid:** 5 mg IV once yearly

**Duration:**
- Oral: Minimum 5 years; reassess; continue >=10 years if age >=70, prior hip/vertebral fracture, glucocorticoids >=7.5 mg/day, or fracture on therapy
- IV zoledronic acid: Minimum 3 years; continue >=6 years in high-risk scenarios

**Administration:**
- Take oral bisphosphonates on empty stomach with full glass of plain water
- Remain upright for 30-60 minutes (alendronate 30 min, risedronate 30 min, ibandronate 60 min)
- Do not eat or take other medications during this period

**Side effects:**
- GI upset, esophagitis (oral agents)
- Acute phase reaction (IV zoledronate; fever, myalgia for 1-3 days)
- Atypical femur fracture (rare; long-term use)
- Osteonecrosis of jaw (rare; associated with dental procedures)
- Hypocalcemia (ensure vitamin D repletion before administration)

### Contraindications
- eGFR <30-35 mL/min (alendronate <30; risedronate <30; zoledronic acid <35)
- Esophageal disorders (oral agents)
- Inability to stand/sit upright
- Hypocalcemia

### Second-Line / Alternative Therapy

#### Denosumab
- **Dosing:** 60 mg SC every 6 months
- **Mechanism:** RANKL inhibitor (monoclonal antibody)
- **Advantages:** No renal dose adjustment, no GI side effects, can be given in CKD
- **Duration:** No fixed limit; DO NOT STOP without transition plan (rebound fractures)
- **Side effects:** Hypocalcemia (ensure vitamin D/calcium), skin infections, ONJ, atypical femur fracture
- **CRITICAL:** Must transition to bisphosphonate if discontinuing (to prevent rebound vertebral fractures)

#### Teriparatide (PTH 1-34)
- **Dosing:** 20 mcg SC daily for maximum 2 years
- **Mechanism:** Anabolic agent (stimulates bone formation)
- **Indications:** Very high fracture risk (T-score <-3.5, multiple fractures, fractures on bisphosphonate therapy)
- **Side effects:** Nausea, dizziness, hypercalcemia, transient orthostatic hypotension
- **Boxed warning:** Osteosarcoma risk in rats; avoid in patients with Paget disease, prior radiation, open epiphyses, bone metastases
- **Must follow with antiresorptive therapy** after 2 years

#### Abaloparatide
- Similar to teriparatide; 80 mcg SC daily; also limited to 2 years

#### Romosozumab
- Anti-sclerostin antibody; monthly SC injection; max 12 months
- Must follow with antiresorptive
- CV risk warning: Avoid in patients with MI or stroke within 1 year

### Calcium and Vitamin D [^28^]
- **Calcium:** 1000-1200 mg/day total (diet + supplements)
- **Vitamin D:** Maintain 25(OH)D >30 ng/mL; typically 800-2000 IU/day
- Doses up to 50,000 IU weekly may be needed for repletion

### Glucocorticoid-Induced Osteoporosis [^28^]
- Start bone protection at same time as glucocorticoids if:
  - Prior fragility fracture, OR
  - Age >=70 (women) or >=50 (men), OR
  - High-dose glucocorticoids (>=7.5 mg prednisone for >=3 months), OR
  - FRAX above threshold
- First-line: Alendronate, risedronate, or zoledronic acid
- Duration matches glucocorticoid use; reassess on discontinuation

## 9.4 Monitoring
- DEXA every 1-2 years until stable, then every 2 years
- Biochemical markers (CTX, P1NP) if available
- FRAX reassessment at treatment milestones
- Calcium and vitamin D levels
- Medication adherence assessment

# 10. Electrolyte Disorders

## 10.1 Hyponatremia [^19^][^20^]

### Definition
- Serum sodium <135 mEq/L
- Most common electrolyte disorder in hospitalized patients

### Classification by Volume Status
| Type | Causes | Treatment |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Hypovolemic** | Diuretics, vomiting, diarrhea, adrenal insufficiency | Isotonic saline; address underlying cause |
| **Euvolemic (SIADH)** | Malignancy, CNS disorders, medications, pain/nausea | Fluid restriction, oral urea, tolvaptan |
| **Hypervolemic** | CHF, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome | Fluid restriction, diuretics, treat underlying |

### Treatment Algorithm
1. **Acute symptomatic (<48h):** 3% saline 100-150 mL bolus; repeat 1-2x if needed; goal increase 4-6 mEq/L acutely
2. **Chronic symptomatic:** 3% saline cautiously; max correction 8-10 mEq/L in 24h, 18 mEq/L in 48h
3. **SIADH (euvolemic):**
   - First-line: Fluid restriction 500-1000 mL/day
   - Second-line: Oral urea 15-30 g/day, tolvaptan 7.5-15 mg/day, or loop diuretic + salt
   - European guidelines: Urea preferred; demeclocycline and vaptans not routinely recommended
4. **Asymptomatic mild (Na >125):** Treat underlying cause, adequate solute intake

### Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS) Prevention
- Risk factors: Na <105, malnutrition, alcoholism, liver disease, hypokalemia
- **Maximum safe correction:** 8-10 mEq/L in first 24 hours; 18 mEq/L in 48 hours
- If overcorrecting: Give D5W, consider desmopressin to re-lower

## 10.2 Hypernatremia [^18^]

### Definition
- Serum sodium >145 mEq/L
- Usually due to impaired thirst mechanism or inadequate water access

### Causes
- **Central DI:** Inadequate ADH secretion
- **Nephrogenic DI:** Renal resistance to ADH
- **Hypodipsia:** Elderly, altered mental status
- **Net water loss:** Osmotic diuresis, profuse sweating, diarrhea
- **Sodium gain:** Hypertonic saline, mineralocorticoid excess

### Treatment
1. **Calculate free water deficit:**
   - Men: 0.6 x weight (kg) x [(Na/140) - 1]
   - Women: 0.5 x weight (kg) x [(Na/140) - 1]
2. **Acute hypernatremia (<48h):** D5W at 3-6 mL/kg/hr; can correct rapidly (1-2 mEq/L/hr)
3. **Chronic hypernatremia (>48h):** Correct slowly at 0.5 mEq/L/hr to avoid cerebral edema
   - Use D5W or enteral water
   - Central DI: Desmopressin
   - Nephrogenic DI: Thiazides, low-solute diet

## 10.3 Hypokalemia [^30^][^31^]

### Definition
- Serum potassium <3.5 mEq/L
- Critical: <2.5 mEq/L or symptoms

### Causes
- Decreased intake, GI losses (vomiting, diarrhea), renal losses (diuretics, hyperaldosteronism, RTA, magnesium deficiency)
- Intracellular shifts (insulin, beta-agonists, alkalosis)

### Treatment [^30^][^31^]

| Severity | Level | Treatment |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| Mild | 3.0-3.4 mEq/L | Oral 20-40 mEq KCl 2-3x/day |
| Moderate | 2.5-2.9 mEq/L | Oral 40 mEq 3-4x/day; or IV 20-40 mEq/L |
| Severe | <2.5 mEq/L | IV 40-80 mEq/L via central line; max 10-40 mEq/hr |

### Key Considerations
- Oral preferred if GI tract functional
- IV concentration: Max 40 mEq/L via peripheral line; higher via central line
- **Rate:** Max 10-20 mEq/hr via peripheral; 20-40 mEq/hr via central with ECG monitoring
- Give with glucose-free fluid (D5W can worsen hypokalemia)
- **Check and replete magnesium** (hypomagnesemia causes refractory hypokalemia)
- Monitor K every 2-4 hours during IV repletion
- Side effects: GI irritation (oral), hyperkalemia, phlebitis, cardiac arrhythmias if too rapid

## 10.4 Hyperkalemia [^32^][^33^][^34^]

### Definition
- Serum potassium >5.0 mEq/L; severe >6.0-6.5 mEq/L
- Life-threatening at >6.5 mEq/L or with ECG changes

### ECG Changes (Progressive)
- Peaked T waves (earliest)
- PR prolongation
- P wave flattening/disappearance
- QRS widening
- Sine wave pattern (pre-arrest)
- VF/asystole

### Treatment - Three-Step Approach [^32^][^34^]

#### Step 1: Cardiac Membrane Stabilization
- **Calcium gluconate 10%:** 10-20 mL (1-2 g) IV over 2-3 minutes
  - Onset: 1-3 minutes; duration: 30-60 minutes
  - Does NOT lower K; protects against arrhythmias
  - Can repeat if ECG changes persist

#### Step 2: Shift K into Cells (Temporizing)
- **Insulin + Glucose:** 5-10 units regular insulin IV + 25g D50 IV
  - Onset: 30-60 minutes; duration: 4-6 hours
  - Monitor glucose hourly; give additional D50 if needed
- **Beta-2 agonists:** Albuterol 10-20 mg nebulized over 10-15 minutes
  - Onset: 30-90 minutes; duration: 2-4 hours
  - Additive with insulin
- **Sodium bicarbonate:** 50-100 mEq IV if metabolic acidosis present (pH <7.2)
  - Less effective if no acidosis

#### Step 3: Remove K from Body (Definitive)
- **Potassium binders:**
  - **Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate (SZC/Lokelma):** 10 g TID x 48h, then 5-10 g daily; onset ~1-2 hours
  - **Patiromer (Veltassa):** 8.4-25.2 g daily; onset 4-7 hours; exchanges Ca for K
  - **SPS (Kayexalate):** 15-30 g PO/PR; avoid if possible (bowel necrosis risk, limited efficacy)
- **Loop diuretics:** Furosemide 40-80 mg IV (if adequate renal function)
- **Dialysis:** Most effective; indicated for life-threatening hyperkalemia with renal failure
- **Fludrocortisone:** 0.1-0.2 mg daily for hyporeninemic hypoaldosteronism (Type 4 RTA)

### Chronic Hyperkalemia Management [^33^]
- Dietary potassium restriction (40-60 mEq/day)
- Optimize RAAS inhibitors rather than discontinuing
- Consider potassium binders (patiromer, SZC) to enable continued ACE-I/ARB
- Loop/thiazide diuretics
- Treat metabolic acidosis (sodium bicarbonate)
- Adjust medications that cause hyperkalemia

## 10.5 Hypocalcemia

### Definition
- Corrected total calcium <8.5 mg/dL or ionized calcium <4.0 mg/dL

### Causes
- Hypoparathyroidism (post-surgical most common, autoimmune, genetic)
- Vitamin D deficiency
- Hypomagnesemia
- CKD (secondary hyperparathyroidism with low/normal Ca)
- Hungry bone syndrome (post-parathyroidectomy)
- Acute pancreatitis
- Hyperphosphatemia

### Clinical Features
- Neuromuscular irritability (perioral numbness, paresthesias, tetany, Chvostek's sign, Trousseau's sign)
- Seizures, laryngospasm
- Prolonged QT interval on ECG
- Cardiac dysfunction

### Treatment
- **Acute symptomatic:** IV calcium gluconate 1-2 g over 10-20 minutes, then infusion 0.5-1.5 mg/kg/hr
- **Hypomagnesemia:** Replete Mg first (refractory to Ca repletion)
- **Chronic:**
  - Calcium carbonate 1000-1500 mg elemental calcium/day (divided doses)
  - Active vitamin D (calcitriol 0.25-1.0 mcg/day) if PTH absent/deficient
  - Cholecalciferol for vitamin D deficiency
  - Thiazide diuretics to reduce urinary calcium loss (in hypoparathyroidism)
- **Target:** Low-normal calcium; avoid hypercalciuria (increases kidney stone risk)

## 10.6 Hypercalcemia [^35^]

### Definition
- Corrected total calcium >10.5 mg/dL or ionized calcium >5.6 mg/dL

### Causes
1. **Primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT):** Most common outpatient cause; elevated PTH with hypercalcemia
2. **Malignancy:** Most common inpatient cause; PTHrP-mediated; suppressed PTH
3. **Vitamin D excess/toxicity**
4. **Granulomatous disease:** Sarcoidosis, TB, lymphoma (ectopic 1-alpha hydroxylase)
5. **Medications:** Thiazides, lithium, vitamin A
6. **Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia (FHH):** Inactivating CaSR mutation; low urine calcium

### Clinical Features
- "Stones, bones, groans, psychic moans, and fatigue overtones"
- Nephrolithiasis, osteopenia/osteoporosis, constipation, peptic ulcer disease, depression, fatigue, polyuria
- Severe (>14 mg/dL): Altered mental status, coma, shortened QT, arrhythmias

### Treatment [^35^]

#### Severe/Symptomatic (Ca >14 mg/dL or crisis)
1. **IV fluids:** 0.9% saline 200-500 mL/hr (correct dehydration from hypercalciuria)
2. **Loop diuretics:** Furosemide after adequate hydration (caution - do not give without hydration)
3. **Calcitonin:** 4 IU/kg SC/IM; onset 2-4 hours; tachyphylaxis after 48 hours
4. **Bisphosphonates:**
   - Zoledronic acid 4 mg IV over 15 minutes (preferred for malignancy)
   - Pamidronate 60-90 mg IV over 24 hours (slower onset)
   - Onset: 2-4 days; duration: weeks
5. **Denosumab:** 120 mg SC (if refractory to bisphosphonates or CKD)
6. **Glucocorticoids:** If vitamin D-mediated (granulomatous disease)
7. **Dialysis:** For life-threatening hypercalcemia with renal failure
8. **Cinacalcet:** Calcimimetic for PHPT; lowers calcium by sensitizing CaSR

#### Primary Hyperparathyroidism Definitive Treatment [^35^]
- **Parathyroidectomy:** Cure rate >95%; indicated for all symptomatic patients and asymptomatic with:
  - Age <50
  - Calcium >1.0 mg/dL above ULN
  - eGFR <60
  - T-score <=-2.5
  - Vertebral fracture
  - Nephrolithiasis
  - Hypercalciuria
- **Medical management (if not surgical candidate):**
  - Cinacalcet (lowers calcium)
  - Bisphosphonates (for bone density)
  - Monitor calcium, eGFR, BMD annually

## 10.7 Hypomagnesemia

### Definition
- Serum magnesium <1.8 mg/dL (<0.74 mmol/L); severe <1.2 mg/dL
- Common and underdiagnosed; causes refractory hypokalemia and hypocalcemia

### Causes
- GI losses (diarrhea, malabsorption, proton pump inhibitors)
- Renal losses (diuretics, alcohol, aminoglycosides, cisplatin, amphotericin)
- Malnutrition, refeeding syndrome

### Clinical Features
- Often asymptomatic; similar to hypocalcemia and hypokalemia
- Tetany, tremors, seizures, cardiac arrhythmias (torsades de pointes)
- Hypokalemia and hypocalcemia refractory to repletion

### Treatment
- **Oral:** Magnesium oxide 400-800 mg 2-3x/day; or magnesium glycinate
- **IV:** Magnesium sulfate 1-2 g over 15 minutes, then 0.5-1 g/hr infusion
  - Recheck levels frequently
  - Requires renal function monitoring
- Treat until level >2.0 mg/dL and other electrolytes normalize

## E. Emergency Protocols

### Adrenal Crisis
1. Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV bolus immediately
2. 0.9% saline 1L over 1 hour
3. Hydrocortisone 50 mg IV q6h or 200 mg/24h infusion
4. Treat precipitating cause
5. Switch to oral when stable

### Thyroid Storm
1. PTU 600-1000 mg load, then 200-250 mg q4h (or MMI 60-80 mg load, then 20-25 mg q4h)
2. SSKI/Lugol's 5 drops q6h (1 hour AFTER ATD)
3. Propranolol 60-80 mg q4h (or IV esmolol)
4. Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV q8h
5. Supportive care (fluids, cooling, treat precipitant)

### Severe DKA
1. 0.9% saline 1L over 1 hour
2. Check K; if >3.3, start insulin 0.1 units/kg/hr IV
3. Add KCl 20-40 mEq/L when K <5.3 and urine output adequate
4. Add D5% when glucose <200-250 mg/dL
5. Continue insulin until ketosis resolves (ketone <0.6, pH >7.3, HCO3 >18)

### Myxedema Coma
1. Levothyroxine 200-400 mcg IV load, then 50-100 mcg daily
2. Hydrocortisone 50-100 mg IV q8h (until adrenal insufficiency excluded)
3. Supportive: Fluids, rewarming, ventilatory support, treat precipitant
4. Consider T3 (liothyronine) 5-20 mcg IV if no improvement

### Hyperkalemia with ECG Changes
1. Calcium gluconate 1-2 g IV over 2-3 minutes (cardiac membrane stabilization)
2. Insulin 10 units + D50 25g IV (shift K into cells)
3. Albuterol 10-20 mg nebulized
4. Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate 10 g or patiromer (remove K)
5. Furosemide 40-80 mg IV if renal function adequate
6. Dialysis if refractory or severe renal failure
